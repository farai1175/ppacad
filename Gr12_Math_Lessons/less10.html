📚 Key Theory & Proofs

▼

Overview

In this lesson, we will prove the formulas used to calculate the sum of a geometric series. Understanding these proofs is essential for the Grade 12 curriculum and helps you appreciate why these formulas work. We will also extend our understanding to series that sum to infinity.

Proof: Sum of a Geometric Series

A geometric series is the sum of terms in a geometric sequence. Let's derive the formula step by step.

📐 Derivation of Sₙ = a(rⁿ - 1)/(r - 1)

1
Write out the sum of n terms:
Sₙ = a + ar + ar² + ar³ + ... + arⁿ⁻² + arⁿ⁻¹

This is equation (1). The series has n terms, starting from a and ending at arⁿ⁻¹.

2
Multiply both sides by r:
r × Sₙ = ar + ar² + ar³ + ... + arⁿ⁻¹ + arⁿ

This is equation (2). Notice each term has been multiplied by r.

3
Subtract equation (1) from equation (2):
rSₙ - Sₙ = arⁿ - a

All the middle terms cancel out! Only the first term of (1) and last term of (2) remain.

4
Factor both sides:
Sₙ(r - 1) = a(rⁿ - 1)

Take out Sₙ as a common factor on the left, and a on the right.

5
Divide both sides by (r - 1):
Sₙ = a(rⁿ - 1)/(r - 1), where r ≠ 1

This is our final formula for the sum of a geometric series!

Sₙ = a(rⁿ - 1)/(r - 1) Use when r > 1 (common ratio greater than 1)

Alternate Formula

If we subtract equation (2) from equation (1) instead, we get an alternate formula that is more convenient when r < 1:

Sₙ = a(1 - rⁿ)/(1 - r) Use when r < 1 (common ratio less than 1)
⚠️ Important: Both formulas are mathematically equivalent! Choose the one that avoids negative numbers in your calculation. If r > 1, use the first formula. If r < 1, use the second.

Sum to Infinity (S∞)

When the common ratio r is between -1 and 1 (i.e., |r| < 1), we can find the sum of an infinite geometric series. Let's derive this formula.

📐 Derivation of S∞ = a/(1 - r)

1
Start with the formula for Sₙ:
Sₙ = a(1 - rⁿ)/(1 - r)
2
Expand the brackets:
Sₙ = (a - arⁿ)/(1 - r)
3
Split into two fractions:
Sₙ = a/(1 - r) - arⁿ/(1 - r)
4
Consider what happens as n → ∞:

When -1 < r < 1, the term rⁿ gets smaller and smaller as n increases.

Examples: (⅓)² = ⅑, (⅓)³ = 1/27, (⅓)⁴ = 1/81, (⅓)⁵ = 1/243 ...

As n → ∞, rⁿ → 0

5
Therefore, the second term vanishes:
S∞ = a/(1 - r) - 0 = a/(1 - r)
S∞ = a/(1 - r), where -1 < r < 1 The sum of an infinite geometric series (only valid when |r| < 1)
Worked Example: Finding S∞

Given: Find the sum to infinity of: 8 + 4 + 2 + 1 + ½ + ...

Step 1: Identify a and r

  • First term: a = 8
  • Common ratio: r = 4/8 = ½
  • Check: |r| = ½ < 1 ✓ (series converges)

Step 2: Apply the formula

S∞ = a/(1 - r) = 8/(1 - ½) = 8/(½) = 16

Key Takeaways

  • The sum formula is derived by multiplying the series by r and subtracting.
  • For r > 1: Use Sₙ = a(rⁿ - 1)/(r - 1)
  • For r < 1: Use Sₙ = a(1 - rⁿ)/(1 - r)
  • Sum to infinity only exists when -1 < r < 1 (the series converges)
  • Formula for S∞: a/(1 - r)

Test your understanding of the proofs and formulas. Select an option for instant feedback.

Question 1: In the proof of the sum formula, why do we multiply Sₙ by r?

A) To make the equation simpler
B) So that when we subtract, most terms cancel out
C) To find the common ratio
D) To convert to a linear sequence
Explanation: Multiplying by r shifts all terms one position, creating matching terms that cancel when we subtract. This leaves only the first and last terms, making it possible to solve for Sₙ.

Question 2: What condition must be satisfied for S∞ to exist?

A) r > 1
B) r = 1
C) -1 < r < 1
D) r < -1
Explanation: For a geometric series to converge (have a finite sum to infinity), the common ratio must satisfy |r| < 1, which means -1 < r < 1. Only then does rⁿ approach 0 as n increases.

Question 3: If a = 6 and r = ⅓, what is S∞?

A) 18
B) 9
C) 6
D) 2
Explanation: S∞ = a/(1-r) = 6/(1-⅓) = 6/(⅔) = 6 × (3/2) = 9

Question 4: When is it more convenient to use Sₙ = a(1-rⁿ)/(1-r) instead of Sₙ = a(rⁿ-1)/(r-1)?

A) When r < 1
B) When r > 1
C) When a < 0
D) When n is very large
Explanation: When r < 1, using Sₙ = a(1-rⁿ)/(1-r) avoids negative numbers in the numerator and denominator, making calculations cleaner and reducing errors.

Question 5: What is the relationship between the second difference (2a) and the formula for S∞?

A) S∞ = 2a
B) The second difference determines r
C) 2a = 1/(1-r)
D) Second difference is for quadratic sequences, not geometric series
Explanation: The second difference (2a) is used for quadratic sequences where Tₙ = an² + bn + c. Geometric series use the common ratio (r), not differences. Don't confuse the two concepts!
