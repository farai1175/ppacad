Geometric Sequences & Series (Lesson 4)

Grade: 12
Week: Term 1 Week 2
Time: 60 minutes
Topic: Series (4)

Advanced problem solving with geometric sequences using simultaneous equations to find unknown first terms and common ratios.

🎯 Lesson Objectives

▼

This lesson focuses on applying your knowledge of geometric sequences to solve more complex problems. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Apply the general term formula Tₙ = a·rⁿ⁻¹ to set up simultaneous equations
  • Solve for unknown first terms (a) and common ratios (r) when given two terms
  • Handle problems where even exponents lead to two possible solutions
  • Calculate the sum of n terms after determining a and r
  • Translate word problems into mathematical equations

🧠 Pre-Knowledge Required

▼

Before starting this lesson, you should be familiar with:

  • The general term formula for geometric sequences: Tₙ = a·rⁿ⁻¹
  • The sum formula for geometric series: Sₙ = a(rⁿ - 1)/(r - 1) for r ≠ 1
  • Solving simultaneous equations by substitution or elimination
  • Working with exponents and their properties
  • Understanding that even roots can give positive AND negative answers

  • If Tₙ = 3·2ⁿ⁻¹, what are T₁ and T₄?
  • Write down the two forms of the sum formula for geometric series.

📐 Key Formulas Review

▼

Essential Geometric Sequence Formulas

General Term: Tₙ = a·rⁿ⁻¹ where: a = first term, r = common ratio, n = term number
Sum Formula (r ≠ 1): Sₙ = a(rⁿ - 1)/(r - 1) OR Sₙ = a(1 - rⁿ)/(1 - r) Use the first form when |r| > 1, second form when |r| < 1

💡 Key Strategy for This Lesson

When given two terms of a geometric sequence (e.g., T₂ and T₅), you can:

Step 1: Write each term using Tₙ = a·rⁿ⁻¹

Step 2: Divide the equations to eliminate 'a' and solve for 'r'

Step 3: Substitute back to find 'a'

⚠️ Important: Even vs Odd Exponents

When solving rⁿ = k:

• Odd exponent (r³ = 27): Only ONE answer: r = 3

• Even exponent (r² = 49): TWO answers: r = 7 OR r = -7

This means the problem may have TWO valid geometric sequences!

📊 Worked Examples

▼

Example 1: Odd Exponent Case (One Solution)

Problem: If the second term of a Geometric Series is 9 and the fifth term is 243, find the sum of 20 terms.

Solution:

Given: T₂ = 9 and T₅ = 243

Using Tₙ = a·rⁿ⁻¹, we set up two equations:

  • Write the equations:
    T₂ = a·r²⁻¹ = a·r¹ = 9 ... equation (1)
    T₅ = a·r⁵⁻¹ = a·r⁴ = 243 ... equation (2)
  • Divide equation (2) by equation (1) to eliminate 'a':
    (a·r⁴)/(a·r) = 243/9
    r³ = 27
  • Solve for r:
    r³ = 27
    r = ∛27 = 3
    (Odd exponent → only one answer)
  • Substitute back to find 'a':
    Using equation (1): a·(3) = 9
    a = 9/3 = 3
  • Calculate S₂₀:
    Sₙ = a(rⁿ - 1)/(r - 1)
    S₂₀ = 3(3²⁰ - 1)/(3 - 1)
    S₂₀ = 3(3486784401 - 1)/2
    S₂₀ = 3(3486784400)/2
    S₂₀ = 5,230,176,600

Example 2: Even Exponent Case (Two Solutions)

Problem: If the second term of a Geometric Series is 35 and the fourth term is 1715, determine the sum of the first 8 terms.

Solution:

Given: T₂ = 35 and T₄ = 1715

  • Write the equations:
    T₂ = a·r¹ = 35 ... equation (1)
    T₄ = a·r³ = 1715 ... equation (2)
  • Divide equation (2) by equation (1):
    (a·r³)/(a·r) = 1715/35
    r² = 49
  • Solve for r (EVEN exponent → TWO answers):
    r² = 49
    r = 7 OR r = -7
  • Find 'a' for BOTH values of r:
    Case 1: r = 7
    a·(7) = 35 → a = 5

    Case 2: r = -7
    a·(-7) = 35 → a = -5
  • Calculate S₈ for BOTH series:

    Series 1 (a = 5, r = 7):
    S₈ = 5(7⁸ - 1)/(7 - 1)
    S₈ = 5(5764801 - 1)/6
    S₈ = 5(5764800)/6
    S₈ = 4,804,000

    Series 2 (a = -5, r = -7):
    S₈ = -5((-7)⁸ - 1)/((-7) - 1)
    S₈ = -5(5764801 - 1)/(-8)
    S₈ = -5(5764800)/(-8)
    S₈ = 3,603,000

Final Answer: S₈ = 4,804,000 OR S₈ = 3,603,000 (two valid answers)

💡 Key Insight

When you get an even power for r, you MUST check both positive and negative values. Both can lead to valid geometric sequences with different sums!

📝 Class Work Problems

1. If T₃ = 24 and T₆ = 192, find the first term, common ratio, and S₁₀.

2. If T₂ = 18 and T₄ = 162, find Tₙ and S₆.

3. The 3rd term of a GP is 20 and the 6th term is 160. Find S₈.

4. If T₃ = 12 and T₅ = 48, find all possible values for a and r.

Solution to Problem 1:
  • T₃ = a·r² = 24 ... (1) and T₆ = a·r⁵ = 192 ... (2)
  • Dividing: r³ = 192/24 = 8 → r = 2
  • From (1): a·(4) = 24 → a = 6
  • S₁₀ = 6(2¹⁰ - 1)/(2-1) = 6(1024-1) = 6(1023) = 6138
Solution to Problem 4 (Two Answers):
  • T₃ = a·r² = 12 and T₅ = a·r⁴ = 48
  • Dividing: r² = 48/12 = 4 → r = 2 OR r = -2
  • If r = 2: a·(4) = 12 → a = 3
  • If r = -2: a·(4) = 12 → a = 3
  • Answer: (a = 3, r = 2) OR (a = 3, r = -2)

Question 1: If T₂ = 6 and T₄ = 54, what is the common ratio r?

A) r = 9
B) r = 3 or r = -3
C) r = 3 only
D) r = 27
Explanation: Dividing: r² = 54/6 = 9. Since r² = 9 (even exponent), r = 3 OR r = -3.

Question 2: If T₂ = 12 and T₅ = 96, find the value of r.

A) r = 2
B) r = 2 or r = -2
C) r = 8
D) r = 4
Explanation: Dividing: r³ = 96/12 = 8. Since r³ = 8 (odd exponent), r = ∛8 = 2 (only one answer).

Question 3: Given T₃ = 18 and T₆ = 486, what is the first term a?

A) a = 6
B) a = 2
C) a = 3
D) a = 9
Explanation: r³ = 486/18 = 27 → r = 3. Then a·r² = 18 → a·9 = 18 → a = 2.

Question 4: Why might a geometric sequence problem have two different sums?

A) The formula is incorrect
B) Odd exponent in the solution
C) Even exponent leads to ± r values
D) The sequence is not geometric
Explanation: When dividing terms gives an even power of r (like r² = 49), we get two valid solutions: r = 7 and r = -7, leading to two different series and sums.

Question 5: If T₂ = 10 and T₄ = 250, find the first term a for both possible r values.

A) a = 2 only
B) a = 2 and a = -2
C) a = 5 and a = -5
D) a = 50
Explanation: r² = 250/10 = 25 → r = 5 or r = -5. If r = 5: a·5 = 10 → a = 2. If r = -5: a·(-5) = 10 → a = -2.

Question 6: Which formula should you use to find S₁₀ when r = 3 and a = 2?

A) Sₙ = a(rⁿ - 1)/(r - 1)
B) Sₙ = n/2(2a + (n-1)d)
C) Sₙ = a/(1 - r)
D) Sₙ = n·a·r
Explanation: For geometric series with r ≠ 1, use Sₙ = a(rⁿ - 1)/(r - 1). Option B is for arithmetic series, C is for infinite geometric series.

Question 7: If T₂ = 9 and T₅ = 243 (from Example 1), what is S₅?

A) 243
B) 363
C) 324
D) 255
Explanation: From Example 1: a = 3, r = 3. S₅ = 3(3⁵ - 1)/(3 - 1) = 3(243 - 1)/2 = 3(242)/2 = 363.

Question 8: When T₃ = 40 and T₇ = 2560, what power of r do you solve for?

A) r³
B) r⁴
C) r²
D) r⁶
Explanation: T₃ = ar² and T₇ = ar⁶. Dividing: r⁶/r² = r⁴ = 2560/40 = 64. So you solve r⁴ = 64.

Question 9: If r⁴ = 16, how many possible values does r have?

A) 2 (r = 2 or r = -2)
B) 1 (r = 2 only)
C) 4
D) 0
Explanation: r⁴ = 16 means r² = 4 (taking square root), then r = ±2. For real numbers, there are 2 solutions: r = 2 or r = -2.

Question 10: For T₂ = 35 and T₄ = 1715 (Example 2), verify: which gives the LARGER sum S₈?

A) r = 7 gives S₈ = 4,804,000
B) r = -7 gives S₈ = 4,804,000
C) Both give equal sums
D) Neither can be determined
Explanation: From Example 2: r = 7 gives S₈ = 4,804,000 while r = -7 gives S₈ = 3,603,000. The positive r gives the larger sum.

Question 11: If T₁ = 4 and T₄ = 32, find r.

A) r = 2
B) r = 2 or r = -2
C) r = 8
D) r = 4
Explanation: T₁ = a = 4, T₄ = ar³ = 32. So 4·r³ = 32 → r³ = 8 → r = 2 (odd exponent, one answer).

Question 12: What is the first step when given T₂ = 14 and T₆ = 1134?

A) Add the terms together
B) Use the sum formula immediately
C) Write both as Tₙ = arⁿ⁻¹ and divide
D) Subtract to find the difference
Explanation: Write T₂ = ar¹ = 14 and T₆ = ar⁵ = 1134. Divide to get r⁴ = 1134/14 = 81, then solve for r.
