📖 Overview

In previous lessons, you've been using the sum formula for geometric series. Now it's time to understand where this formula comes from and prove it mathematically. We'll also derive the famous sum to infinity formula, which is essential for convergent series.

Sn = a(rn − 1)/r − 1
Sum Formula (r ≠ 1)
S∞ = a/1 − r
Sum to Infinity (−1 < r < 1)
💡 Why Learn Proofs?

Understanding proofs helps you remember formulas better, apply them correctly, and recognize when they can (or cannot) be used.

📝 Proof: Sum of a Geometric Series

We want to prove that the sum of the first n terms of a geometric series is:

Sn = a(rn − 1)/r − 1 where r ≠ 1
🔍 Step-by-Step Derivation
1
Write out the sum of n terms:
Sn = a + ar + ar² + ar³ + ... + arn−2 + arn−1

This has n terms, starting from a (which is ar⁰) to arn−1

2
Multiply the entire equation by r:
r·Sn = ar + ar² + ar³ + ar⁴ + ... + arn−1 + arn

Notice: Each term shifts right by one power of r

3
Subtract equation (1) from equation (2):
r·Sn − Sn = arn − a

All the middle terms cancel out! (ar − ar = 0, ar² − ar² = 0, etc.)

4
Factor out common terms on both sides:
Sn(r − 1) = a(rn − 1)

Left side: factor out Sn. Right side: factor out a

5
Divide both sides by (r − 1):
Sn = a(rn − 1)/r − 1 , r ≠ 1

This is our sum formula! ✓

📌 Alternate Form:

If we subtract (1) from (2) instead, we get:

Sn = a(1 − rn)/1 − r

This form is more convenient when |r| < 1

∞ Proof: Sum to Infinity

For certain geometric series, we can find the sum of infinitely many terms. Let's derive this formula.

🌌 Deriving the Sum to Infinity Formula
1
Start with the alternate form of the sum formula:
Sn = a(1 − rn)/1 − r
2
Expand by multiplying into the brackets:
Sn = a − arn/1 − r
3
Split into two fractions:
Sn = a/1 − r − arn/1 − r
4
Consider what happens when −1 < r < 1:

Key Insight: When |r| < 1, as n gets larger, rn gets smaller and smaller, approaching 0.

5
As n → ∞, rn → 0, so the second term vanishes:
Sn → a/1 − r − 0
6
Therefore, the sum to infinity is:
S∞ = a/1 − r , where −1 < r < 1

✓ This is our sum to infinity formula!

⚠️ Important Condition:

The sum to infinity only exists when −1 < r < 1 (i.e., |r| < 1). This is called a convergent geometric series.

If |r| ≥ 1, the series diverges and has no finite sum.
