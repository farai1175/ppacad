Double Angle Identities

📖 Week 11
📝 Lesson 1
⏱️ 60 minutes

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Derive the double angle identity for sine: sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A
  • Derive the double angle identities for cosine: cos 2A = cos²A − sin²A = 2cos²A − 1 = 1 − 2sin²A
  • Apply double angle identities to simplify trigonometric expressions
  • Solve problems involving double angle identities

📋 Overview

Double angle identities are special cases of compound angle identities where both angles are equal. When we add an angle to itself, we get a "double angle." These identities are fundamental tools in trigonometry, calculus, and physics, allowing us to express trigonometric functions of 2A in terms of functions of A.

💡 Key Insight: Double angle identities are derived from compound angle identities by setting both angles equal to the same value.

🔄 Prerequisites Review

Before we derive double angle identities, let's review the compound angle identities we'll use:

Cosine Compound Angle Identities
cos(P + Q) = cos P cos Q − sin P sin Q
cos(P − Q) = cos P cos Q + sin P sin Q
Sine Compound Angle Identities
sin(P + Q) = sin P cos Q + cos P sin Q
sin(P − Q) = sin P cos Q − cos P sin Q
Pythagorean Identity
sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

📚 Main Concepts & Derivations

Deriving sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A

Starting point: We use sin(P + Q) = sin P cos Q + cos P sin Q

↓

Let P = A and Q = A:

sin(A + A) = sin A cos A + cos A sin A
↓

Simplify the left side:

sin 2A = sin A cos A + cos A sin A
↓

Combine like terms:

sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A
📝 Worked Example 1

Expand and simplify: sin(3x + 3x)

1 Recognize that 3x + 3x = 6x, so we're finding sin 6x
2 Apply the double angle identity with A = 3x:
sin 6x = 2 sin 3x cos 3x

Deriving cos 2A = cos²A − sin²A

Starting point: We use cos(P + Q) = cos P cos Q − sin P sin Q

↓

Let P = A and Q = A:

cos(A + A) = cos A cos A − sin A sin A
↓

Simplify:

cos 2A = cos²A − sin²A
📌 Remember: cos A × cos A = cos²A and sin A × sin A = sin²A

Three Forms of the Cosine Double Angle Identity

Form 1: Mixed Form

cos 2A = cos²A − sin²A

Contains both sine and cosine

Form 2: Cosine Only

cos 2A = 2cos²A − 1

Derived using sin²A = 1 − cos²A

Form 3: Sine Only

cos 2A = 1 − 2sin²A

Derived using cos²A = 1 − sin²A

Deriving Form 2: cos 2A = 2cos²A − 1

Start with: cos 2A = cos²A − sin²A

↓

Substitute sin²A = 1 − cos²A:

cos 2A = cos²A − (1 − cos²A)
↓
cos 2A = cos²A − 1 + cos²A
↓
cos 2A = 2cos²A − 1

Deriving Form 3: cos 2A = 1 − 2sin²A

Start with: cos 2A = cos²A − sin²A

↓

Substitute cos²A = 1 − sin²A:

cos 2A = (1 − sin²A) − sin²A
↓
cos 2A = 1 − 2sin²A
💡 Choosing the Right Form: The form you use depends on what information you have. If you know cos A, use Form 2. If you know sin A, use Form 3. If you know both, any form works!

📌 Summary of Double Angle Identities

sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A
cos 2A = cos²A − sin²A
cos 2A = 2cos²A − 1
cos 2A = 1 − 2sin²A

✏️ Worked Examples

📝 Example 1: Simplify using double angles

Simplify: 2 sin 15° cos 15°

1 Recognize the pattern: This matches 2 sin A cos A where A = 15°
2 Apply the identity: 2 sin A cos A = sin 2A
3 Answer: 2 sin 15° cos 15° = sin 30° = ½
📝 Example 2: Express in terms of single angle

Express cos²x − sin²x as a single trigonometric function

1 Recognize this is the form: cos 2A = cos²A − sin²A
2 Here A = x, so 2A = 2x
3 Answer: cos²x − sin²x = cos 2x
📝 Example 3: Using specific values

If sin θ = 3/5 and θ is acute, find sin 2θ and cos 2θ

1 Find cos θ using Pythagorean identity:
cos²θ = 1 − sin²θ = 1 − 9/25 = 16/25
cos θ = 4/5 (positive since θ is acute)
2 Find sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ = 2 × (3/5) × (4/5) = 24/25
3 Find cos 2θ = cos²θ − sin²θ = 16/25 − 9/25 = 7/25
4 Answers: sin 2θ = 24/25, cos 2θ = 7/25
📝 Example 4: Simplifying expressions

Simplify: 1 − 2sin²(2x)

1 Recognize the pattern: cos 2A = 1 − 2sin²A
2 Here A = 2x, so 2A = 4x
3 Answer: 1 − 2sin²(2x) = cos 4x
