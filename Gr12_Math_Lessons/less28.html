Topic: Compound Angles

Lesson 1 (30 minutes)

Compound Angle Identities

Deriving and using sin(α ± β) and cos(α ± β) identities

📚 Learning Objectives

  • Understand why sin(A + B) ≠ sin A + sin B
  • Derive and use the identity: sin(α ± β) = sin α cos β ± cos α sin β
  • Derive and use the identity: cos(α ± β) = cos α cos β ∓ sin α sin β
  • Apply compound angle identities to simplify trigonometric expressions
  • Evaluate trigonometric expressions using compound angle formulas

📋 Overview

In this lesson, we will discover one of the most common misconceptions in trigonometry and learn the correct way to handle the sine and cosine of combined angles. Many students incorrectly assume that sin(A + B) = sin A + sin B, but we will prove this is false and learn the actual compound angle identities.

⚠️ Common Misconception Alert!

Students often think: sin(A + B) = sin A + sin B

This is WRONG! Let's discover why...

📐 Main Concepts & Principles

1. Pre-Knowledge Check

Before we begin, recall:

  • CAST Diagram: Signs of trig ratios in each quadrant
  • sin A = opposite/hypotenuse (in a right triangle)
  • cos A = adjacent/hypotenuse
  • tan A = opposite/adjacent = sin A / cos A

2. Discovering the Truth

🔍 Investigation: Is sin(60° + 20°) = sin 60° + sin 20°?

Let's use a calculator to check:

Expression Calculation Value
sin(60° + 20°) sin(80°) 0.9848
sin 60° + sin 20° 0.8660 + 0.3420 1.2080

Conclusion: These are NOT equal! In fact, sin 60° + sin 20° > 1, which is impossible for sine!

🔍 Investigation: Is sin(75° - 45°) = sin 75° - sin 45°?

Expression Calculation Value
sin(75° - 45°) sin(30°) 0.5000
sin 75° - sin 45° 0.9659 - 0.7071 0.2588

Conclusion: Again, NOT equal!

3. The Compound Angle Identities for Sine

Sine Compound Angle Identities

sin(A + B) = sin A cos B + cos A sin B sin(A - B) = sin A cos B - cos A sin B

🔑 Memory Tip

For SINE: "Sin Cos Cos Sin" - the functions ALTERNATE

The sign between them is the SAME as the sign in the original expression (+ or -)

Verification: sin(60° + 20°)

Using the identity: sin(60° + 20°) = sin 60° cos 20° + cos 60° sin 20°

1 sin 60° = 0.8660, cos 20° = 0.9397

2 cos 60° = 0.5000, sin 20° = 0.3420

3 = (0.8660)(0.9397) + (0.5000)(0.3420)

4 = 0.8138 + 0.1710

5 = 0.9848 ✓

This matches sin(80°) = 0.9848!

4. The Compound Angle Identities for Cosine

🔍 Quick Check: Is cos(90° - 30°) = cos 90° - cos 30°?

  • cos(90° - 30°) = cos(60°) = 0.5
  • cos 90° - cos 30° = 0 - 0.8660 = -0.8660

Not equal! We need another formula.

Cosine Compound Angle Identities

cos(A + B) = cos A cos B - sin A sin B cos(A - B) = cos A cos B + sin A sin B

🔑 Memory Tip

For COSINE: "Cos Cos Sin Sin" - the functions stay TOGETHER

The sign between them is OPPOSITE to the sign in the original expression!

cos(A + B) uses - between terms; cos(A - B) uses +

5. Summary: All Four Identities

SINE ADDITION
sin(A + B) = sin A cos B + cos A sin B
SINE SUBTRACTION
sin(A - B) = sin A cos B - cos A sin B
COSINE ADDITION
cos(A + B) = cos A cos B - sin A sin B
COSINE SUBTRACTION
cos(A - B) = cos A cos B + sin A sin B

6. Using Identities with Variables

Examples with Variables

Expression Expanded Form
sin(x + y) sin x cos y + cos x sin y
sin(2α - θ) sin 2α cos θ - cos 2α sin θ
cos(P + Q) cos P cos Q - sin P sin Q
cos(P - 2Q) cos P cos 2Q + sin P sin 2Q
📝 Note: Notice the pattern! The sign rule stays consistent whether we use numbers or variables.
