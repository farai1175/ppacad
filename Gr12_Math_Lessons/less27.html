The Power Law & Real-Life Applications

Using logarithms to solve compound interest and carbon dating problems

  • Use logarithms to solve compound interest problems
  • Use logarithms to solve carbon dating problems
  • Apply the power law to solve real-life exponential equations
  • Calculate the time required for investments to reach target values
  • Determine the age of fossils using radioactive decay formulas

📋 Overview

In the previous lesson, we learned the definition of logarithms and how to convert between logarithmic and exponential forms. In this lesson, we will learn the power law of logarithms - one of the most important laws because it allows us to solve equations where the unknown is in the exponent.

🎯 Why is the Power Law Important?

The power law allows us to "bring down" exponents, making them multipliers. This is essential for solving real-world problems like:

  • Compound Interest: How long until my investment doubles?
  • Carbon Dating: How old is this fossil?
  • Population Growth: When will the population reach a certain size?
  • Radioactive Decay: What is the half-life of a substance?

2. The Power Law of Logarithms

Power Law

logaxn = n · logax

The exponent comes down as a multiplier!

📝 Proof of the Power Law (For Understanding)

Let logax = p, then ap = x

Raise both sides to power m: (ap)m = xm

Simplify left side: apm = xm

Convert back to log form: logaxm = pm

Substitute p = logax: logaxm = m · logax ✓

✓ Verification Example

Show that log5252 = 2·log525

Left Side (LHS) Right Side (RHS)
log5252 = log5625 2·log525 = 2 × 2
Since 54 = 625, this equals 4 = 4
LHS = RHS ✓

3. Application 1: Compound Interest

💰 Real-World Application

Compound Interest Formula

F = P(1 + i)n

Where:

  • F = Future value (total amount)
  • P = Present value (initial investment)
  • i = Interest rate (as a decimal, e.g., 10% = 0.10)
  • n = Number of years

Worked Example: Investment Doubling Time

Problem: If I invest R5,000 at a compound interest rate of 10% annually, how long will it take for this amount to grow to R10,000?

1 Identify known values:

P = R5,000, F = R10,000, i = 10% = 0.10, n = ?

2 Substitute into formula:

10000 = 5000(1 + 0.10)n

3 Simplify:

10000/5000 = (1.1)n

2 = (1.1)n

4 Take log of both sides:

log(2) = log(1.1)n

5 Apply power law:

log(2) = n · log(1.1)

6 Solve for n:

n = log(2) / log(1.1)

n = 0.3010 / 0.0414

n ≈ 7.27 years

Answer: It will take approximately 7.27 years (or about 7 years and 3 months) for the investment to double.

4. Application 2: Carbon Dating

🦴 Real-World Application

Carbon Dating Formula

R = R0 × 10-kt

Where:

  • R = Current ratio of ¹⁴C to ¹²C in the sample
  • R0 = Normal ratio of ¹⁴C to ¹²C in the atmosphere
  • k = Decay constant = 0.000121
  • t = Age of the fossil in years
📝 Background: Carbon-14 (¹⁴C) is a radioactive isotope that decays over time. Living organisms maintain the same ¹⁴C/¹²C ratio as the atmosphere. After death, ¹⁴C decays, so measuring the remaining ratio tells us how old a specimen is.

Worked Example: Dating a Fossil

Problem: An archaeologist found a bone fragment in which the ratio of ¹⁴C to ¹²C is 1/5 of the ratio in the atmosphere. How old is this bone fragment?

1 Express given information:

R = R0/5 (the current ratio is 1/5 of the atmospheric ratio)

2 Substitute into formula:

R0/5 = R0 × 10-0.000121t

3 Divide both sides by R0:

1/5 = 10-0.000121t

4 Take log of both sides:

log(1/5) = log(10-0.000121t)

5 Apply power law:

log(1/5) = -0.000121t × log(10)

(Note: log(10) = 1)

6 Solve for t:

t = log(1/5) / (-0.000121)

t = -0.6990 / (-0.000121)

t ≈ 5,776.61 years

Answer: The bone fragment is approximately 5,777 years old.

5. Summary: The Method

🔑 Steps to Solve Exponential Equations Using Logs

  1. Isolate the exponential expression on one side
  2. Take log of both sides
  3. Apply power law to bring the exponent down
  4. Solve for the unknown
