Geometric Sequences (Lesson 1)

Grade: 12
Week: Term 1 Week 2

Topic: Geometric Sequences

Introduction to geometric sequences: Identifying patterns, deriving formulas, and solving problems

🎯 Lesson Objectives

▼

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Revise concepts and formulae learnt for arithmetic sequences and series
  • Identify a geometric sequence and define a geometric sequence
  • Derive and use the formula for the general term of a geometric sequence
  • Use the general term formula to find the value of a term given n
  • Find n given the value of a term in a geometric sequence
  • Calculate the common ratio of geometric sequences

📝 Arithmetic Sequences Revision

▼

Before learning about geometric sequences, let's revise arithmetic sequences:

📐 First Term

a = T₁

The first term in the sequence

↔️ Common Difference

d = T₂ - T₁

Constant difference between terms

🔢 General Term

Tₙ = a + (n-1)d

Formula for nth term

∑ Sum of Series

Sₙ = n/2 [2a + (n-1)d]

Sum of first n terms
Key Facts about Arithmetic Sequences:
  • Each term is calculated by adding the same number (constant) to the previous term
  • The common difference (d) can be positive, negative, or zero
  • Arithmetic mean between a and b = (a + b)/2
  • If given the last term (l), sum formula is: Sₙ = n/2 (a + l)

  • In sequence 2: Multiply each term by 3 to get the next term
  • In sequence 3: Multiply each term by 2 to get the next term

These are Geometric Sequences - each term is obtained by multiplying the previous term by a constant number called the common ratio (r)

Common Ratio Formula:
r = Tₖ₊₁ ÷ Tₖ Divide any term by the previous term to find the common ratio

📚 Key Theory & Formulas

▼

Geometric Sequence Definition

A geometric sequence is a sequence of numbers where each term after the first is found by multiplying the previous term by a constant called the common ratio (r).

General Term Formula

Tₙ = a × rⁿ⁻¹ where: a = first term, r = common ratio, n = position of term

Important Notes:

  • r > 1: Sequence increases (grows rapidly)
  • 0 < r < 1: Sequence decreases (gets smaller)
  • r < 0: Sequence alternates between positive and negative
  • r = 1: All terms are equal (constant sequence)
  • Always use brackets when substituting: Tₙ = (a) × (r)ⁿ⁻¹
Common Ratio Calculation:
r = T₂ ÷ T₁ = T₃ ÷ T₂ = Tₙ ÷ Tₙ₋₁ The ratio is constant throughout the sequence

📊 Worked Examples

▼
Example 1: If a = 5 and r = -3, find T₈
  • Given: a = 5, r = -3, n = 8
  • Formula: Tₙ = a × rⁿ⁻¹
  • T₈ = (5) × (-3)⁸⁻¹ = 5 × (-3)⁷
  • (-3)⁷ = -2187 (negative because odd exponent)
  • T₈ = 5 × (-2187) = -10,935
  • Use calculator: 5 × (-3)^7 = -10,935
Example 2: Given sequence: 2; 6; 18; 54; ... Find T₂₁
  • First term: a = 2
  • Common ratio: r = 6 ÷ 2 = 3
  • Position: n = 21
  • T₂₁ = 2 × 3²¹⁻¹ = 2 × 3²⁰
  • 3²⁰ = 3,486,784,401
  • T₂₁ = 2 × 3,486,784,401 = 6,973,568,802
  • Always check: 2×3=6, 6×3=18, 18×3=54 ✓
Example 3: Given sequence: 9; 3; 1; ... Find T₆
  • First term: a = 9
  • Common ratio: r = 3 ÷ 9 = ⅓ (decreasing sequence)
  • Position: n = 6
  • T₆ = 9 × (⅓)⁶⁻¹ = 9 × (⅓)⁵
  • (⅓)⁵ = 1/243
  • T₆ = 9 × (1/243) = 9/243 = 1/27
  • Note: r is a fraction for decreasing sequences
Example 4: Which term in sequence: 3; 9; 27; ... equals 243?
  • First term: a = 3
  • Common ratio: r = 9 ÷ 3 = 3
  • Given: Tₙ = 243
  • Formula: Tₙ = 3 × 3ⁿ⁻¹ = 3ⁿ (since 3 × 3ⁿ⁻¹ = 3¹ × 3ⁿ⁻¹ = 3ⁿ)
  • Set 3ⁿ = 243
  • 243 = 3⁵ (because 3×3×3×3×3 = 243)
  • So 3ⁿ = 3⁵
  • Therefore n = 5 (equating exponents)
  • The fifth term is 243

✓ Comprehensive Quiz (12 Questions)

▼

Test your understanding of geometric sequences. Select an option and get instant feedback.

Question 1: What is the common ratio in sequence: 3; 15; 75; 375?

A) 3
B) 5
C) 15
D) 25
Explanation: 15 ÷ 3 = 5, 75 ÷ 15 = 5, 375 ÷ 75 = 5. Common ratio = 5.

Question 2: What is the general term formula for geometric sequences?

A) Tₙ = a + (n-1)d
B) Tₙ = a × rⁿ⁻¹
C) Tₙ = an + b
D) Tₙ = an² + bn + c
Explanation: Geometric sequence formula: Tₙ = a × rⁿ⁻¹ where a = first term, r = common ratio.

Question 3: If a = 5 and r = -3, what is T₈?

A) 10,935
B) -10,935
C) -3,645
D) 3,645
Explanation: T₈ = 5 × (-3)⁷ = 5 × (-2187) = -10,935. Negative because odd exponent of negative number.

Question 4: Find T₂₁ for sequence: 2; 6; 18; 54; ...

A) 1,162,261,467
B) 3,486,784,401
C) 6,973,568,802
D) 10,460,353,203
Explanation: a = 2, r = 3, T₂₁ = 2 × 3²⁰ = 2 × 3,486,784,401 = 6,973,568,802.

Question 5: Which term in sequence: 3; 9; 27; ... equals 243?

A) 4th term
B) 5th term
C) 6th term
D) 7th term
Explanation: 3 × 3ⁿ⁻¹ = 243 → 3ⁿ⁻¹ = 81 → 3ⁿ⁻¹ = 3⁴ → n-1 = 4 → n = 5.

Question 6: For sequence 9; 3; 1; ..., what is the common ratio?

A) ⅓
B) 3
C) -3
D) -⅓
Explanation: r = 3 ÷ 9 = ⅓. For decreasing sequences, r is a fraction between 0 and 1.

Question 7: If a geometric sequence has r = 2 and T₅ = 48, what is a?

A) 2
B) 4
C) 3
D) 6
Explanation: T₅ = a × 2⁴ = 48 → a × 16 = 48 → a = 48 ÷ 16 = 3.

Question 8: What is T₆ for sequence: 9; 3; 1; ...?

A) 1/9
B) 1/81
C) 1/27
D) 1/3
Explanation: T₆ = 9 × (⅓)⁵ = 9 × (1/243) = 9/243 = 1/27.

Question 9: Which sequence is geometric?

A) 2, 4, 6, 8, 10
B) 2, 4, 8, 16, 32
C) 2, 5, 8, 11, 14
D) 2, 6, 18, 38, 66
Explanation: B has constant ratio: 4÷2=2, 8÷4=2, 16÷8=2, 32÷16=2. Others don't have constant ratios.

Question 10: If r = -2 and T₃ = 12, what is T₁?

A) 3
B) -3
C) 3 (Wait: T₃ = a × (-2)² = a × 4 = 12 → a = 3)
D) -6
Explanation: T₃ = a × r² = a × (-2)² = a × 4 = 12 → a = 3.

Question 11: How do you calculate common ratio r?

A) Subtract consecutive terms
B) Divide consecutive terms
C) Average of terms
D) Square root of product
Explanation: For geometric sequences: r = T₂ ÷ T₁ = T₃ ÷ T₂ = Tₙ ÷ Tₙ₋₁

Question 12: Which term in sequence: 2; 6; 18; ... equals 4374?

A) 6th term
B) 7th term
C) 8th term
D) 9th term
Explanation: 2 × 3ⁿ⁻¹ = 4374 → 3ⁿ⁻¹ = 2187 → 3ⁿ⁻¹ = 3⁷ → n-1 = 7 → n = 8.
