Lesson 4 & 5

Inverse of Exponential Functions

Exploring the relationship between exponential functions and their inverses

📚 Learning Objectives

  • Demonstrate the ability to work with various types of functions and relations, including inverses
  • Investigate and generate graphs of inverse relations of functions, specifically the inverse of y = aˣ (where a > 0)
  • Identify characteristics of graphs: domain, range, intercepts with axes, and asymptotes
  • Apply rules of reflection to exponential functions and determine equations of resultant images
  • Understand the relationship between exponential functions and their inverses

📋 Overview

In this lesson, we will explore the inverse of exponential functions. We'll begin by revising key exponent definitions, then examine the characteristics of exponential functions before investigating their inverses. Understanding the relationship between a function and its inverse is crucial for working with logarithms, which we'll explore in subsequent lessons.

📐 Main Concepts & Principles

2. Characteristics of Exponential Functions

For the exponential function y = aˣ where a > 0 and a ≠ 1

  • Domain: x ∈ (-∞, ∞) or x ∈ ℝ (all real numbers)
  • Range: y ∈ (0, ∞) (all positive real numbers)
  • x-intercept: None (the graph never crosses the x-axis)
  • y-intercept: (0, 1) since a⁰ = 1
  • Asymptote: The x-axis (y = 0) is a horizontal asymptote
  • Function type: One-to-one function

3. Two Types of Exponential Functions

y = aˣ for a > 1

Increasing function: As x increases, y increases

Examples: y = 2ˣ, y = 3ˣ, y = eˣ

y = aˣ for 0 < a < 1

Decreasing function: As x increases, y decreases

Examples: y = (½)ˣ, y = (⅓)ˣ, y = (0.5)ˣ

4. Reflections of Exponential Functions

Reflection about the y-axis

y = 2-x is the reflection of y = 2x about the y-axis

This is because x has been replaced by -x

Note: 2-x = (½)x

Reflection about the x-axis

y = -2x is the reflection of y = 2x about the x-axis

This is because y has been replaced by -y

⚠️ Remember: -ax ≠ a-x

5. Finding the Equation of an Exponential Function

Worked Example 1

Question: The graph y = aˣ passes through the point (4, 81). Find a.

Step 1: Since (4, 81) lies on the graph, it satisfies the equation y = aˣ
Step 2: Substitute: 81 = a⁴
Step 3: Take the 4th root: a = 81¼
Step 4: Simplify: a = (3⁴)¼ = 3
Step 5: Since a > 0, we have a = 3

∴ The equation is y = 3ˣ

6. The Inverse of an Exponential Function

Since the exponential function y = aˣ is one-to-one, its inverse will also be a one-to-one function.

Finding the Inverse

Original function: y = aˣ

To find the inverse: swap x and y

Inverse: x = ay

(This can be written as y = logax)

Worked Example 2: Graphing y = 2ˣ and its Inverse

Using the table method to show the swap of x and y values:

y = 2ˣ (Original Function)
x -1 0 1
y ½ 1 2
x = 2y (Inverse Function)
x ½ 1 2
y -1 0 1

Key Observations:

  • The inverse is the reflection of the original about the line y = x
  • For the original: Domain is ℝ, Range is (0, ∞)
  • For the inverse: Domain is (0, ∞), Range is ℝ
  • The y-intercept of the original becomes the x-intercept of the inverse
