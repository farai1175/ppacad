Advanced geometric sequences: Solving simultaneous equations, variable terms, and complex applications

🎯 Lesson Objectives

▼

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Given two terms of a geometric sequence, solve simultaneously to determine the sequence
  • Apply the general term formula to find another term given n
  • Apply the definition of a geometric sequence to find terms when expressed in terms of a variable
  • Use the general term formula in a variety of complex questions
  • Solve geometric sequence problems involving algebraic expressions
  • Work with simultaneous equations derived from geometric sequences

📝 Revision & Homework Review

▼
Geometric Sequence General Term Formula:
Tₙ = a × rⁿ⁻¹ where: a = first term, r = common ratio, n = position of term

Key Concepts to Recall:

  • Geometric sequence: Each term is obtained by multiplying the previous term by constant ratio r
  • Common ratio: r = T₂ ÷ T₁ = T₃ ÷ T₂ = Tₙ ÷ Tₙ₋₁
  • Always use brackets when substituting values into the formula
  • For decreasing sequences: 0 < r < 1 (r is a fraction)
  • For alternating sequences: r < 0 (negative ratio)
Common Homework Problem Areas:
  • Forgetting to use brackets: Tₙ = (a) × (r)ⁿ⁻¹
  • Miscalculating r for decreasing sequences
  • Not checking if sequence is actually geometric
  • Algebraic manipulation errors when solving for n

⚖️ Solving Simultaneous Equations

▼

Example 1: Find Sequence from Two Terms

Problem: The third term of a geometric sequence is 9 and the sixth term is 243. Find the tenth term.

(1) T₃ = a × r² = 9
(2) T₆ = a × r⁵ = 243
Solution Steps:
  • We have two equations with two unknowns (a and r)
  • Divide equation (2) by equation (1) to eliminate 'a':
  • (a × r⁵) ÷ (a × r²) = 243 ÷ 9
  • Simplify: r³ = 27
  • Therefore: r = ³√27 = 3
  • Substitute r = 3 into equation (1): a × 3² = 9 → a × 9 = 9 → a = 1
  • Now find T₁₀: T₁₀ = 1 × 3⁹ = 3⁹ = 19,683

Important Strategy

When given Tₘ and Tₙ:
Divide: Tₙ ÷ Tₘ = (a × rⁿ⁻¹) ÷ (a × rᵐ⁻¹) = rⁿ⁻ᵐ This eliminates 'a' and leaves only r to solve for
Alternative Example:

If T₄ = 16 and T₇ = 128, find the sequence.

  • T₇ ÷ T₄ = (a × r⁶) ÷ (a × r³) = 128 ÷ 16
  • r³ = 8 → r = 2
  • T₄ = a × 2³ = 16 → a × 8 = 16 → a = 2
  • Sequence: 2; 4; 8; 16; 32; ...

🔤 Geometric Sequences with Variable Terms

▼

Example 2: Terms Expressed in Terms of x

Problem: x+1; 2x+2; 6x-4 are the first three terms of a geometric sequence. Find x, if x > 0, and hence the first three terms.

Solution:
  • Since it's a geometric sequence, the ratio between consecutive terms is constant:
  • (2x+2) ÷ (x+1) = (6x-4) ÷ (2x+2)
  • Cross multiply: (2x+2) × (2x+2) = (6x-4) × (x+1)
  • Expand: 4x² + 8x + 4 = 6x² + 6x - 4x - 4
  • Simplify: 4x² + 8x + 4 = 6x² + 2x - 4
  • Rearrange: 0 = 2x² - 6x - 8
  • Divide by 2: 0 = x² - 3x - 4
  • Factorize: 0 = (x - 4)(x + 1)
  • Solutions: x = 4 or x = -1
  • Since x > 0: x = 4
Find the Sequence:
  • T₁ = x + 1 = 4 + 1 = 5
  • T₂ = 2x + 2 = 2(4) + 2 = 8 + 2 = 10
  • T₃ = 6x - 4 = 6(4) - 4 = 24 - 4 = 20
  • Check ratio: 10 ÷ 5 = 2, 20 ÷ 10 = 2 ✓
  • Sequence: 5; 10; 20; 40; ... with r = 2

General Approach for Variable Terms

For three consecutive terms T₁, T₂, T₃:
(T₂)² = T₁ × T₃ In a geometric sequence, the middle term squared equals product of adjacent terms
Using the Shortcut:

For x+1; 2x+2; 6x-4:

  • (2x+2)² = (x+1) × (6x-4)
  • 4x² + 8x + 4 = 6x² + 2x - 4
  • 0 = 2x² - 6x - 8
  • Same equation as before! (This is faster)

📝 Class Work (Exercise 1.4)

2(a): The 2nd term of a geometric sequence is 6 and the 5th term is 162. Find the 8th term.

7(a): The first three terms of a geometric sequence are: k+1; 2k-2; 3k+1. Find k and the sequence.

9(a): In a geometric sequence, T₃ = 20 and T₆ = 160. Find T₁₀.

Solutions to Class Work:

2(a): T₂ = 6, T₅ = 162

  • T₅ ÷ T₂ = a×r⁴ ÷ a×r¹ = r³ = 162 ÷ 6 = 27
  • r = ³√27 = 3
  • T₂ = a×3¹ = 6 → a = 2
  • T₈ = 2 × 3⁷ = 2 × 2187 = 4,374

7(a): k+1; 2k-2; 3k+1 are geometric

  • (2k-2)² = (k+1)(3k+1)
  • 4k² - 8k + 4 = 3k² + k + 3k + 1
  • 4k² - 8k + 4 = 3k² + 4k + 1
  • k² - 12k + 3 = 0
  • Solve quadratic: k = 6 ± √33

9(a): T₃ = 20, T₆ = 160

  • T₆ ÷ T₃ = r³ = 160 ÷ 20 = 8
  • r = 2
  • T₃ = a×2² = 20 → 4a = 20 → a = 5
  • T₁₀ = 5 × 2⁹ = 5 × 512 = 2,560

Question 4: For x+1; 2x+2; 6x-4 to be geometric, what equation must hold?

C) (6x-4) - (2x+2) = (2x+2) - (x+1)
D) (x+1)(2x+2) = (6x-4)²
Explanation: For geometric sequence: (middle term)² = (first term) × (third term).

Question 5: What is x if x+1; 2x+2; 6x-4 are geometric and x > 0?

A) 2
B) 3
C) 4
D) 5
Explanation: (2x+2)² = (x+1)(6x-4) → 4x²+8x+4 = 6x²+2x-4 → 2x²-6x-8=0 → x²-3x-4=0 → (x-4)(x+1)=0 → x=4 or x=-1 → x=4 since x>0.

Question 6: What is T₂ for the sequence x+1; 2x+2; 6x-4 when x=4?

A) 5
B) 10
C) 20
D) 40
Explanation: T₂ = 2x+2 = 2(4)+2 = 8+2 = 10.

Question 7: If T₂=6 and T₅=162, what is r?
