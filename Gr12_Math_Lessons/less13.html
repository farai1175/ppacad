📊 Understanding Different Types of Means

▼

What are Means?

In mathematics, a mean is a value that represents the "middle" or "average" of a set of numbers. When we insert terms between two numbers to form a sequence, these inserted terms are called means.

➕ Arithmetic Mean

Definition: The term(s) inserted between two numbers to form an arithmetic sequence.

Formula: For numbers a and b, the arithmetic mean is:

AM = (a + b)/2

Key Property: Equal spacing between terms.

✖️ Geometric Mean

Definition: The term(s) inserted between two numbers to form a geometric sequence.

Formula: For numbers a and c, the geometric mean is:

GM = √(a·c)

Key Property: Equal multiplicative spacing between terms.

💡 Important Distinction

Arithmetic Means: Create equal differences between terms.

Geometric Means: Create equal ratios between terms.

The type of mean determines whether the resulting sequence will be arithmetic or geometric.

Type Sequence Created Relationship Example
Arithmetic Mean Arithmetic Sequence Constant difference: T₂ - T₁ = T₃ - T₂ 2, 5, 8 (difference = 3)
Geometric Mean Geometric Sequence Constant ratio: T₂/T₁ = T₃/T₂ 2, 4, 8 (ratio = 2)

➕ Arithmetic Mean Formula & Examples

▼

The Arithmetic Mean Formula

For two numbers a and b, the arithmetic mean is simply the average of the two numbers:

Arithmetic Mean Formula

AM = (a + b)/2

This creates three terms: a, (a+b)/2, b that form an arithmetic sequence

Example: Simple Arithmetic Mean

Find the arithmetic mean of 8 and 20
  • Identify a and b:
    a = 8, b = 20
  • Apply the formula:
    AM = (a + b)/2
    AM = (8 + 20)/2
    AM = 28/2 = 14
  • Verify the arithmetic sequence:
    Sequence: 8, 14, 20
    Common difference: 14 - 8 = 6
    20 - 14 = 6 ✓

Inserting Multiple Arithmetic Means

When we need to insert more than one arithmetic mean between two numbers, we create a longer arithmetic sequence.

Insert 2 arithmetic means between 3 and 15
  • Set up the sequence:
    We want: 3, _, _, 15
    This is 4 terms total
  • Use arithmetic sequence formula:
    T₁ = a = 3
    T₄ = a + 3d = 15
    3 + 3d = 15
  • Solve for d:
    3d = 12
    d = 4
  • Find the means:
    T₂ = 3 + 4 = 7
    T₃ = 3 + 8 = 11
    Sequence: 3, 7, 11, 15

📌 General Formula for Multiple Means

To insert k arithmetic means between a and b:

• Total terms = k + 2

• Common difference: d = (b - a)/(k + 1)

• Means: a + d, a + 2d, ..., a + kd

✖️ Geometric Mean Formula & Examples

▼

The Geometric Mean Formula

For two numbers a and c, the geometric mean is the square root of their product:

Geometric Mean Formula

GM = √(a·c)

This creates three terms: a, √(a·c), c that form a geometric sequence

💡 Alternative Derivation

If a, b, and c form a geometric sequence, then:

b/a = c/b (common ratio is constant)

Cross-multiplying: b² = a·c

Therefore: b = √(a·c)

The geometric mean is also called the mean proportional.

Example: Simple Geometric Mean

Find the geometric mean of 2 and 8
  • Identify a and c:
    a = 2, c = 8
  • Apply the formula:
    GM = √(a·c)
    GM = √(2 × 8)
    GM = √16 = 4
  • Verify the geometric sequence:
    Possible sequences: 2, 4, 8 (r = 2) or 2, -4, 8 (r = -2)
    Both satisfy b² = a·c

⚠️ Important: Two Possible Values

Unlike arithmetic mean which has one unique value, geometric mean can have two possible values:

• Positive: √(a·c)

• Negative: -√(a·c)

Both satisfy b² = a·c and create valid geometric sequences!

Inserting Multiple Geometric Means

When inserting multiple geometric means, we create a longer geometric sequence.

Insert 2 geometric means between 7 and -56
  • Set up the sequence:
    We want: 7, _, _, -56
    This is 4 terms total
  • Use geometric sequence formula:
    T₁ = a = 7
    T₄ = a·r³ = -56
    7·r³ = -56
  • Solve for r:
    r³ = -8
    r = -2 (cube root of -8)
  • Find the means:
    T₂ = 7 × (-2) = -14
    T₃ = 7 × (-2)² = 28
    Sequence: 7, -14, 28, -56

📌 General Formula for Multiple Means

To insert k geometric means between a and b:

• Total terms = k + 2

• Common ratio: r = (b/a)^(1/(k+1))

• Means: a·r, a·r², ..., a·rᵏ

📝 Comprehensive Worked Examples

▼

Example 1: Geometric Mean with Variables

Find the geometric mean of 2a and 98a
  • Identify a and c:
    a = 2a, c = 98a
  • Apply the formula:
    GM = √(a·c)
    GM = √(2a × 98a)
    GM = √(196a²)
  • Simplify:
    GM = √(196) × √(a²)
    GM = 14 × |a|
    Answer: 14|a| (two possible sequences)

Example 2: Inserting 3 Geometric Means

Insert 3 geometric means between 3 and 189
  • Set up the sequence:
    We want: 3, _, _, _, 189
    5 terms total
  • Use geometric sequence formula:
    T₁ = a = 3
    T₅ = a·r⁴ = 189
    3·r⁴ = 189
  • Solve for r:
    r⁴ = 63
    r = ±(63)^(1/4)
  • Find the means:
    T₂ = 3 × r
    T₃ = 3 × r²
    T₄ = 3 × r³
    Sequence depends on whether r is positive or negative

Example 3: Finding Missing Terms

Find a and b in the geometric sequence: 9, a, b, 72
  • Set up relationships:
    Since it's geometric: a/9 = b/a = 72/b
  • Use the property b² = a·c:
    Here, a and b are means between 9 and 72
    For three terms: 9, x, 72: x² = 9×72 = 648
    x = ±√648 = ±18√2
  • Find sequence pattern:
    If x = 18√2, then r = (18√2)/9 = 2√2
    Sequence: 9, 18√2, 72, ... wait, 72/(18√2) = 4/√2 = 2√2 ✓
    So a = 18√2, b = 72
  • Check negative possibility:
    If x = -18√2, then r = -2√2
    Sequence: 9, -18√2, 72, ... 72/(-18√2) = -4/√2 = -2√2 ✓
    So a = -18√2, b = 72 also works
