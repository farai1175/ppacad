Lesson Overview

This lesson focuses on identifying and understanding different types of forces, with special emphasis on the normal force. You will learn how to analyze forces on both horizontal and inclined planes.

Learning Objectives

  • Outline Newton's laws and their applications
  • Identify and define different kinds of forces: weight, normal force, friction, tension, and applied force
  • Define normal force as the force perpendicular to a surface
  • Analyze forces on horizontal and inclined planes
  • Draw and interpret free-body diagrams

Key Concept

The normal force (N) is defined as the force or the component of a force which a surface exerts on an object in contact with it, and which is perpendicular to the surface.

Definitions and Scientific Principles

w Weight (w)

The gravitational force exerted by the Earth on an object. It is a non-contact force that always acts vertically downwards toward the center of the Earth.

Formula: w = mg

N Normal Force (N)

The force exerted by a surface on an object in contact with it, acting perpendicular to the surface. It prevents objects from passing through surfaces.

f Friction (f)

The force that opposes motion (or tendency of motion) of an object and acts parallel to the surface.

T Tension (T)

The pulling force exerted by ropes, strings, or cables. It always acts away from the object along the length of the rope.

F Applied Force (FA)

A general force exerted on an object by a person or another object. It can act in any direction.

Main Body

Introduction to Force Analysis

In mechanics, to apply Newton's laws effectively, we must first identify all the different types of forces acting on an object. Forces are vector quantities, having both magnitude and direction, and are measured in Newtons (N).

I. Different Kinds of Forces

Understanding the nature and direction of the forces acting on an object is the essential first step in applying Newton's laws.

1

Weight (w)

The gravitational force exerted by the Earth on an object. It is a non-contact force and always acts vertically downwards, towards the centre of the Earth.

2

Normal Force (N)

The force exerted by a surface on an object in contact with it, acting perpendicular to the surface.

3

Tension (T)

This is the pulling force exerted by a rope, cable, or string. The tension force is always directed away from the object along the length of the rope.

4

Friction (f)

The force that opposes the motion (or tendency of motion) of an object relative to the surface it is in contact with. It acts parallel to the surface.

5

Applied Force (FA)

A general force exerted on an object by a person or another object.

II. Definition and Application of the Normal Force (N)

The normal force (N) is defined as the force or the component of a force which a surface exerts on an object in contact with it, and which is perpendicular to the surface. This characteristic—being perpendicular to the surface—is crucial and remains irrespective of whether the plane is horizontal or inclined.

Horizontal Planes

On a horizontal plane:

  • The normal force (N) acts vertically upwards, perpendicular to the surface
  • The only forces acting perpendicular to the plane are the weight (w) and the normal force (N)
  • All other forces (applied force, friction, tension) should be drawn parallel to the plane

Inclined Planes

On an inclined plane:

  • The normal force (N) still acts perpendicular to the inclined surface
  • The weight (w) can be resolved into two components:
    • Perpendicular to the plane: w cos θ
    • Parallel to the plane: w sin θ
  • The only forces acting perpendicular to the plane are the normal force (N) and the perpendicular component of weight (w cos θ)

Weight Components on Inclined Plane:

Perpendicular component: \[ w_{\perp} = w \cos \theta \]

Parallel component: \[ w_{\parallel} = w \sin \theta \]
