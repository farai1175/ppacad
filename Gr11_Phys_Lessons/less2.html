Lesson Overview

This lesson focuses on calculating the magnitude and direction of a resultant vector from its perpendicular components. You will learn how to apply Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometric ratios to completely define a resultant vector.

Learning Objectives

  • Define resultant vector and explain its significance
  • Calculate the magnitude of a resultant vector using Pythagoras' theorem
  • Determine the direction of a resultant vector using trigonometric ratios
  • Interpret vector directions in different quadrants
  • Apply the component method to solve vector problems

Key Concept

The resultant vector is a single vector that has the same effect as all the other vectors acting together. When we have perpendicular components Rx and Ry, we can find the resultant R using Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry.

Main Body

Introduction to Resultant Vectors

In Physical Sciences, particularly in the Mechanics module for Grade 11, we transition from analyzing vectors in one dimension to understanding vectors in two dimensions. When dealing with multiple vectors that are not collinear or perpendicular, the component method is the most effective approach for finding the overall resultant.

This method simplifies the problem by finding the net effect in the horizontal direction (Rx) and the net effect in the vertical direction (Ry). Once Rx and Ry are determined, they are perpendicular vectors, forming the two sides of a right-angled triangle where the resultant (R) is the hypotenuse.

Key Formulas:

Magnitude: \[ R = \sqrt{R_x^2 + R_y^2} \]

Direction: \[ \theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{R_y}{R_x}\right) \]

Steps for Calculating Resultant Magnitude and Direction

1

Sketch the Components (Rx and Ry)

The net vertical vector (Ry) and the resultant horizontal vector (Rx) must be sketched on a Cartesian plane.

  • Plot the components: Draw Rx along the x-axis and Ry along the y-axis, starting at the origin (or in the correct quadrant based on their positive/negative directions).
  • Form the resultant vector (R): To find the resultant R of these two perpendicular vectors, we apply vector addition using either the tail-to-head or tail-to-tail method.
2

Calculate the Magnitude of the Resultant (R)

Since the resultant components Rx and Ry are perpendicular, the magnitude of the resultant (R) is found by treating them as the two non-hypotenuse sides of a right-angled triangle and applying the Theorem of Pythagoras.

\[ R^2 = R_x^2 + R_y^2 \]

\[ R = \sqrt{R_x^2 + R_y^2} \]

The final calculated magnitude must be stated with appropriate units, e.g., N for force.

3

Determine the Direction of the Resultant (θ)

The direction of the resultant vector (R) is typically expressed as the angle (θ) it makes with the horizontal axis (Rx). This is calculated using simple trigonometric ratios, specifically the tangent function, based on the right-angled triangle formed in the sketch.

\[ \tan \theta = \frac{\text{opposite side}}{\text{adjacent side}} = \frac{R_y}{R_x} \]

\[ \theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{R_y}{R_x}\right) \]

Important Note on Direction: When stating the final answer, the angle must be specified clearly with respect to a reference direction, such as "measured anticlockwise from the positive x-direction" or by stating the quadrant.

Understanding Quadrants

Quadrant I

Rx > 0, Ry > 0

θ = tan-1(Ry/Rx)

Direction: 0° to 90°

Quadrant II

Rx < 0, Ry > 0

θ = 180° + tan-1(Ry/Rx)

Direction: 90° to 180°

Quadrant III

Rx < 0, Ry < 0

θ = 180° + tan-1(Ry/Rx)

Direction: 180° to 270°

Quadrant IV

Rx > 0, Ry < 0

θ = 360° + tan-1(Ry/Rx)

Direction: 270° to 360°
