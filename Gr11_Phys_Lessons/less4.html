Key Concept

Vector resolution is the process of breaking a vector down into its components. We resolve vectors into components that, when added, give the original vector as their resultant.

Definitions and Principles

Term/Principle Definition/Description
Resolution of a Vector The process of breaking a vector down into its components. We resolve vectors into components that, when added, give the original vector as their resultant.
Horizontal Component (Rx) The component of vector R that is parallel with the x-axis. Calculated using Rx = R cos θ.
Vertical Component (Ry) The component of vector R that is perpendicular to the x-axis (in the y-direction). Calculated using Ry = R sin θ.
Angle θ The angle between the vector R and the x-axis. The angle is typically measured counter-clockwise from the positive x-axis.

Key Formulas:

Horizontal Component: \[ R_x = R \cos \theta \]

Vertical Component: \[ R_y = R \sin \theta \]

Main Body

Introduction to Vector Resolution

In physics, we often need to analyze vectors that act at angles. The resolution of a vector is the process of taking a single vector and breaking it into its equivalent component parts. This process is called the resolution of a vector into components.

It is particularly useful to resolve a vector into two perpendicular components because a change in one component does not cause a change in the other. Once resolved, the component vectors, when added together, produce the same effect as the original vector.

The Relationship Using Trigonometry

The key to resolving a vector is recognizing that the original vector and its perpendicular components form a right-angled triangle. If θ is the angle between the vector and the x-axis:

  • The horizontal component (Rx) is the side adjacent to angle θ
  • The vertical component (Ry) is the side opposite to angle θ
  • The vector magnitude (R) is the hypotenuse

Using trigonometric ratios:

\[ \cos \theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \frac{R_x}{R} \]

\[ \sin \theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \frac{R_y}{R} \]

Rearranging gives us the component formulas:

\[ R_x = R \cos \theta \]

\[ R_y = R \sin \theta \]

Component Signs and Quadrants

When calculating components, using the angle θ measured counter-clockwise from the positive x-axis (0° to 360°) automatically yields the correct sign (positive or negative) for the component based on which quadrant the vector lies in.

Quadrant I (0° to 90°)

Rx > 0, Ry > 0

Both components positive

Quadrant II (90° to 180°)

Rx < 0, Ry > 0

Horizontal negative, vertical positive

Quadrant III (180° to 270°)

Rx < 0, Ry < 0

Both components negative

Quadrant IV (270° to 360°)

Rx > 0, Ry < 0

Horizontal positive, vertical negative

Example Calculation

If a force of 12.5 N acts at 230° (third quadrant):

Rx = 12.5 cos(230°) ≈ -8.03 N (Negative x-direction)

Ry = 12.5 sin(230°) ≈ -9.58 N (Negative y-direction)

Application of Resolution

Resolution of vectors is a crucial step in the overall component method for finding the resultant of several vectors (up to four).

1

Resolve Vectors: Break down all vectors acting at an angle into their Rx and Ry components.

2

Find Net Horizontal Component: Sum all horizontal components to find ΣRx.

3

Find Net Vertical Component: Sum all vertical components to find ΣRy.

4

Calculate Final Resultant: Use Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry on ΣRx and ΣRy to find the final resultant R.
