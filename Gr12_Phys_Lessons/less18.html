🧩 Doppler Effect: Problem Solving

Sub-topic: Consolidation & Exam-Style Questions

Lesson 3: Applying the Doppler Equation to Real-World Problems

🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:

  • Apply the Doppler equation to various real-world scenarios
  • Solve problems involving police cars, ambulances, trains, and dolphins
  • Calculate unknown velocities when given observed frequencies
  • Understand and explain conceptual questions about the Doppler Effect
  • Recognize that observed frequency is independent of distance

📐 Formula Review

🔑 Key Equations

Source Approaching Listener
fL = v/(v - vS) × fS
Source Moving Away
fL = v/(v + vS) × fS
Listener Approaching Source
fL = (v + vL)/v × fS
Listener Moving Away
fL = (v - vL)/v × fS
💡
Remember: v = 340 m/s (speed of sound in air) unless otherwise stated. Always convert km/h to m/s by dividing by 3.6!

Question 1 🚔 Police Car

📋 Scenario

The siren of a police car produces a sound of frequency 420 Hz. A man sitting next to the road notices that the pitch of the sound changes as the car moves towards and then away from him. The speed of sound in air is 340 m/s.

1.1 Write down the name of the above phenomenon.
1.2 Calculate the frequency of the sound observed by the man when the car is moving towards him at 16 m/s.
1.3.1 How will the observed frequency compare with the original frequency when the car moves away? (GREATER THAN, SMALLER THAN, or EQUAL TO)
1.3.2 How will the observed frequency change as the car slows down whilst moving away? (INCREASES, DECREASES, or REMAINS THE SAME)
✅ Complete Solution
1.1 Name of Phenomenon
Doppler Effect
1.2 Calculate Observed Frequency (Approaching)

Given: fS = 420 Hz, vS = 16 m/s, v = 340 m/s

Formula: Source approaching, listener stationary

fL = v/(v - vS) × fS fL = 340/(340 - 16) × 420 fL = 340/324 × 420 fL = 1.0494 × 420 fL = 440.74 Hz
fL = 440.74 Hz
1.3.1 Comparison when Moving Away

When the source moves away, the observed frequency is lower than the source frequency.

SMALLER THAN
1.3.2 Change as Car Slows Down

As the car slows down (vS decreases), the denominator (v + vS) decreases, making the fraction larger. This means the observed frequency gets closer to the source frequency.

INCREASES
Question 2 🚨 Burglar Alarm

📋 Scenario

The siren of a burglar alarm system has a frequency of 960 Hz. During a patrol, a security officer, travelling in his car, hears the siren and approaches the house at constant velocity. A detector in his car registers the frequency as 1000 Hz. Speed of sound = 340 m/s.

2.1 Name the phenomenon that explains the change in observed frequency.
2.2 Calculate the speed at which the patrol car approaches the house.
2.3 If the car approached at a higher speed, how would the detected frequency compare to 1000 Hz? (HIGHER THAN, LOWER THAN, or EQUAL TO)
✅ Complete Solution
2.1 Name of Phenomenon
Doppler Effect
2.2 Calculate Speed of Patrol Car

Given: fS = 960 Hz, fL = 1000 Hz, v = 340 m/s

Note: The source (alarm) is stationary, listener (car) is moving towards!

fL = (v + vL)/v × fS 1000 = (340 + vL)/340 × 960 1000/960 = (340 + vL)/340 1.0417 × 340 = 340 + vL 354.17 = 340 + vL vL = 354.17 - 340 vL = 14.17 m/s
vL = 14.17 m/s
2.3 Effect of Higher Speed

If the car approaches at a higher speed, vL increases, making (v + vL) larger. This increases the observed frequency.

HIGHER THAN
Question 3 🐬 Dolphin Ultrasound

📋 Scenario

Dolphins use ultrasound to scan their environment. When a dolphin is 100 m from a rock, it emits ultrasound waves of frequency 250 kHz whilst swimming at 20 m/s towards the rock. Speed of sound in water = 1500 m/s.

3.1 Calculate the frequency of the sound waves detected by a detector on the rock.
3.2 When the dolphin is 50 m from the rock, another wave of 250 kHz is emitted. How will this frequency compare with 3.1? (HIGHER, LOWER, or REMAINS THE SAME)
✅ Complete Solution
3.1 Calculate Detected Frequency

Given: fS = 250 kHz = 250,000 Hz, vS = 20 m/s, v = 1500 m/s

Note: Dolphin (source) approaching rock (stationary detector)

fL = v/(v - vS) × fS fL = 1500/(1500 - 20) × 250000 fL = 1500/1480 × 250000 fL = 1.0135 × 250000 fL = 253,378.38 Hz ≈ 253.38 kHz
fL = 253.38 kHz (or 253,378 Hz)
3.2 Effect of Distance
🔑 Key Insight: The detected frequency is independent of the distance between the source and the observer! Only the relative velocity matters.

Since the dolphin is still moving at 20 m/s and emitting at 250 kHz, the detected frequency will be the same.

REMAINS THE SAME
Question 4 🚑 Ambulance - Advanced

📋 Scenario

An ambulance with its siren on moves away at constant velocity from a person standing next to the road. The person measures a frequency which is 90% of the frequency of the sound emitted by the siren. Speed of sound = 340 m/s.

4.1 Name the phenomenon observed.
4.2 Calculate the speed of the ambulance.
✅ Complete Solution
4.1 Name of Phenomenon
Doppler Effect
4.2 Calculate Speed of Ambulance

Given: fL = 90% of fS = 0.9fS, v = 340 m/s

Note: Source moving away from stationary listener

fL = v/(v + vS) × fS 0.9fS = 340/(340 + vS) × fS 0.9 = 340/(340 + vS) 0.9(340 + vS) = 340 306 + 0.9vS = 340 0.9vS = 34 vS = 34/0.9 vS = 37.78 m/s
vS = 37.78 m/s
Question 5 🚂 Train Whistle

📋 Scenario

The whistle of a train emits sound waves of frequency 2000 Hz. A stationary listener measures the frequency as 2080 Hz. Speed of sound = 340 m/s.

5.1 Name the phenomenon responsible for the observed change in frequency.
5.2 Is the train moving AWAY FROM or TOWARDS the stationary listener?
5.3 Calculate the speed of the train.
5.4 Will the frequency observed by a passenger sitting IN the train be GREATER THAN, EQUAL TO, or SMALLER THAN 2000 Hz? Explain.
✅ Complete Solution
5.1 Name of Phenomenon
Doppler Effect
5.2 Direction of Train

Since fL (2080 Hz) > fS (2000 Hz), the frequency has increased. This only happens when the source approaches.

TOWARDS the listener
5.3 Calculate Speed of Train
fL = v/(v - vS) × fS 2080 = 340/(340 - vS) × 2000 2080/2000 = 340/(340 - vS) 1.04 = 340/(340 - vS) 1.04(340 - vS) = 340 353.6 - 1.04vS = 340 1.04vS = 13.6 vS = 13.08 m/s
vS = 13.08 m/s
5.4 Frequency for Passenger in Train
🔑 Key Concept: A passenger in the train moves at the same velocity as the train. There is no relative motion between the passenger and the whistle!

Since there is no relative motion between the source (whistle) and the observer (passenger), the passenger hears the actual source frequency.

EQUAL TO 2000 Hz

Explanation: The passenger moves at the same velocity as the train, so there is no relative velocity between the passenger and the source of sound.

📋 Key Concepts Summary

🧠 Important Points to Remember

  • Doppler Effect: The apparent change in frequency due to relative motion between source and observer
  • Approaching = Higher frequency (waves compressed)
  • Receding = Lower frequency (waves stretched)
  • Distance doesn't matter! Only relative velocity affects observed frequency
  • No relative motion = No Doppler Effect (e.g., passenger on train)
⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid:
  • Forgetting to convert km/h to m/s
  • Using the wrong sign (+ or -) in the equation
  • Confusing source moving vs listener moving
  • Thinking distance affects observed frequency
🎓
Exam Strategy: Always start by identifying WHO is moving (source, listener, or both), WHICH direction (towards or away), and WHETHER any unit conversions are needed. Then select the appropriate equation.
