The Doppler Equation

fL = (v ± vL) / (v ± vS) × fS

Remember the sign convention:

+ vL when listener moves toward source

- vL when listener moves away from source

- vS when source moves toward listener

+ vS when source moves away from listener

Question 1: Police Car Siren

The siren of a police car produces a sound of frequency 420 Hz. A man sitting next to the road notices that the pitch of the sound changes as the car moves towards and then away from him.

Full Solution:

1.1 Doppler Effect

1.2 fL = v / (v - vS) × fS = 340 / (340 - 16) × 420 = 440.74 Hz

1.3.1 Smaller than (when source moves away, frequency decreases)

1.3.2 Increases (as the car slows down, the relative velocity decreases, so the frequency approaches the source frequency)

Question 2: Burglar Alarm

The siren of a burglar alarm system has a frequency of 960 Hz. A security officer in his car hears the siren at 1000 Hz as he approaches the house.

Full Solution:

2.1 Doppler Effect

2.2 fL = (v + vL) / v × fS

1000 = (340 + vL) / 340 × 960

vL = 14.17 m/s

2.3 Higher than (greater relative velocity means greater frequency shift)

Question 3: Dolphin Ultrasound

A dolphin emits ultrasound waves of frequency 250 kHz while swimming at 20 m/s toward a rock. Speed of sound in water is 1500 m/s.

Full Solution:

3.1 fL = v / (v - vS) × fS = 1500 / (1500 - 20) × 250000 = 253,380 Hz

3.2 Remains the same (Doppler effect depends on relative velocity, not distance)

Key Concepts to Remember

  • The Doppler effect depends only on relative velocity, not distance
  • When source and listener move toward each other: frequency increases
  • When source and listener move away from each other: frequency decreases
  • At the moment of passing, the frequency equals the source frequency
  • If both move with the same velocity, there is no Doppler effect
