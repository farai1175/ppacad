Work, Power and Energy

Conservation of Mechanical Energy

📚 Lesson 1
⚡ Energy Conservation
⏱️ ~60 min

Lesson Overview

In this lesson, we explore the Conservation of Mechanical Energy—one of the most fundamental principles in physics. You'll learn how potential energy transforms into kinetic energy while total mechanical energy remains constant in isolated systems.

Learning Objectives

  • 1Define mechanical energy as the sum of kinetic and potential energy
  • 2State the principle of conservation of mechanical energy
  • 3Apply conservation of energy to calculate velocities and heights
  • 4Solve problems involving energy transformations

Types of Mechanical Energy

🏃

Kinetic Energy (Eₖ)

Energy due to motion

Eₖ = ½mv²
📍

Potential Energy (Eₚ)

Energy due to position

Eₚ = mgh
⚡

Mechanical Energy (Eₘ)

Sum of Eₖ and Eₚ

Eₘ = Eₖ + Eₚ

Conservation Principle

📜 Law of Conservation of Mechanical Energy
"In an isolated system without friction, the total mechanical energy remains constant. Energy transforms between kinetic and potential forms, but the sum stays the same."
Eₚ₁ + Eₖ₁ = Eₚ₂ + Eₖ₂
Energy at position 1 equals energy at position 2
mgh₁ + ½mv₁² = mgh₂ + ½mv₂²
Expanded form

💡 Key Insights

  • Energy cannot be created or destroyed
  • As height decreases, velocity increases
  • At maximum height: Eₚ = max, Eₖ = 0
  • At ground level: Eₚ = 0, Eₖ = max

Worked Examples

📝 Example 1: Falling Rock

Problem: A 50 kg rock falls from 45.92 m. Find its speed at 25.51 m height.

1Apply conservation: mgh₁ = mgh₂ + ½mv²
2Solve: 9.8 × 45.92 = 9.8 × 25.51 + ½v²
v² = 400 → v = 20 m/s
📝 Example 2: Pendulum

Problem: A 0.8 kg pendulum swings to height 0.5 m. Find velocity at lowest point.

1Max Eₚ: mgh = 0.8 × 9.8 × 0.5 = 3.92 J
2At bottom (all Eₖ): ½mv² = 3.92 → v = 3.13 m/s

Lesson Summary

📌 Key Points

  • Mechanical energy = Kinetic + Potential energy
  • In isolated systems: Eₚ₁ + Eₖ₁ = Eₚ₂ + Eₖ₂
  • Energy transforms but total remains constant
  • With friction: energy is "lost" as heat
  • SI unit of energy is the Joule (J)
