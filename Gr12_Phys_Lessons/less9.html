Overview

+

Mechanical energy is the sum of kinetic energy and potential energy in a system. According to the principle of conservation of mechanical energy, in an isolated system (with no external forces like friction), the total mechanical energy remains constant.

This means that energy can transform between potential and kinetic forms, but the total amount of energy stays the same.

Main Body

+

Mechanical Energy Formula

The total mechanical energy (Em) of an object is the sum of its kinetic energy (Ek) and potential energy (Ep):

Em = Ek + Ep

Conservation of Mechanical Energy

In an isolated system (no external forces like friction), the total mechanical energy remains constant:

Em initial = Em final

Ek initial + Ep initial = Ek final + Ep final

Worked Example 1: Free Fall

A 2 kg object is dropped from a height of 20 m. Calculate its speed just before it hits the ground.

Solution:

At the top: Ek = 0 (object at rest), Ep = mgh = 2 × 9.8 × 20 = 392 J

At the bottom: Ep = 0 (h = 0), Ek = ½mv²

Using conservation of energy: Em top = Em bottom

392 = ½ × 2 × v²

v² = 392

v = √392 ≈ 19.8 m/s

Worked Example 2: Pendulum

A pendulum with a mass of 0.5 kg is raised to a height of 0.8 m and released. Calculate its speed at the lowest point of its swing.

Solution:

At the highest point: Ek = 0, Ep = mgh = 0.5 × 9.8 × 0.8 = 3.92 J

At the lowest point: Ep = 0, Ek = ½mv²

Using conservation of energy: Em highest = Em lowest

3.92 = ½ × 0.5 × v²

v² = 15.68

v = √15.68 ≈ 3.96 m/s
