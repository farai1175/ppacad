Work, Power and Energy

Understanding the fundamental concepts of work in physics

📚 Week 5
📝 Lesson 1
⏱️ ~60 minutes

Lesson 1: Definition of Work

In this lesson, we explore the scientific definition of work, distinguish between everyday and physics uses of the term, and learn when an applied force does or does not do work on an object.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • 1 Define work done on an object by a force in the scientific (physics) context
  • 2 Calculate net work done on an object using the formula: W = F Δx cos θ
  • 3 Give examples of when an applied force does and does not do work on an object
  • 4 Understand that positive net work increases the energy of a system and negative net work decreases it
  • 5 Recognize that work is a scalar quantity measured in joules (J)

Overview

In everyday language, we use the word "work" to describe any activity that requires effort. However, in physics, work has a very specific meaning. Work is done on an object when a force causes the object to move in the direction of that force.


Understanding work is crucial because it connects force and motion to energy. When work is done on an object, energy is transferred to or from that object. This concept forms the foundation for understanding power, kinetic energy, potential energy, and the conservation of energy.

Main Concepts & Principles

📖 Scientific Definition of Work

Work is done on an object when a force exerted on the object moves that object in the direction of the force. For work to be done, there must be both a force AND displacement in the direction of that force.

📐 Requirements for Work

  • A force must act on the object
  • The object must move (displacement)
  • Displacement must have a component in the direction of the force

⚡ Work is a Scalar Quantity

Unlike force (which is a vector), work is a scalar quantity. It has magnitude but no direction. Work is measured in joules (J), where 1 J = 1 N·m

🔄 Work and Energy Transfer

Positive work done on a system increases its energy.
Negative work done on a system decreases its energy (like friction).

W = F · Δx · cos θ

Where:

W = Work done (in Joules, J)

F = Applied force (in Newtons, N)

Δx = Displacement (in metres, m)

θ = Angle between force and displacement direction

🎯 Important Angle Cases

  • θ = 0°: Force and displacement in same direction → cos 0° = 1 → Maximum positive work
  • θ = 90°: Force perpendicular to displacement → cos 90° = 0 → No work done (W = 0 J)
  • θ = 180°: Force opposite to displacement → cos 180° = -1 → Maximum negative work (like friction)

When is Work Done?

Work IS Done ✓ Work is NOT Done ✗
Pushing a box across the floor (force and displacement aligned) Holding a heavy bag stationary (no displacement)
Lifting a book vertically (force upward, displacement upward) Carrying a bag horizontally (force vertical, displacement horizontal)
A car engine accelerating a car (force causes displacement) A ball moving at constant velocity with no forces acting
Friction slowing down a sliding object (negative work) Pushing against a wall that doesn't move

Worked Examples

📝 Example 1: Horizontal Force

Problem: A force of 20 N is applied horizontally to a box to slide it 5 m along the floor. Calculate the work done in sliding the box.

1 Identify known values:
F = 20 N, Δx = 5 m, θ = 0° (force and displacement in same direction)
2 Apply the formula:
W = F · Δx · cos θ
3 Calculate:
W = 20 × 5 × cos 0°
W = 20 × 5 × 1
W = 100 J
📝 Example 2: Force with Friction

Problem: A 30 N force is applied to a box. There is a frictional force of 5 N between the floor and the box. The box slides for 10 m along the floor.

Part 1.1: Calculate the work done by the applied force

W = F · Δx · cos 0°
W = 30 × 10 × 1
W = 300 J

Part 1.2: Calculate the work done against friction

W = F · Δx · cos 180° (friction acts opposite to motion)
W = 5 × 10 × (-1)
W = -50 J (negative because energy is removed from the system)
📝 Example 3: Applied Force with Friction

Problem: Tsepiso wants to move a concrete block of mass 4 kg a distance of 12 m to the right by applying a force of 16 N. There is a frictional force of 2 N acting against the block.

Calculate the work done by the 16 N force:

W = F · Δx · cos 0°
W = 16 × 12 × 1
W = 192 J

Calculate the work done by the frictional force:

W = F_friction · Δx · cos 180°
W = 2 × 12 × (-1)
W = -24 J

What happens to the work done against friction?

The work done against friction is converted into heat energy. This is why surfaces warm up when rubbed together.

Lesson Summary

📌 Key Points to Remember

  • Work is done when a force displaces an object in the direction of the force
  • The SI unit of work is the Joule (J), which is equivalent to N·m
  • Work is a scalar quantity with magnitude only (no direction)
  • 1 joule of work is done when a force of 1 N acts through a distance of 1 m
  • Under frictionless conditions, no work is done when an object moves at uniform velocity (no resultant force)
  • If a force is applied to an object with no movement, the work done = 0 J
  • When force is perpendicular to displacement, work done = 0 J
  • Friction always acts opposite to the direction of motion
