Work in Physics

Understanding the scientific definition of work and when work is done

By the end of this lesson, learners must be able to:

  • Define work done on an object by a force
  • Calculate net work done using W = FΔx cosθ
  • Identify when an applied force does and does not do work
  • Understand that work is a scalar quantity measured in joules
  • Explain how positive and negative work affect system energy

Lesson Overview

+

This lesson explores the scientific definition of work in physics, which differs from everyday usage. We'll learn when work is done, how to calculate it, and understand its relationship with energy.

Key Formula

W = F × Δx × cosθ

Where:

  • W = Work done (Joules, J)
  • F = Force applied (Newtons, N)
  • Δx = Displacement (meters, m)
  • θ = Angle between force and displacement

1 Joule = 1 Newton × 1 meter

Scientific Definition of Work

In physics, work is done when a force causes an object to move in the direction of the force.

Two conditions must be met for work to be done:

  1. A force must act on the object
  2. The object must move in the direction of the applied force

Key points about work in physics:

  • Work is a scalar quantity (magnitude only, no direction)
  • Unit of work is the Joule (J) = 1 Newton × 1 meter
  • Work can be positive or negative depending on the angle between force and displacement
  • No work is done if there's no movement or if movement is perpendicular to the force

Everyday Usage of "Work"

In everyday language, "work" refers to any activity involving effort, whether physical or mental.

Examples of everyday work that is NOT work in physics:

  • Holding a heavy object stationary (no displacement)
  • Pushing against a wall that doesn't move (no displacement)
  • Carrying a box while walking horizontally (force vertical, displacement horizontal)
  • Thinking or studying (no physical displacement)

These activities require energy and effort but do zero work in the physics sense because there's no displacement in the direction of the applied force.

Work Formula Components

W = F × Δx × cosθ

The angle θ between the force and displacement determines how much work is done:

Angle (θ) cosθ Work Done Explanation
0° 1 Maximum positive work Force and displacement in same direction
90° 0 Zero work Force perpendicular to displacement
180° -1 Maximum negative work Force opposite to displacement (e.g., friction)
0° < θ < 90° 0 < cosθ < 1 Positive work Force has component in direction of motion
90° < θ < 180° -1 < cosθ < 0 Negative work Force has component opposite to motion

Worked Examples

Problem 1: Box with Friction

A 30 N force is applied to a box. There is a frictional force of 5 N between the floor and the box. The box slides for 10 m along the floor.

a) Calculate the work done to move the box.

b) Calculate the work done against friction.

Interactive Calculator for Problem 1

Calculate work done by applied force

Work done by applied force:

W = F × Δx × cosθ

W = 30 N × 10 m × cos(0°) (since force and displacement are in same direction)

W = 30 × 10 × 1 = 300 J

The applied force does 300 J of work on the box.

Calculate work done against friction

Work done by friction:

W = F × Δx × cosθ

W = 5 N × 10 m × cos(180°) (since friction opposes motion)

W = 5 × 10 × (-1) = -50 J

Friction does -50 J of work on the box (or you can say 50 J of work is done against friction).

Problem 2: Horizontal Force

A force of 20 N is applied horizontally to a box to slide it 5 m along the floor. Calculate the work done in sliding the box.

Interactive Calculator for Problem 2

Calculate work done

Work done:

W = F × Δx × cosθ

W = 20 N × 5 m × cos(0°) (force and displacement are both horizontal)

W = 20 × 5 × 1 = 100 J

The force does 100 J of work on the box.

Problem 3: Box with Friction and Applied Force

Tsepiso wants to move a concrete block of mass 4 kg a distance of 12 m to the right by applying a force of 16 N to the block. There is a frictional force of 2 N which acts against the block due to the rough surface.

a) Calculate the work done by the 16 N force.

b) Calculate the work done by the frictional force.

c) What happens to the work done by the frictional force?

Interactive Calculator for Problem 3

Calculate work done by applied force

Work done by applied force:

W = F × Δx × cosθ

W = 16 N × 12 m × cos(0°) = 192 J

The applied force does 192 J of work on the block.

Calculate work done by friction

Work done by friction:

W = F × Δx × cosθ

W = 2 N × 12 m × cos(180°) = 2 × 12 × (-1) = -24 J

Friction does -24 J of work on the block.

What happens to the work done by friction?

The work done by friction is converted into heat energy.

When friction does negative work, it removes mechanical energy from the system and converts it to thermal energy (heat). This is why surfaces get warm when rubbed together.
