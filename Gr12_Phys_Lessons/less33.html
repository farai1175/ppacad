📚 Overview

When light encounters a material, three things can happen: the light can pass through (transmission), be absorbed by the material (absorption), or bounce back (reflection). The specific behavior depends on the properties of the material's electrons and how they interact with the incoming light's frequency.

💡
Key Concept: Light is energy. When it strikes a material, this energy is transferred to the electrons in the atoms. What happens next depends on whether the light's frequency matches the natural frequency of those electrons.

🔄 Pre-Knowledge Review

Before we explore how light interacts with materials, let's review some essential concepts:

Electronic Properties of Matter

  • Electrons: The electrical conductivity of metals depends on electrons.
  • Band Theory: In metals, the conduction and valence bands are close together (or overlap), making it easy for excited electrons to move between bands.
  • Why metals conduct: The small gap between energy bands allows electrons to move freely when energy is applied.

📖 Main Concepts & Principles

1. How Light Interacts with Materials

When light strikes a material, it interacts with the electrons in the atoms of that material. Here's what happens:

  • Light carries energy that is transferred to electrons in atoms
  • The amount of energy transferred depends on the material's properties
  • In metals, energy levels are close together, so almost all frequencies of light can be absorbed
  • When electrons absorb energy, they become excited and jump to higher energy levels

2. Three Types of Light Interaction

🔵 Transmission

Light passes through a substance virtually unchanged. This occurs when the frequency of light is different from the natural frequency of the electrons in the material.

Example: Light passing through glass

🔴 Absorption

Light is neither transmitted nor reflected, but absorbed by the material. Electrons use the energy and vibrate more energetically. The absorbed energy is converted to internal energy (heat).

Example: Black objects absorbing all light

🟠 Reflection

Light beams hit the surface and "bounce off" rather than penetrating the material. This happens when electrons absorb light and immediately re-emit it.

Example: Mirrors, polished metals

3. Frequency Matching

The relationship between the incident light frequency and the natural frequency of electrons determines what happens:

Condition Result What Happens
flight = fnatural Reflection Light is absorbed and immediately re-emitted
flight > fnatural Transmission Light passes through the material
flight < fnatural Absorption Light energy is converted to heat

4. Why Objects Have Colour

Colour and Light

  • The colour of an object is determined by the frequency of light it transmits or reflects.
  • If no light is reflected or transmitted, the object appears black.
  • If all frequencies are reflected, the object appears white or silver.
  • If only certain frequencies are reflected, the object appears that colour.

5. Metals: Absorption and Re-emission

Metals have special properties regarding light interaction:

  • Because energy levels in metals are close together, almost all frequencies can be absorbed
  • The absorbed light is then quickly re-emitted (reflected)
  • This is why metals appear shiny - they reflect most of the light that hits them
  • Different metals reflect different combinations of frequencies, giving them distinct colours (copper = reddish-orange, gold = yellow, silver = silvery-white)
