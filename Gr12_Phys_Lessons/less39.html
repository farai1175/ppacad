Electric Circuits

📘 Sub-topic: Short Circuits & Open Circuits 📝 Lesson 5 ⏱️ 60 minutes 🔌 Practical Investigation

🎯 Learning Objectives

▼
  • ✓ Set up a series-parallel network with an ammeter in each branch and external circuit.
  • ✓ Connect voltmeters across each resistor, branch, and battery.
  • ✓ Position switches in each branch and external circuit.
  • ✓ Use this circuit to investigate short circuits and open circuits.
  • ✓ Explain the effects of opening switches and creating short circuits on current and voltage readings.

📋 Overview & Pre-Knowledge

▼

📌 Pre-Knowledge Required

  • Understanding of series and parallel circuits
  • Current division in parallel branches
  • Voltage distribution in series circuits
  • Ohm's Law: V = IR

🔑 Key Concepts

Open Circuit: A break in the circuit path that stops current flow in that section.


Short Circuit: A path of very low resistance that bypasses components, causing current to take the easier path.


Kirchhoff's Current Law: The sum of currents entering a junction equals the sum leaving: I₁ = I₂ + I₃

⚠️
Safety Warning: Short circuits can cause excessive current flow, leading to overheating, damaged components, or fire hazards in real circuits. Always use appropriate safety measures.

🔬 Practical Investigation

▼

⚡ Investigating Short Circuits and Open Circuits

Aim: To investigate the effects of short circuits and open circuits on current and voltage in a series-parallel network.

Apparatus Required:
🔋 Battery 🔌 Connecting wires ⚡ Three resistors (different values) 📊 Voltmeters (×5) 📏 Ammeters (×3) 🔘 Switches (×3) 🧵 Low resistance wire
⚠️
Important: Be sure to open all switches before connecting the circuit. Have your circuit checked before closing any switches.

📐 Circuit Diagram

The circuit consists of:

  • R₁ in series with the main circuit (controlled by S₁)
  • R₂ and R₃ in parallel (controlled by S₂ and S₃)
  • A₁ measures total current, A₂ and A₃ measure branch currents
  • V₁ across R₁, V₂ across R₂, V₃ across R₃
  • V₄ across the parallel combination, V₅ across the battery
  • Points a, b, and c for short circuit connections

📝 Method & Questions

1. Close all switches. Record the reading on each ammeter A₁, A₂, and A₃.
2. What is the relationship between ammeter readings A₁, A₂, and A₃?
3. Record the reading on each voltmeter V₁, V₂, V₃, V₄, and V₅.
4. What is the relationship between voltmeters V₁, V₄, and V₅?
5. What is the relationship between voltmeters V₂, V₃, and V₄?
6. Open switch S₁. Record all your observations.
7. Close all switches and open switch S₂. Record all your observations.
8. Close all switches and open switch S₃. Record all your observations.
9. Close all switches and join points a and b with the low resistance wire. Record all your observations.
10. Explain your observations in question 9.
11. Now join points b and c with the low resistance wire. Record all your observations.
12. Explain your observations in question 11.
13. Now join points a and c with the low resistance wire. Record all your observations.
14. Explain your observations in question 13.

✅ Expected Results & Answers

1. I₁, I₂, and I₃ readings depend on resistor values
2. I₁ = I₂ + I₃ (Kirchhoff's Current Law)
3. V₁, V₂=V₃=V₄, V₅ = V₁ + V₄
4. V₅ = V₁ + V₄ (voltages in series add up)
5. V₂ = V₃ = V₄ (same voltage across parallel components)
6. All ammeters read zero. Only V₅ gives a reading (= EMF).
7. A₁ decreases, V₁ decreases, V₃, V₄, V₅ increase, V₂ = 0V, A₂ = 0A
8. A₁ decreases, V₁ decreases, V₂, V₄, V₅ increase, V₃ = 0V, A₃ = 0A
9. A₁ increases significantly. V₁ becomes zero (or nearly zero).
10. When a-b is joined by low resistance wire, effective resistance between a and b is almost zero. Current takes path of least resistance. A₁ increases because total circuit resistance has decreased.
11. Readings on A₂ and A₃ become zero. V₂, V₃, and V₄ all become zero.
12. When b-c is joined by low resistance wire, effective resistance between b and c is almost zero. Charge follows the path of least resistance, bypassing R₂ and R₃.
13. A₁ increases. All other ammeters read zero. All voltmeters read zero except V₅. V₅ decreases.
14. a-c is effectively a path of zero resistance. Resistance in circuit has decreased dramatically, increasing A₁. V₅ decreases because the potential difference across internal resistance has increased.
