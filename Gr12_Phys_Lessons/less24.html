A transformer that reduces voltage (fewer turns on secondary coil).

💡 Main Concepts: Alternating Current

⚡ Alternating Current (AC)
Current that keeps changing direction backwards and forwards in a repetitive manner.
🏭

South African Power Supply

The electricity supplied by Eskom is alternating current. It changes direction 50 times per second, meaning it has a frequency of 50 Hz.

It is important that this frequency is kept constant to avoid damage to electrical equipment.

🌊 Visualizing AC vs DC

〰️

Alternating Current (AC)

  • Changes direction periodically
  • Follows a sine wave pattern
  • Has frequency (e.g., 50 Hz)
  • Voltage varies continuously
  • Can be transformed (stepped up/down)
  • Used for power transmission
➡️

Direct Current (DC)

  • Flows in one direction only
  • Constant or pulsating level
  • No frequency (0 Hz)
  • Voltage remains constant
  • Cannot be easily transformed
  • Used in batteries, electronics
💡 Key Point: Alternating current is easier to generate than direct current, which is one reason why AC became the standard for power distribution worldwide.

✨ Advantages of Alternating Current

🔑 The Power Equation
P = V × I

Power = Voltage × Current

🎯 The Main Advantage: Transformers!

AC voltage can be changed using transformers. This is crucial for efficient power transmission because:

  1. Step-up at power stations: Voltage is increased to very high levels (e.g., 400,000 V)
  2. High voltage = Low current: Since P = VI, if V goes up, I can go down for the same power
  3. Low current = Less heat loss: Energy loss due to heating in cables is reduced
  4. Step-down for use: Voltage is reduced to safe levels (220V) for homes and businesses

⚠️ Why Not Just Use High Current?

From P = VI, we could deliver power using either high voltage or high current. However:

  • Power cables have resistance
  • When current flows through resistance, heat is produced (P = I²R)
  • High current through hundreds of kilometers of cable = HUGE energy loss
  • Therefore: Keep current LOW by making voltage HIGH
⚡ Heat Loss in Cables
Ploss = I²R

Power loss increases with the square of current!

Halving the current reduces heat loss to 1/4!

🔄 Why Transformers Only Work with AC

Transformers use the principle of mutual induction:

  • A changing current in the primary coil creates a changing magnetic field
  • This changing field induces a voltage in the secondary coil
  • DC creates a constant magnetic field - no induction!
  • AC constantly changes - continuous induction!

Therefore: Transformers can only work with AC, which is why AC is used for power transmission.

📊 Characteristics of Alternating Current

⚡ Key Characteristics of AC

  • Self-Inductance: When AC changes direction, the magnetic field associated with it changes in a way that opposes the change. This is called self-inductance.
  • Energy Loss: Because of self-inductance, some electrical energy is wasted, and appliances receive a lower maximum voltage than the peak value supplied.
  • RMS Value: The lower effective voltage is called the Root-Mean-Square (RMS) value. This is the equivalent DC voltage that would deliver the same power.
  • Frequency: AC has a specific frequency (50 Hz in South Africa, 60 Hz in USA).
  • Waveform: AC typically follows a sinusoidal (sine wave) pattern.
💡 Did You Know? When we say household voltage is 220V in South Africa, we're talking about the RMS voltage, not the peak voltage. The actual peak voltage is about 311V!
