Main Concepts & Principles

What is Alternating Current?

📝 Definition

An alternating current (AC) is current that keeps changing direction backwards and forwards in a repetitive manner.

We have seen that the electricity produced by AC generators alternates, which is why it's called alternating current. The current flows first in one direction, then reverses and flows in the opposite direction, repeating this cycle continuously.

Advantages of Alternating Current

The main advantage of alternating current is that the voltage can be easily changed using transformers. This makes it ideal for:

  • Long-distance power transmission (high voltage reduces energy loss)
  • Safe household distribution (stepped down to safer voltages)
  • Various industrial applications

Self-Inductance in AC

When alternating current flows through wires, it causes self-inductance. When the current changes direction, the magnetic field associated with it changes in such a way as to oppose the change. This is described by Lenz's Law.

Understanding RMS Values

📝 Definition

Root Mean Square (RMS) is the effective value of alternating current that corresponds with a direct current that produces the same heating effect.

Appliances receive a lower maximum voltage than the peak value supplied. We call this lower effective voltage the root-mean-square (RMS) value.

⚡ Peak vs RMS Voltage

Vrms = Vmax / √2

The RMS voltage is approximately 70.7% of the peak voltage.

🔌 Peak vs RMS Current

Irms = Imax / √2

The RMS current is approximately 70.7% of the peak current.

Worked Examples

1

Identifying Generator Type from Graph

A graph shows induced emf varying sinusoidally between positive and negative values over time (0 to 0.2 s). The emf crosses zero at 0.05 s and 0.15 s.

a) Is this an AC or DC generator? Explain.

b) Is the coil horizontal or vertical at t = 0.05 s?

c) How would you convert this to the other type of generator?

Solution:

a) This is a DC generator because the current flows in one direction only. Wait - looking at the description more carefully, if the EMF varies between positive and negative values, this is actually an AC generator. The EMF alternates between positive and negative, indicating the current changes direction.

b) The coil is vertical at t = 0.05 s. When the EMF is zero (crossing the time axis), the coil is cutting minimum magnetic field lines, which occurs when the coil is vertical (parallel to the magnetic field).

c) To convert an AC generator to a DC generator, replace the slip rings with a split ring commutator. The split ring commutator reverses the connection to the external circuit every half rotation, ensuring current flows in one direction only.

2

Energy Conversion in Generators

a) What energy conversions take place in an AC generator?

b) Draw a sketch graph of emf vs time for one full rotation of the coil.

Solution:

a) In an AC generator, mechanical energy is converted to electrical energy. The mechanical energy (from rotation of the coil or magnet) is transformed into electrical energy through electromagnetic induction.

b) The graph should show:

  • A sinusoidal wave starting at EMF = 0 when θ = 0°
  • Maximum positive EMF at θ = 90° (¼ rotation)
  • EMF = 0 again at θ = 180° (½ rotation)
  • Maximum negative EMF at θ = 270° (¾ rotation)
  • Returns to EMF = 0 at θ = 360° (full rotation)
