Overview

+

Power is defined as the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred. It measures how quickly work is performed or energy is expended.

P = W / Δt = ΔE / Δt

Where:

P = Power (W)

W = Work done (J)

ΔE = Energy transferred (J)

Δt = Time interval (s)

For constant velocity motion, power can also be calculated as:

P = F·v

Where:

F = Force applied (N)

v = Velocity (m/s)

Efficiency measures how effectively a system converts input energy into useful work:

Efficiency = (Useful Output Energy / Input Energy) × 100%

Main Body

+

Understanding Power

Power measures how quickly work is done or energy is transferred. The SI unit of power is the watt (W), which equals one joule per second (J/s).

1 W = 1 J/s

Power is a scalar quantity - it has magnitude but no direction

Key Concepts:

  • Power is the rate of doing work or transferring energy
  • Greater power means work is done more quickly
  • For constant velocity: P = F·v (where F and v are in the same direction)
  • Efficiency tells us how much input energy does useful work
Worked Example 1: Car Moving at Constant Speed

A car moves at a constant speed of 20 m/s. The frictional force equals the driving force. Determine the frictional force if the car has an 88 kW motor.

Solution:

P = F·v

88,000 W = F × 20 m/s

F = 88,000 / 20 = 4,400 N

The frictional force is 4,400 N.

Worked Example 2: Electric Motor Efficiency

A 100 W electric motor takes 1 minute to lift a 50 kg mass vertically through 6 m at constant speed. Calculate the efficiency of the motor.

Solution:

Input energy: Win = P·Δt = 100 W × 60 s = 6,000 J

Useful output: Wout = m·g·h = 50 kg × 9.8 m/s² × 6 m = 2,940 J

Efficiency = (Wout / Win) × 100% = (2,940 / 6,000) × 100% = 49%

The motor efficiency is 49%.

Worked Example 3: Mine Elevator

In gold mines, an elevator lowers 14 miners (average mass 70 kg each) down a 300 m shaft in 100 seconds. If the lift mass is 1,500 kg, calculate the power the elevator engine must generate.

Solution:

Total mass: m = (14 × 70 kg) + 1,500 kg = 2,480 kg

Work done: W = m·g·h = 2,480 kg × 9.8 m/s² × 300 m = 7,291,200 J

Power: P = W / Δt = 7,291,200 J / 100 s = 72,912 W = 72.9 kW

The elevator engine must generate 72.9 kW of power.
