Grade 12 Physical Sciences Week 4 Lesson 4 Conservation Law

TOPIC: Momentum and Impulse

Lesson 4: Conservation of Momentum

Master one of physics' most powerful principles: the conservation of linear momentum in isolated systems.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • State and explain the principle of conservation of momentum
  • Identify isolated systems where momentum is conserved
  • Apply conservation of momentum to collisions of two objects moving along a straight line
  • Solve problems involving objects that separate after collision
  • Handle collisions involving objects moving in opposite directions

📖 Overview

The principle of conservation of momentum is one of the most fundamental laws in physics. It tells us that in any collision or interaction within an isolated system, the total momentum before equals the total momentum after – regardless of how complex the interaction is!

Pre-knowledge Required:
  • Momentum: p = mv (the product of mass and velocity)
  • Vectors: Momentum has direction – opposite directions have opposite signs
  • Impulse: Change in momentum relates to force and time

Baseline Question

Before continuing, can you answer: Define momentum.
Answer: Momentum is the product of mass and velocity (p = mv). It is a vector quantity with units kg·m·s⁻¹.

⚖️ The Principle of Conservation of Momentum

📜 The Law

The total linear momentum in an isolated system remains constant in both magnitude and direction.

What Does This Mean?

In simpler terms:

  • In an isolated system, the total momentum before a collision equals the total momentum after the collision
  • If one object loses momentum, another object gains that exact amount of momentum
  • The vector sum of all momenta stays the same
Isolated System: A system where no external forces act on the objects. Internal forces (like collision forces between objects) are allowed – they are equal and opposite (Newton's 3rd Law) and cancel out.
Σpbefore = Σpafter
Total momentum before collision = Total momentum after collision

For two objects colliding, this becomes:

m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = m₁v₁f + m₂v₂f
Where: i = initial (before), f = final (after)

Types of Collisions

💥➡️🔵 🟠

Objects Separate After Collision

Both objects continue moving (possibly in different directions) after the collision. They have separate final velocities.

m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = m₁v₁f + m₂v₂f
💥➡️🟣

Objects Stick Together

Objects combine into one mass after collision. They move together with the same final velocity.

m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = (m₁ + m₂)vf
🔵➡️ ⬅️🟠

Objects Moving in Opposite Directions

Head-on collisions where objects approach from opposite directions. Use opposite signs for velocities!

Choose + direction, use - for opposite
Critical Reminder - Signs Matter!
  • Always choose a positive direction first (e.g., "Take east as positive")
  • Velocities in the positive direction are positive (+)
  • Velocities in the opposite direction are negative (-)
  • A negative answer means the object moves in the negative direction

📚 Worked Examples

1 Worked Example: Ball Collision (Objects Separate)
Problem: A ball with mass of 1 kg is moving eastward with a speed of 2 m·s⁻¹. It collides head-on with a second ball. The second ball has a mass of 1.5 kg and is moving with a speed of 1.5 m·s⁻¹ in the opposite direction. After the collision, the first ball bounces back with a velocity of 2.05 m·s⁻¹. Calculate the velocity of the second ball after the collision.
Before:
1 kg
→ 2 m/s
1.5 kg
← 1.5 m/s
📍 Take EAST as POSITIVE

Given:
m₁ = 1 kg, v₁ᵢ = +2 m·s⁻¹ (east)
m₂ = 1.5 kg, v₂ᵢ = -1.5 m·s⁻¹ (west, so negative)
v₁f = -2.05 m·s⁻¹ (bounces back west)
v₂f = ?

Apply conservation of momentum:
Σpbefore = Σpafter
m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = m₁v₁f + m₂v₂f
(1)(2) + (1.5)(-1.5) = (1)(-2.05) + (1.5)(v₂f)
2 - 2.25 = -2.05 + 1.5v₂f
-0.25 = -2.05 + 1.5v₂f
1.5v₂f = -0.25 + 2.05
1.5v₂f = 1.8
v₂f = 1.2 m·s⁻¹
v₂f = 1.2 m·s⁻¹ east
Check: Positive answer means the second ball moves east after collision – it reversed direction!
2 Worked Example: Masses Collision - Opposite Directions
Problem: A 9 kg mass travelling eastward at 4 m·s⁻¹ collides with a 3 kg mass travelling westward at 8 m·s⁻¹. After collision, the 9 kg mass is moving westward at 1 m·s⁻¹. Calculate the velocity of the 3 kg mass after collision.
Before:
9 kg
→ 4 m/s
3 kg
← 8 m/s
📍 Take EAST as POSITIVE

Given:
m₁ = 9 kg, v₁ᵢ = +4 m·s⁻¹ (east)
m₂ = 3 kg, v₂ᵢ = -8 m·s⁻¹ (west)
v₁f = -1 m·s⁻¹ (now moving west)
v₂f = ?

Apply conservation of momentum:
m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = m₁v₁f + m₂v₂f
(9)(4) + (3)(-8) = (9)(-1) + (3)(v₂f)
36 - 24 = -9 + 3v₂f
12 = -9 + 3v₂f
3v₂f = 12 + 9
3v₂f = 21
v₂f = 7 m·s⁻¹
v₂f = 7 m·s⁻¹ east
Interpretation: The 3 kg mass reversed direction – it was moving west but now moves east at 7 m·s⁻¹!
3 Worked Example: Railway Trucks (Same Direction)
Problem: A railway truck with a mass of 5000 kg and a velocity of 6 m·s⁻¹ collides with the second truck. The second truck has a mass of 4000 kg, and before the collision, it had a velocity of 3 m·s⁻¹ in the same direction as the first truck. The two trucks separate after the collision. The new velocity of the second truck is 5 m·s⁻¹ in the same direction as before. Calculate the new velocity of the first truck.
Before:
5000 kg
→ 6 m/s
4000 kg
→ 3 m/s
📍 Take direction of initial velocity as POSITIVE

Given:
m₁ = 5000 kg, v₁ᵢ = +6 m·s⁻¹
m₂ = 4000 kg, v₂ᵢ = +3 m·s⁻¹ (same direction)
v₂f = +5 m·s⁻¹ (same direction)
v₁f = ?

Apply conservation of momentum:
m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = m₁v₁f + m₂v₂f
(5000)(6) + (4000)(3) = (5000)(v₁f) + (4000)(5)
30000 + 12000 = 5000v₁f + 20000
42000 = 5000v₁f + 20000
5000v₁f = 42000 - 20000
5000v₁f = 22000
v₁f = 4.4 m·s⁻¹
v₁f = 4.4 m·s⁻¹ in the same direction as initial velocity
Note: The first truck slowed down (from 6 to 4.4 m·s⁻¹) while the second truck sped up (from 3 to 5 m·s⁻¹). Momentum was transferred!
4 Worked Example: Toy Cars (One Becomes Stationary)
Problem: Two toy cars are approaching each other. Car 1, with mass 0.8 kg, has a velocity of 5 m·s⁻¹ to the right. Car 2 has a mass of 1 kg and a velocity of 5 m·s⁻¹ to the left. After the collision, car 2 is stationary. Calculate car 1's velocity after the collision.
Before:
0.8 kg
→ 5 m/s
1 kg
← 5 m/s
📍 Take RIGHT as POSITIVE

Given:
m₁ = 0.8 kg, v₁ᵢ = +5 m·s⁻¹ (right)
m₂ = 1 kg, v₂ᵢ = -5 m·s⁻¹ (left)
v₂f = 0 m·s⁻¹ (stationary)
v₁f = ?

Apply conservation of momentum:
m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = m₁v₁f + m₂v₂f
(0.8)(5) + (1)(-5) = (0.8)(v₁f) + (1)(0)
4 - 5 = 0.8v₁f + 0
-1 = 0.8v₁f
v₁f = -1.25 m·s⁻¹
v₁f = 1.25 m·s⁻¹ to the left
Interpretation: The negative sign tells us car 1 bounced back and is now moving to the left at 1.25 m·s⁻¹!
