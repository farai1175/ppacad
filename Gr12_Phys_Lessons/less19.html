Redshifts and the Expanding Universe

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners must be able to:

  • State that light from many stars is shifted towards the red, or longer wavelength/lower frequency, end of the spectrum
  • Apply the Doppler effect to these "redshifts" to conclude that most stars are moving away from Earth
  • Understand that the universe is expanding based on redshift observations

Lesson Overview

This lesson explores how the Doppler Effect applies to light from distant stars and galaxies, leading to the discovery of redshifts. We'll examine how these observations provide evidence for an expanding universe and support the Big Bang theory.

Key concepts include: spectrum of light, frequency and wavelength relationships, Doppler Effect for light, redshifts and blueshifts, and the expanding universe model.

Main Body

The Doppler Effect and Light

The Doppler Effect, which we typically associate with sound, also applies to light waves. When a light source moves relative to an observer, the observed frequency (and wavelength) of the light changes.

Doppler Effect for Light

When a light source moves away from an observer, the waves are stretched, resulting in longer wavelengths (redshift). When it moves toward the observer, waves are compressed, resulting in shorter wavelengths (blueshift).

Redshifts and the Expanding Universe

Astronomers have observed that light from distant galaxies is consistently shifted toward the red end of the spectrum. This redshift indicates that these galaxies are moving away from us.

Spectrum Comparison

Absorption lines in stellar spectra are shifted toward longer wavelengths (red end) compared to laboratory references, indicating motion away from Earth.

Hubble's Law and the Big Bang

Edwin Hubble discovered that the redshift of galaxies is proportional to their distance from us. The further away a galaxy is, the greater its redshift and the faster it appears to be moving away.

This relationship, known as Hubble's Law, provides strong evidence for an expanding universe. If we extrapolate this expansion backward in time, we arrive at the concept of the Big Bang - a moment when the entire universe was concentrated in an extremely hot, dense state.

Blueshifts

While most galaxies show redshifts, a small number exhibit blueshifts, indicating they are moving toward us. These are typically nearby galaxies within our Local Group, where gravitational interactions can overcome the general expansion of the universe.
