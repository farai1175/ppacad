🌌 Redshift & The Expanding Universe

Sub-topic: Doppler Effect Applied to Light from Stars

Lesson 4: Understanding Redshift and Evidence for the Big Bang

🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:

  • State that light from many stars is shifted towards the red (longer wavelength/lower frequency) end of the spectrum
  • Apply the Doppler effect to explain "redshifts" in starlight
  • Conclude that most stars and galaxies are moving away from Earth
  • Explain how redshift provides evidence that the universe is expanding
  • Describe the Big Bang theory and its connection to redshift observations

🌟 The Doppler Effect and Light

Just as the Doppler Effect changes the perceived frequency of sound when there is relative motion between source and observer, it also affects light waves from distant stars and galaxies!

How It Works with Light

  • The velocities of distant galaxies can be determined using the Doppler Effect
  • When we observe light from distant galaxies, we can measure shifts in the frequency of that light
  • By comparing the absorption spectrum of a star to our Sun's spectrum, we can observe frequency shifts
  • These shifts tell us whether the star is moving towards us or away from us

🔴 Redshift

What happens: Light shifts towards the red end of the spectrum (lower frequency, longer wavelength)

What it means: The galaxy is moving AWAY from us

Key insight: The greater the redshift, the faster the galaxy is moving away!

→

🔵 Blueshift

What happens: Light shifts towards the blue end of the spectrum (higher frequency, shorter wavelength)

What it means: The galaxy is moving TOWARDS us

Key insight: Very few galaxies show blueshift - most are redshifted!

←
🔑 Critical Discovery: Astronomers in the late 1800s and early 1900s noticed that the absorption spectra from most distant galaxies are redshifted. This means almost all galaxies are moving away from us!

🌌 The Expanding Universe

What Redshift Tells Us

  • Most galaxies show redshift → Most galaxies are moving away from Earth
  • Greater redshift = Greater velocity → More distant galaxies move faster
  • The further the galaxy, the faster it recedes → This is Hubble's Law
  • Conclusion: The universe itself is expanding!

ℹ️ Hubble's Discovery: In 1929, Edwin Hubble observed that galaxies are moving away from us, and the farther away they are, the faster they're moving. This led to the revolutionary conclusion that the universe is expanding!

💥 The Big Bang Theory

Big Bang Theory: The scientific theory that the universe began as a highly concentrated point and has been expanding outward ever since, approximately 13.8 billion years ago.

How Redshift Supports the Big Bang

If we observe that:

  1. Almost all galaxies are moving away from us (redshifted)
  2. More distant galaxies move faster
  3. The universe is expanding in all directions

Then if we "rewind" time:

  1. All matter was once concentrated in one point
  2. An explosion (the Big Bang) caused this expansion
  3. The universe has been expanding ever since!

🕐 Timeline of the Universe

💥 The Big Bang

13.8 billion years ago: All matter and energy was concentrated at a single point. The universe began to expand.

💥

⭐ First Stars Form

About 400 million years later: First stars and galaxies begin to form from hydrogen and helium.

⭐

🌌 Galaxies Develop

Billions of years: Galaxies continue to form, merge, and spread apart as the universe expands.

🌌

🌍 Today

Present: We observe redshift from distant galaxies, providing evidence that expansion continues.

🌍

❓ The Future

Possibilities: Continued expansion, or possibly a "Big Crunch" where gravity pulls everything back together.

❓
⚠️ The Big Crunch: One possible future of the universe is called the "Big Crunch." If gravity eventually overcomes the expansion, the universe could collapse back on itself. However, current evidence suggests the expansion is actually accelerating!

🔍 Additional Evidence for the Big Bang

Other Evidence Supporting the Big Bang Theory:

  1. Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation (CMB): The "afterglow" of the Big Bang - faint radiation detected throughout the universe
  2. Abundance of Light Elements: The proportions of hydrogen, helium, and lithium match Big Bang predictions
  3. Galaxy Evolution: Distant (older) galaxies look different from nearby (younger) ones
  4. The fact that the universe is expanding - if we reverse time, everything converges to a single point
🔭
Fun Fact: When we look at distant galaxies, we're actually looking back in time! Light from a galaxy 1 billion light-years away took 1 billion years to reach us, so we see it as it was 1 billion years ago.

📋 Key Concepts Summary

Redshift: The shift of light towards longer wavelengths (lower frequencies, towards the red end of the spectrum) indicating that the source is moving AWAY from the observer.

Blueshift: The shift of light towards shorter wavelengths (higher frequencies, towards the blue end of the spectrum) indicating that the source is moving TOWARDS the observer.

🔑 Key Points to Remember:

  • Light from most galaxies is redshifted → they are moving away from us
  • Greater redshift = greater recession velocity
  • More distant galaxies have greater redshift → they move faster
  • This evidence leads to the conclusion that the universe is expanding
  • The Big Bang theory explains that the universe began as a concentrated point and has been expanding since
  • A small number of nearby galaxies show blueshift (moving towards us)
