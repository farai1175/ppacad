Dual Nature of Electromagnetic Radiation

🔑 Key Concept

Electromagnetic radiation exhibits both wave-like and particle-like properties. This is known as wave-particle duality.

How is EM Radiation Formed?

🌀 Changing Fields

Changing electric and magnetic fields create a disturbance which is propagated as an electromagnetic wave.

⚡ Accelerated Charges

Whenever charges are accelerated, a changing magnetic field is formed.

🔄 Field Interaction

The changing magnetic field causes a changing electric field, and vice versa.

📐 Right Angles

The magnetic and electric fields are at right angles to each other and to the direction of propagation.

Electromagnetic Wave Propagation

Electric Field ↔ Magnetic Field ↔ Direction of Travel

⚠️ Important Note

Electromagnetic waves are transverse waves and do NOT need a medium for propagation. They can travel through vacuum!

🔄

Wave vs Particle Models

Both the wave and particle models are necessary to fully explain the behavior of electromagnetic radiation:

🌊 Wave Model

EM radiation acts as a wave when it undergoes:

  • Reflection
  • Refraction
  • Diffraction
  • Interference

🔬 Particle Model

EM radiation acts like particles (photons) when it displays:

  • Photoelectric effect
  • Emission and absorption spectra
  • Quantized energy transfer

🤔 Complementary Models

Neither the wave model nor the particle model can exclusively explain all properties of light. The two models complement each other and relate through the Planck-Einstein relation.

📐

Energy of Photons

📝 Planck-Einstein Relation

The wave and particle models relate to each other via the relationship between the wavelength/frequency of light and the energy of photons.

📊 Essential Photon Energy Formulas

E = hf

Energy of a photon = Planck's constant × frequency

E = hc/λ

Alternative form using wavelength

c = fλ

Relationship between frequency and wavelength

📻 Radio Waves

Longest wavelength, lowest frequency. Used in communication, broadcasting, and radar.

🔍 Microwaves

Used in microwave ovens, satellite communications, and radar systems.

🔥 Infrared

Felt as heat. Used in remote controls, thermal imaging, and night vision.

👁️ Visible Light

The only part of the spectrum visible to human eyes. ROYGBIV colors.

☀️ Ultraviolet

Causes sunburn and stimulates vitamin D production. Used in sterilization.

🦴 X-rays

Penetrate soft tissues but are absorbed by bones. Used in medical imaging.

☢️ Gamma Rays

Shortest wavelength, highest frequency. Used in cancer treatment and sterilization.

Worked Examples

1

Calculating Photon Energy from Frequency

A photon of light has a frequency of 325 Hz. Find its energy.

Solution:
Step 1: Write down the formula

E = hf

Step 2: Substitute values

E = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s) × (325 Hz)

Step 3: Calculate

E = 2.16 × 10⁻³¹ J

The energy of the photon is 2.16 × 10⁻³¹ J.

2

Calculating Photon Energy from Wavelength

What is the energy of a photon with:

a) wavelength of 827 nm

b) wavelength of 2 nm

Solution for part (a):
Step 1: Convert nm to m

827 nm = 827 × 10⁻⁹ m = 8.27 × 10⁻⁷ m

Step 2: Use formula E = hc/λ

E = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3 × 10⁸) / (8.27 × 10⁻⁷)

Step 3: Calculate

E = 2.41 × 10⁻¹⁹ J

Solution for part (b):
Step 1: Convert nm to m

2 nm = 2 × 10⁻⁹ m

Step 2: Use formula E = hc/λ

E = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3 × 10⁸) / (2 × 10⁻⁹)

Step 3: Calculate

E = 9.95 × 10⁻¹⁷ J

Notice how shorter wavelengths correspond to higher energy photons.

3

Finding Wavelength from Photon Energy

A photon has energy of 3 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. Find its wavelength.

Solution:
Step 1: Find frequency from E = hf

3 × 10⁻¹⁹ = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴) × f

f = (3 × 10⁻¹⁹) / (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴)

f = 4.53 × 10¹⁴ Hz

Step 2: Use c = fλ to find wavelength

3 × 10⁸ = (4.53 × 10¹⁴) × λ

λ = (3 × 10⁸) / (4.53 × 10¹⁴)

λ = 6.62 × 10⁻⁷ m

Step 3: Convert to nm

λ = 6.62 × 10⁻⁷ m = 662 nm

This wavelength corresponds to red light in the visible spectrum.
