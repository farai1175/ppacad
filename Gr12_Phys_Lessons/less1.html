TOPIC: Momentum and Impulse

Lesson 1: The Meaning of Momentum

Master the fundamental concept of momentum and learn to calculate momentum and change in momentum.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Understand and explain the meaning of momentum
  • Apply the momentum formula to calculate the momentum of objects
  • Calculate the change in momentum when velocity changes
  • Recognize momentum as a vector quantity and handle direction in calculations

📖 Overview

Momentum is one of the most fundamental concepts in physics. It describes the quantity of motion an object has and depends on both the object's mass and velocity. Understanding momentum is crucial for analyzing collisions, explosions, and many other physical phenomena.

Pre-knowledge Required: Before diving into momentum, make sure you understand:
  • Mass - A scalar quantity measuring the amount of matter (kg)
  • Velocity - A vector quantity describing speed with direction (m·s⁻¹)

📚 Main Concepts & Principles

What is Momentum?

The momentum of an object is the product of its mass and velocity. It tells us how much "motion" an object possesses.

p = m × v
Where: p = momentum (kg·m·s⁻¹) | m = mass (kg) | v = velocity (m·s⁻¹)
Key Properties of Momentum:
  • Unit: kg·m·s⁻¹ (kilogram metres per second)
  • Momentum is a vector quantity - it has both magnitude AND direction
  • The direction of momentum is the same as the direction of velocity

Why is Momentum a Vector?

Since velocity is a vector and mass is a scalar, multiplying them gives a vector. This means when working with momentum, we must consider direction. Objects moving in opposite directions have momenta with opposite signs.

1 Worked Example: Calculating Momentum

Problem: A 5000 kg bus moves due west at 10 m·s⁻¹. Find its momentum.

Solution:
Given: m = 5000 kg, v = 10 m·s⁻¹ west

p = mv
p = 5000 × 10
p = 50 000 kg·m·s⁻¹ west

Change in Momentum (Δp)

When a net force acts on an object, its velocity changes, and therefore its momentum changes. The change in momentum is calculated as the difference between final and initial momentum.

Δp = m(vf - vi)
Where: Δp = change in momentum | vf = final velocity | vi = initial velocity
Important: When an object reverses direction, the change in velocity (and thus momentum) can be much larger than you might expect. Always consider the signs carefully!
2 Worked Example: Change in Momentum (Same Direction)

Problem: A 1100 kg car accelerates along a straight road and its velocity increases from 10 m·s⁻¹ to 20 m·s⁻¹. Calculate its change in momentum.

Solution:
Given: m = 1100 kg, vi = 10 m·s⁻¹, vf = 20 m·s⁻¹

Δp = m(vf - vi)
Δp = 1100(20 - 10)
Δp = 1100 × 10
Δp = 11 000 kg·m·s⁻¹ (in the direction of motion)
3 Worked Example: Change in Momentum (Deceleration)

Problem: The brakes of a 1500 kg car are applied and its velocity decreases from 10 m·s⁻¹ to 2 m·s⁻¹. Calculate its change in momentum.

Solution:
Given: m = 1500 kg, vi = 10 m·s⁻¹, vf = 2 m·s⁻¹

Δp = m(vf - vi)
Δp = 1500(2 - 10)
Δp = 1500 × (-8)
Δp = -12 000 kg·m·s⁻¹

The negative sign indicates the change is opposite to the original direction of motion.
= 12 000 kg·m·s⁻¹ in the opposite direction to the motion
4 Worked Example: Direction Reversal (Cricket Ball)

Problem: A batsman hits a 160 g cricket ball approaching him at 30 m·s⁻¹ so that it moves in the opposite direction at 40 m·s⁻¹. Calculate the change in momentum of the ball.

Solution:
Take approach (toward batsman) as positive
Given: m = 0.16 kg, vi = +30 m·s⁻¹, vf = -40 m·s⁻¹

Δp = m(vf - vi)
Δp = 0.16(-40 - 30)
Δp = 0.16 × (-70)
Δp = -11.2 kg·m·s⁻¹

= 11.2 kg·m·s⁻¹ away from the batsman
