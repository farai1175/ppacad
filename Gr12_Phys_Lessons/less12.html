Lesson 1: Introduction to Projectile Motion

  • Apply understanding to describe motion of falling objects and objects thrown upward

📋 Overview

Vertical projectile motion is one of the most fundamental concepts in physics. Every time you throw a ball into the air, drop an object, or watch a fountain, you're observing vertical projectile motion in action.

Vertical Projectile Motion deals with:

  • Objects that fall straight down
  • Objects that are thrown straight up
  • Motion of an object as it goes up and then comes down

🧠 Prior Knowledge Review

Before we dive in, let's review some important concepts:

Quantity Definition Unit Type
Distance The actual path length covered m Scalar
Displacement Straight line distance from start to finish m + direction Vector
Speed Rate of change in distance m·s⁻¹ Scalar
Velocity Rate of change in displacement m·s⁻¹ + direction Vector
Acceleration Rate of change in velocity m·s⁻²+ direction Vector

📚 Main Concepts

1. Free Fall

Free Fall: An object is in free fall when the ONLY force acting on it is the gravitational force of the Earth pulling it downwards.

🌍 Gravitational Acceleration

The acceleration due to gravity on Earth is:

g = 9,8 m·s⁻²

Always directed DOWNWARDS toward Earth's centre

2. Objects Falling Straight Down

When an object is dropped from rest:

Parameter Value/Description
Initial velocity (vᵢ) 0 m·s⁻¹ (starts from rest)
Acceleration 9,8 m·s⁻² downwards (constant)
Displacement (y) Equal to the height from which it falls
Final velocity (vf) Maximum value reached on impact

3. Objects Projected Vertically Upwards

When an object is thrown straight up:

⬆️
Going UP

Velocity decreasing
Speed slowing down

⭐ PEAK
Highest Point

v = 0 m·s⁻¹
a = 9,8 m·s⁻² ↓

⬇️
Coming DOWN

Velocity increasing
Speed increasing

Phase Velocity Acceleration
Going UP Decreasing (slowing down) 9,8 m·s⁻² downward
At Highest Point 0 m·s⁻¹ (momentarily stops) 9,8 m·s⁻² downward
Coming DOWN Increasing (speeding up) 9,8 m·s⁻² downward

⚠️ COMMON MISCONCEPTION - IMPORTANT!

Many students think acceleration is zero at the highest point because velocity is zero.

THIS IS WRONG!

Acceleration due to gravity is ALWAYS 9,8 m·s⁻² downward - whether the object is:

  • Moving upward
  • At the highest point
  • Moving downward

4. Time Symmetry

⏱️ Important Principle

The time taken for an object to reach its maximum height is exactly the same as the time it takes to fall back to the launch point.

t(up) = t(down)

✏️ Worked Example

📝 Example Problem

A stone is thrown vertically upwards at an initial velocity of 24 m·s⁻¹. It reaches its maximum height after 2 s.

a) Describe the motion of the stone in terms of velocity and acceleration.

1 Initial motion: The stone starts with a velocity of 24 m·s⁻¹ upwards.
2 Throughout the motion: It experiences a constant downward acceleration of 9,8 m·s⁻².
3 Going up: Its velocity decreases from 24 m·s⁻¹ to zero at the turning point.
4 At the top: The direction of motion changes and velocity begins increasing downward.

b) How long will it take to return to the thrower's hand?

Time up = 2 s, Time down = 2 s
Total time = 4 s

c) What is the velocity at the turning point?

v = 0 m·s⁻¹ (the stone momentarily stops)

d) What is the acceleration at the turning point?

a = 9,8 m·s⁻² downward (acceleration is constant throughout!)
