Overview

This consolidation lesson reinforces key concepts of vertical projectile motion through practical problem-solving and analysis of real-world scenarios.

Key Equations of Motion

vf = vi + g·Δt
Δy = vi·Δt + ½·g·Δt²
vf² = vi² + 2·g·Δy

Where g = -9.8 m/s² (when upward is positive)

Important concepts to remember:

  • Velocity is zero at maximum height
  • Acceleration is constant at -9.8 m/s²
  • Time up = Time down (for same height)
  • Always specify your positive direction
  • Include units with final answers

Projectile Motion Characteristics

up tdown tup = tdown

Consolidation Exercises

Work through these exercises to reinforce your understanding of vertical projectile motion concepts.

Exercise 1: Ball Kicked from Roof

A boy stands on a roof and kicks a ball from a position 5 m above the ground. The ball goes vertically into the air with an initial velocity of 4 m/s. The ball lands on the ground after 1.8 s.

Full Solution:

a) At maximum height, velocity is always 0 m/s

b) Acceleration is always -9.8 m/s² (gravity)

c) vf = vi + g·Δt → 0 = 4 + (-9.8)·Δt → Δt = 0.41 s

d) vf = vi + g·Δt = 4 + (-9.8)(1.8) = -13.64 m/s

Exercise 2: Bouncing Ball

A ball is dropped from a height of 2.5 m and after bouncing it reaches a height of 1.5 m. It then bounces for a second time reaching a height of 0.6 m. Let up be positive.

Full Solution:

a) When dropped, initial velocity is 0 m/s

b) vf² = vi² + 2gΔy = 0² + 2(-9.8)(-2.5) → vf = -7 m/s

c) At maximum height after bounce, velocity is 0 m/s

d) vf² = vi² + 2gΔy → 0 = vi² + 2(-9.8)(1.5) → vi = 5.42 m/s

Galileo's Experiment

Galileo Galilei formulated the Law of Uniform Acceleration of Falling Bodies, predicting that any two bodies falling from the same height would hit the ground at the same time, regardless of mass.

Key Scientific Method Terms:

  • Hypothesis: What you think will happen
  • Independent variable: The variable you change
  • Dependent variable: The variable you measure
  • Controlled variables: Variables kept constant

In Galileo's experiment, the independent variable was mass, and the dependent variable was time to fall.
