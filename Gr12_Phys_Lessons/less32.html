Transmission and Scattering of Light

Lesson 1: Understanding Light Scattering

Lesson Progress: 0%

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Explain what is meant by the scattering of light
  • Describe how the size of particles affects light scattering
  • Understand Rayleigh Scattering and its characteristics
  • Explain why the sky appears blue during the day
  • Explain why the sky appears red/orange at sunrise and sunset
  • Apply the relationship between wavelength and frequency of light

📚 Overview

When light travels through the atmosphere, it interacts with tiny particles such as dust, water droplets, and gas molecules. Unlike reflection from smooth surfaces where light bounces in a predictable direction, light hitting small particles scatters in various directions. This phenomenon, known as scattering, is responsible for many beautiful optical effects we observe in nature, including the blue color of the sky and the red hues of sunsets.

💡
Key Concept: Scattering occurs when light is deflected in different directions upon encountering particles. The degree of scattering depends on the size of the particles and the wavelength of light.

🔄 Pre-Knowledge Review

Before we explore scattering, let's review some important concepts:

Essential Prior Knowledge

  • Light as a wave: Light is an electromagnetic wave that can be characterized by its wavelength and frequency.
  • Reflection: The change in direction of a wavefront at an interface between two different media so that the wavefront returns into the medium from which it originated.
  • Refraction: Light bends when it travels from one medium to another of different optical density.
  • Spectrum of light: White light can be separated into its component colors (ROYGBIV).

Wave Equation

c = f × λ

where c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s (speed of light), f = frequency (Hz), λ = wavelength (m)

📖 Main Concepts & Principles

1. What is Scattering?

When parallel light rays fall onto a smooth surface, they are reflected parallel and evenly in a new direction. However, when light encounters a rough surface or small particles, the reflected rays scatter in various directions. This reflection off a rough surface or from particles is called diffuse reflection or scattering.

Definition of Scattering

Scattering is the process by which light is deflected in various directions when it interacts with particles or irregularities in a medium. When light passes through the atmosphere, it is scattered by dust particles in the air and water droplets in clouds.

2. Factors Affecting Scattering

The degree of scattering depends on two main factors:

  • The size of the particles: Smaller particles scatter light more effectively.
  • The wavelength of the light: Shorter wavelengths are scattered more than longer wavelengths.

4. Rayleigh Scattering

Definition

Rayleigh Scattering is the scattering of light by particles that are smaller than the wavelength of the light. Named after Lord Rayleigh, this type of scattering explains why the sky appears blue.

Key principle: Short wavelengths (blue end of the spectrum) are scattered far more than long wavelengths (red end).

5. Why is the Sky Blue?

The sky appears blue because:

  • Sunlight enters Earth's atmosphere and encounters tiny gas molecules (mainly nitrogen and oxygen).
  • These molecules are much smaller than the wavelength of visible light.
  • Blue light has a shorter wavelength than red light.
  • Shorter wavelengths are scattered more effectively in all directions.
  • When we look at the sky (not directly at the sun), we see this scattered blue light coming from all directions.

6. Why are Sunsets Red?

At sunrise and sunset:

  • Sunlight has to travel through more of the atmosphere to reach our eyes.
  • Most of the blue wavelengths have been scattered away during this longer journey.
  • The remaining light that reaches us is predominantly red and orange.
  • This is why the sun and sky appear red/orange at these times.

✏️ Worked Examples

Example 1: Calculating Wavelength from Frequency

Question: What colour is associated with the frequency 4.9 × 10¹⁴ Hz?

Step 1: Write down the wave equation

c = f × λ

Step 2: Rearrange to find wavelength

λ = c / f

Step 3: Substitute values

λ = (3 × 10⁸ m/s) / (4.9 × 10¹⁴ Hz)

λ = 6.12 × 10⁻⁷ m

Step 4: Convert to nanometers

λ = 6.12 × 10⁻⁷ m × (10⁹ nm/m) = 612 nm

Answer: From the table, 612 nm falls in the orange range (622 - 597 nm). The colour is Orange.

Example 2: Why Does an Apple Appear Red?

Question: Explain why an apple appears to be red in white light.

Explanation:

The apple consists of atoms whose electrons have the same natural frequency as all the colours of light, except red.

This means:

  • All colours except red are absorbed by the apple's surface.
  • Red light is reflected because it doesn't match the natural frequency of the electrons.
  • Therefore, the apple appears red in colour under white light because only red light reaches our eyes.

🔬 Demonstration: The Milk Experiment

Materials Needed:

  • A clear, straight-sided glass jar or drinking glass
  • Water
  • Milk (a few teaspoons)
  • Torch (flashlight)
  • Darkened room

Observations:

  • The image on the screen appears red/orange
  • The milk in the glass appears blue
  • As more milk is added, less light passes through the liquid

Explanation:

The fat particles in milk are small enough to scatter light (similar to Rayleigh scattering):

  • Blue wavelengths are scattered more, making the milk appear blue when viewed from the side.
  • Red wavelengths pass through more easily, making the light on the screen appear red/orange.
  • This mimics how our atmosphere scatters sunlight!
