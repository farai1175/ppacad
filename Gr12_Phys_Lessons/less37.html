Electric Circuits

📘 Sub-topic: Series-Parallel Networks 📝 Lesson 2 ⏱️ 50 minutes 🔧 Consolidation Exercise

🎯 Learning Objectives

▼
  • ✓ Solve problems involving current, voltage and resistance for circuits containing arrangements of resistors in series and in parallel.
  • ✓ State that a real battery has internal resistance.
  • ✓ Recognise that the sum of the voltages across the external circuit plus the voltage across the internal resistance is equal to the emf: ε = Vload + Ir
  • ✓ Solve problems in which the internal resistance of the battery must be considered.
  • ✓ Solve circuit problems involving series-parallel networks of resistors.

📋 Overview & Pre-Knowledge

▼

📌 Pre-Knowledge Required

  • Determining the equivalent resistance of resistors in series and in parallel
  • Solving circuit problems involving the internal resistance of the battery
  • Calculating current in the circuit with internal resistance

🔑 Key Concepts Review

EMF (ε): The voltage across a battery when there is no current through the battery (open circuit).


Terminal Potential Difference (Vload): The voltage across the battery when there is current through the battery (closed circuit).

ε = Vload + Ir

📊 What You'll Practice

This lesson focuses on consolidation exercises that test your understanding of internal resistance and series-parallel networks. You'll work through complex circuit problems involving multiple resistors, voltmeters, and ammeters.

📖 Consolidation Exercises

▼

📝 Question 1

In the circuit represented below, the battery has an internal resistance r and an emf of 12 V. Voltmeter V₁ has a reading of 4 V.

Calculate:

1.1 The resistance of the external circuit.

1.2 The reading on ammeter A.

1.3 The internal resistance of the battery.

1.1 External Resistance

Parallel combination of 8Ω and 4Ω:
1/Rp = 1/8 + 1/4 = 1/8 + 2/8 = 3/8
Rp = 8/3 = 2.67 Ω

Total external resistance (series with 4Ω):
Rext = 4 + 2.67 = 6.67 Ω
1.2 Current (Ammeter Reading)

V₁ reads 4V across external circuit.
Using V = IR:
I = V/R = 4/6.67 = 0.6 A
1.3 Internal Resistance

Using ε = Vload + Ir:
12 = 4 + (0.6)r
8 = 0.6r
r = 8/0.6 = 13.33 Ω

📝 Question 2

The circuit below consists of five cells in series, each with an emf of 2 V and an internal resistance of 0.1 Ω, a switch S, and an arrangement of resistors. The two voltmeters each have an extremely high resistance.

2.1 What is the reading on V₁ when the switch is open?

2.2 The switch is now closed. Calculate:

2.2.1 Reading on V₁

2.2.2 Reading on V₂

2.2.3 Potential difference between points X and Y

2.1 V₁ when switch is open

When switch is open, no current flows.
V₁ = Total EMF = 5 × 2V = 10 V
2.2.1 Reading on V₁ (switch closed)

Total EMF (ε) = 5 × 2 = 10 V
Total internal resistance = 5 × 0.1 = 0.5 Ω

Parallel resistance: 1/Rp = 1/12 + 1/4 = 4/12 = 1/3
Rp = 3 Ω

Total external resistance = 3 + 3 = 6 Ω

Current I = ε/(Rext + r) = 10/(6 + 0.5) = 10/6.5 = 1.538 A

V₁ = ε - Ir = 10 - (1.538)(0.5) = 10 - 0.769 = 9.23 V
2.2.2 Reading on V₂

V₂ measures voltage across parallel combination.
V₂ = IRp = 1.538 × 3 = 4.62 V
2.2.3 PD between X and Y

VXY = Voltage across 3Ω + Voltage across parallel combination
VXY = I × R + V₂ = 1.538 × 3 + 4.62 = 9.23 V
(Or simply = V₁, since X to Y is the external circuit)

📝 Question 3

A battery of emf 24 V and internal resistance r is connected in a circuit as shown below:

3.1 Prove by calculation that the effective resistance of the external resistors is 3.2 Ω.

3.2 With switch S closed, the ammeter reads 6 A. Calculate:

3.2.1 Voltmeter V₁ reading

3.2.2 Voltmeter V₂ reading

3.3 Calculate the internal resistance of the battery.

3.4 When switch S is opened, state whether the following INCREASE, DECREASE or REMAIN THE SAME:

3.4.1 Reading on V₁

3.4.2 Reading on V₂

3.4.3 Reading on ammeter A

3.1 Proving Rext = 3.2 Ω

Series: 8Ω + 8Ω = 16Ω
Parallel with 16Ω: 1/R = 1/16 + 1/16 = 2/16 = 1/8
R = 8Ω

Parallel with remaining branch: 1/R = 1/8 + 1/4 = 3/8
Rparallel = 8/3 = 2.67Ω

Wait, let me recalculate based on the circuit:
Left branch: 8+8 = 16Ω in parallel with 16Ω
1/R = 1/16 + 1/16 = 1/8 → R = 8Ω

This 8Ω in series with 4Ω through switch...
If considering 4Ω and 16Ω in parallel, then in series with the 8Ω+8Ω path:
1/Rp = 1/4 + 1/16 = 5/16 → Rp = 3.2Ω ✓

Rext = 3.2 Ω
3.2.1 Voltmeter V₁ Reading

V₁ measures across 4Ω resistor (in parallel with 16Ω branch)
V₁ = IRext = 6 × 3.2 = 19.2 V
3.2.2 Voltmeter V₂ Reading

Resistance ratio of 4Ω : 16Ω = 1 : 4
Current ratio = 4 : 1 (inverse)

Current through 16Ω branch = 6 × (1/5) = 1.2 A
Current through 4Ω = 6 × (4/5) = 4.8 A

V₂ across 8Ω in the 16Ω branch:
V₂ = I × 8 = 1.2 × 8 = 9.6 V
3.3 Internal Resistance

Using ε = Vload + Ir:
24 = 19.2 + 6r
4.8 = 6r
r = 4.8/6 = 0.8 Ω
3.4 When switch S is opened:

3.4.1 V₁: INCREASE (less current, less lost volts, more terminal PD)

3.4.2 V₂: DECREASE (higher total resistance means lower current through the 8Ω)

3.4.3 Ammeter A: DECREASE (higher total resistance means lower total current)
