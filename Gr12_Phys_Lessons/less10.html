Overview

+

The work-energy theorem states that the net work done by an external/resultant force on an object/system equals the change in kinetic energy of the object or system.

Wnet = ΔEk = Ekf - Eki

Where:

Wnet = Net work done (J)

ΔEk = Change in kinetic energy (J)

Ekf = Final kinetic energy (J)

Eki = Initial kinetic energy (J)

This theorem connects the concepts of work and energy, showing that when work is done on an object, it results in a change in the object's kinetic energy.

Understanding the Work-Energy Theorem

The work-energy theorem is derived from Newton's second law of motion. When a net force acts on an object, it causes acceleration, which changes the object's velocity and therefore its kinetic energy.

From Newton's Second Law: Fnet = m·a

Work done: W = F·d·cosθ

Kinetic energy: Ek = ½mv²

Therefore: Wnet = ΔEk = ½mvf² - ½mvi²

Key Points:

  • Work is done when an external force acts on an object
  • If this external force results in acceleration, the kinetic energy changes
  • The net work done equals the change in kinetic energy
  • Both work and energy are scalar quantities with SI units of joules (J)
Worked Example 1: Horizontal Motion with Friction

A 4 kg block initially at rest is pulled to the right along a rough horizontal surface by a constant horizontal force of 12 N. The coefficient of dynamic friction is 0.15. Using the work-energy theorem, calculate the speed of the block after it has moved a distance of 3 m.

Solution:

Frictional force: Ff = μ·m·g = 0.15 × 4 × 9.8 = 5.88 N (left)

Net force: Fnet = 12 N - 5.88 N = 6.12 N (right)

Net work: Wnet = Fnet·Δx·cosθ = 6.12 × 3 × 1 = 18.36 J

Using work-energy theorem: Wnet = ΔEk = ½mvf² - ½mvi²

18.36 = ½ × 4 × vf² - 0

vf² = 9.18

vf = √9.18 ≈ 3.03 m/s

Worked Example 2: Inclined Plane

A 4 kg object slides up a frictionless ramp at 20° to the horizontal with an initial speed of 4 m/s. How far does it slide up the ramp before coming to rest?

Solution:

Component of gravity parallel to incline: Fg// = m·g·sinθ = 4 × 9.8 × sin20° ≈ 13.4 N

Using work-energy theorem: Wnet = ΔEk

Fg//·Δx·cos180° = ½mvf² - ½mvi²

-13.4 × Δx = 0 - ½ × 4 × 4²

-13.4 × Δx = -32

Δx ≈ 2.39 m
