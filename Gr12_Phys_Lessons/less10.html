Work, Power and Energy

The Work-Energy Theorem

📚 Lesson 2
⚡ W = ΔEₖ
⏱️ ~60 min

Lesson Overview

The Work-Energy Theorem is one of the most powerful tools in physics. It directly connects the work done by forces to the change in an object's kinetic energy. In this lesson, you'll learn to apply this theorem to solve problems involving motion on horizontal surfaces, inclined planes, and situations with friction.

Learning Objectives

  • 1Define and state the Work-Energy Theorem
  • 2Apply the theorem: W = ΔEₖ = Eₖf - Eₖi
  • 3Solve problems on horizontal and inclined planes (with and without friction)
  • 4Understand how net force changes kinetic energy

The Work-Energy Theorem

📜 Work-Energy Theorem
"The net work done on an object by the resultant force equals the change in the kinetic energy of that object."
Wnet = ΔEk = Ekf - Eki
Wnet = ½mvf² - ½mvi²
Net work equals final kinetic energy minus initial kinetic energy
Wnet = Fnet · Δx · cos θ
Work by net force
Ek = ½mv²
Kinetic energy
ΔEk = Ekf - Eki
Change in KE

Understanding the Theorem

➕

Positive Net Work

When Wnet > 0, kinetic energy increases. The object speeds up.

➖

Negative Net Work

When Wnet < 0, kinetic energy decreases. The object slows down.

⚖️

Zero Net Work

When Wnet = 0, kinetic energy stays constant. Constant velocity.

💡 Key Insights

  • A net force causes acceleration, which changes velocity
  • Change in velocity means change in kinetic energy
  • Work done = Energy transferred
  • Both work and energy are scalar quantities (measured in Joules)
  • The theorem applies regardless of the path taken

Applications

On Horizontal Surfaces:
Wnet = (Fapplied - Ffriction) × Δx = ΔEk
On Inclined Planes:
Wnet = (Fapplied - Ffriction - mg sin θ) × Δx = ΔEk
For motion up the slope. Reverse signs for motion down.
Friction Force:
Ffriction = μ × N = μ × mg (on horizontal surface)
Ffriction = μ × mg cos θ (on inclined plane)

Worked Examples

📝 Example 1: Cart Acceleration

Problem: Jabu pushes a 5 kg cart, increasing its speed from 1 m/s to 2 m/s over 50 m. Calculate the work done and the force applied.

1Initial kinetic energy:
Eki = ½mvi² = ½ × 5 × 1² = 2.5 J
2Final kinetic energy:
Ekf = ½mvf² = ½ × 5 × 2² = 10 J
3Work done (Work-Energy Theorem):
W = ΔEk = Ekf - Eki = 10 - 2.5 = 7.5 J
4Force applied:
W = F × Δx × cos 0°
7.5 = F × 50 × 1
F = 0.15 N
📝 Example 2: Block with Friction

Problem: A 4 kg block initially at rest is pulled by a 12 N force on a rough surface (μ = 0.15). Find its speed after moving 3 m.

1Calculate friction force:
Ff = μmg = 0.15 × 4 × 9.8 = 5.88 N
2Calculate net force:
Fnet = 12 - 5.88 = 6.12 N
3Calculate net work:
Wnet = Fnet × Δx = 6.12 × 3 = 18.36 J
4Apply Work-Energy Theorem:
Wnet = ½mvf² - ½mvi²
18.36 = ½ × 4 × vf² - 0
vf² = 9.18
vf = 3.03 m/s
📝 Example 3: Braking Vehicle

Problem: A 2400 kg vehicle brakes from 20 m/s to rest with a net braking force of 9600 N. How far does it travel?

1Apply Work-Energy Theorem:
Fnet × Δx × cos 180° = ½mvf² - ½mvi²
(cos 180° because force opposes motion)
2Substitute values:
-9600 × Δx = ½ × 2400 × 0² - ½ × 2400 × 20²
-9600 × Δx = 0 - 480,000
-9600 × Δx = -480,000
3Solve for distance:
Δx = 480,000 ÷ 9600 = 50 m
📝 Example 4: Object on Frictionless Ramp

Problem: A 4 kg object moving at 4 m/s slides up a frictionless ramp at 20°. How far does it travel up the ramp?

1Force parallel to ramp (opposing motion):
F// = mg sin θ = 4 × 9.8 × sin 20° = 13.4 N
2Apply Work-Energy Theorem:
F// × Δx × cos 180° = ½mvf² - ½mvi²
-13.4 × Δx = 0 - ½ × 4 × 4²
-13.4 × Δx = -32
3Solve for distance:
Δx = 32 ÷ 13.4 = 2.39 m
📝 Example 5: Cyclist Acceleration

Problem: A net force of 20 N accelerates a 50 kg bicycle from rest over 50 m. (a) Find kinetic energy after 50 m. (b) Find velocity.

aWork done = Kinetic energy gained:
Wnet = F × Δx = 20 × 50 = 1000 J
Since starting from rest, Ekf = 1000 J
bFind velocity:
Ek = ½mv²
1000 = ½ × 50 × v²
v² = 40
v = 6.32 m/s

Lesson Summary

📌 Key Points

  • Work-Energy Theorem: Wnet = ΔEk = Ekf - Eki
  • Net work done equals the change in kinetic energy
  • Positive work increases kinetic energy (speeds up)
  • Negative work decreases kinetic energy (slows down)
  • Kinetic energy: Ek = ½mv²
  • Friction always does negative work (opposes motion)
  • On inclines: consider mg sin θ component
  • SI unit for both work and energy is the Joule (J)
