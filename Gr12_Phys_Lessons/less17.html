Overview

+

This lesson focuses on the mathematical formulation of the Doppler Effect. We'll learn to use the Doppler equation to calculate the frequency heard by a listener when either the source or listener is moving.

The Doppler Equation

fL = (v ± vL) / (v ± vS) × fS

Where:

fL = frequency heard by listener (Hz)

fS = frequency emitted by source (Hz)

v = speed of sound in air (340 m/s)

vS = speed of source (m/s)

vL = speed of listener (m/s)

Sign Convention Guide

S Source Moving away from listener Use: v + vS Listener Moving toward source Use: v + vL Listener Moving away from source Use: v - vL

Main Body

+

Understanding the Doppler Equation

The Doppler equation allows us to calculate the frequency heard by a listener when there is relative motion between the source and listener. The key is using the correct sign convention based on the direction of motion.

Worked Example: Ambulance Siren

An ambulance moving at 15 m/s emits a siren at 1000 Hz. Calculate the frequency heard by a stationary listener when:

a) The ambulance is moving toward the listener

b) The ambulance is moving away from the listener

Solution:

Given: v = 340 m/s, vS = 15 m/s, fS = 1000 Hz, vL = 0 m/s

a) Source moving toward listener: fL = v / (v - vS) × fS

fL = 340 / (340 - 15) × 1000 = 340 / 325 × 1000 = 1046.15 Hz

b) Source moving away from listener: fL = v / (v + vS) × fS

fL = 340 / (340 + 15) × 1000 = 340 / 355 × 1000 = 957.75 Hz

Practice Exercises

Exercise 1: Ambulance Scenario

An ambulance moving at 40 m/s approaches a traffic light where a blind man and his dog are waiting. The siren transmits waves at 350 Hz. The speed of sound in air is 340 m/s.

Solution:

fL = v / (v - vS) × fS

fL = 340 / (340 - 40) × 350

fL = 340 / 300 × 350 = 396.67 Hz

Exercise 2: Train Scenario

A train moving at 180 km/h approaches a railway crossing and transmits a siren at 2000 Hz. Calculate the frequency heard by pedestrians as the train approaches.

Solution:

First convert train speed: 180 km/h = 180 / 3.6 = 50 m/s

fL = v / (v - vS) × fS

fL = 340 / (340 - 50) × 2000

fL = 340 / 290 × 2000 = 2344.83 Hz

Exercise 3: Racing Car Scenario

A racing car approaches an observer at 180 km/h. The car emits sound at 1250 Hz.

Solution:

Car speed: 180 km/h = 180 / 3.6 = 50 m/s

a) fL = v / (v - vS) × fS = 340 / (340 - 50) × 1250 = 1465.52 Hz

b) At the instant of passing, relative velocity is perpendicular, so fL = fS = 1250 Hz

c) fL = v / (v + vS) × fS = 340 / (340 + 50) × 1250 = 1089.74 Hz

Unit Conversion Guide

km/h to m/s: Divide by 3.6

Example: 72 km/h = 72 ÷ 3.6 = 20 m/s

kHz to Hz: Multiply by 1000

Example: 20 kHz = 20 × 1000 = 20,000 Hz
