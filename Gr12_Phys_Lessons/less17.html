🔢 Doppler Effect Calculations

Sub-topic: Using the Doppler Equation

Lesson 2: Calculating Observed Frequency

🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:

  • Use the Doppler equation to calculate the frequency of sound detected by a listener
  • Apply the equation when the listener is stationary and the source is moving
  • Apply the equation when the source is stationary and the listener is moving
  • Correctly identify when to add or subtract velocities in the equation
  • Convert units appropriately (km/h to m/s, kHz to Hz)

📚 Pre-Knowledge Review

🔢 Units of Frequency ▼

Frequency is measured in Hertz (Hz)

1 Hz = 1 wave per second

Common conversions:

  • 1 kHz (kilohertz) = 1000 Hz = 10³ Hz
  • 1 MHz (megahertz) = 1,000,000 Hz = 10⁶ Hz
Example: 20 kHz = 20 × 10³ Hz = 20,000 Hz
🚗 Speed Unit Conversions ▼

Converting km/h to m/s:

Divide by 3.6 (or multiply by 1000/3600)

72 km/h = 72 ÷ 3.6 = 20 m/s

🔊 Speed of Sound ▼

The speed of sound in air at room temperature is approximately 340 m/s

This value may vary slightly depending on temperature and altitude, but for most calculations, we use 340 m/s as the standard value.

📐 The Doppler Equation

The Doppler equation allows us to calculate the frequency of sound waves that a listener hears when there is relative motion between the source and the listener.

The Doppler Equation for Sound
fL = v ± vL v ∓ vS × fS
fL Frequency heard by listener (Hz)
fS Frequency emitted by source (Hz)
v Speed of sound in air (≈340 m/s)
vL Speed of listener (m/s)
vS Speed of source (m/s)

When to Use + or - Signs

🟢 Moving Towards Each Other

When source and listener move towards each other:

fL = (v + vL) / (v - vS) × fS

↑ Higher frequency (higher pitch)

🔴 Moving Away From Each Other

When source and listener move away from each other:

fL = (v - vL) / (v + vS) × fS

↓ Lower frequency (lower pitch)

💡
Memory Tip: When approaching, the fraction should be greater than 1 (larger numerator, smaller denominator) to give a higher frequency. When receding, the fraction should be less than 1 to give a lower frequency.
⚠️ Important: If the listener is stationary, vL = 0. If the source is stationary, vS = 0. Always convert speeds to m/s before calculating!

✏️ Worked Examples

Ambulance Approaching a Stationary Listener

The siren of an ambulance emits waves at a frequency of 1000 Hz. Determine the frequency of the sound heard by a stationary listener when the ambulance is moving towards the listener at a speed of 15 m/s.

Use speed of sound = 340 m/s

📋 Step 1: Identify Given Information
  • fS = 1000 Hz (source frequency)
  • vS = 15 m/s (source moving towards)
  • vL = 0 m/s (listener stationary)
  • v = 340 m/s (speed of sound)
📝 Step 2: Choose the Correct Equation

Source approaching listener → use minus in denominator:

fL = (v + vL) / (v - vS) × fS
🔢 Step 3: Substitute and Calculate
fL = (340 + 0) / (340 - 15) × 1000 fL = 340 / 325 × 1000 fL = 1.0462 × 1000 fL = 1046.15 Hz
✓ Answer: fL = 1046.15 Hz

The frequency is higher than 1000 Hz because the ambulance is approaching!

Ambulance Moving Away from Listener

Using the same ambulance (siren frequency 1000 Hz), determine the frequency heard when it moves away from the stationary listener at 15 m/s.

📝 Step 1: Choose the Correct Equation

Source moving away → use plus in denominator:

fL = (v + vL) / (v + vS) × fS
🔢 Step 2: Substitute and Calculate
fL = (340 + 0) / (340 + 15) × 1000 fL = 340 / 355 × 1000 fL = 0.9577 × 1000 fL = 957.75 Hz
✓ Answer: fL = 957.75 Hz

The frequency is lower than 1000 Hz because the ambulance is moving away!

Train Approaching with Unit Conversion

A train moving at 180 km/h approaches a railway crossing. It transmits a siren at a frequency of 2000 Hz. Calculate the frequency the pedestrians hear as the train approaches.

Use speed of sound = 340 m/s

🔄 Step 1: Convert Units
vS = 180 km/h ÷ 3.6 = 50 m/s
📋 Step 2: List Given Values
  • fS = 2000 Hz
  • vS = 50 m/s
  • vL = 0 m/s (pedestrians stationary)
  • v = 340 m/s
🔢 Step 3: Calculate
fL = (v + vL) / (v - vS) × fS fL = (340 + 0) / (340 - 50) × 2000 fL = 340 / 290 × 2000 fL = 1.1724 × 2000 fL = 2344.83 Hz
✓ Answer: fL = 2344.83 Hz

Racing Car - Complete Scenario

A racing car approaches an observer at 180 km/h. The frequency of the sound the car emits is 1250 Hz. Calculate:

  1. The frequency when the car approaches
  2. The frequency at the instant the car passes (relative velocity = 0)
  3. The frequency when the car moves away
🔄 Convert Speed
vS = 180 km/h ÷ 3.6 = 50 m/s
1️⃣ Car Approaching
fL = (340 + 0) / (340 - 50) × 1250 fL = 340 / 290 × 1250 fL = 1465.52 Hz
Approaching: fL = 1465.52 Hz ↑
2️⃣ Car Passing (at the instant)

At the exact moment the car passes, there is no relative motion towards or away from the observer.

Passing: fL = 1250 Hz (same as source)
3️⃣ Car Moving Away
fL = (340 + 0) / (340 + 50) × 1250 fL = 340 / 390 × 1250 fL = 1089.74 Hz
Receding: fL = 1089.74 Hz ↓

📋 Lesson Summary

Key Formulas to Remember:

Approaching: fL = (v + vL) / (v - vS) × fS

Receding: fL = (v - vL) / (v + vS) × fS

Key Points:

  • Always convert units first: km/h ÷ 3.6 = m/s; kHz × 1000 = Hz
  • Stationary listener: vL = 0
  • Stationary source: vS = 0
  • Approaching: frequency increases (higher pitch)
  • Receding: frequency decreases (lower pitch)
  • At passing moment: observed frequency = source frequency
🎓
Exam Tip: Always show your working clearly in calculations. Write down given values, state the equation you're using, substitute values, and clearly indicate your final answer with the correct unit (Hz).
