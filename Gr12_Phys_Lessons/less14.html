📌 Important Note

Equations of motion are equations used to determine the motion of a body experiencing a force as a function of time. They apply only to bodies moving in one dimension with constant acceleration.

📈 The Three Motion Graphs

For vertical projectile motion, we use three different graphs:

📍 Position-Time

Curved parabola shape. Shows height vs time.

🚀 Velocity-Time

Straight line with gradient = -9,8 m·s⁻²

⚡ Acceleration-Time

Horizontal line at a = -9,8 m·s⁻²

🔍 Understanding Each Graph

1. Acceleration-Time Graph

📊 Characteristics

  • Always a horizontal line
  • Value is constant at a = g = -9,8 m·s⁻²
  • Negative because acceleration is always downward
  • Does NOT change regardless of direction of motion

2. Velocity-Time Graph

📊 Characteristics

  • Always a straight line
  • Gradient = acceleration = -9,8 m·s⁻²
  • Starts at initial velocity (vi)
  • Crosses zero at maximum height (for upward throw)
  • Area under graph = displacement

3. Position-Time (Displacement-Time) Graph

📊 Characteristics

  • Always a curved parabola
  • Maximum point at the highest position reached
  • Gradient at any point = velocity at that instant
  • Gradient is zero at maximum height
  • Follows equation: y = vit + ½gt²

✏️ Worked Example

📝 Object Dropped from Ascending Balloon

An object is dropped from a hot air balloon which is ascending at a constant speed of 2 m·s⁻¹. Ignore the effects of air resistance.

a) Calculate how far below the point of release the object will be after 4 s.

b) Draw velocity vs time and acceleration vs time graphs for the motion of the object.

Part (a): Calculate displacement

1 Choose direction: Take UP as positive
2 Identify known values:
vi = +2 m·s⁻¹ (moving up with balloon)
g = -9,8 m·s⁻²
t = 4 s
y = ?
3 Choose equation: y = vit + ½gt²
4 Substitute and solve:
y = vit + ½gt²
y = (2)(4) + ½(-9,8)(4)²
y = 8 + ½(-9,8)(16)
y = 8 - 78,4
y = -70,4 m

The negative sign indicates the object is 70,4 m below the point of release.

Part (b): Draw the graphs

💡 Key Insight

The object initially has an upward velocity of +2 m·s⁻¹ (same as the balloon). It then decelerates, stops momentarily, and falls downward with increasing speed.

Velocity-Time Graph

Starts at +2 m·s⁻¹, crosses zero, becomes negative

Acceleration-Time Graph

Constant at -9,8 m·s⁻² throughout
