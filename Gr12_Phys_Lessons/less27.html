Advantages of Alternating Current

🔑 Key Advantage

The main advantage of alternating current is that the voltage can be changed using transformers.

Why is this important?

🏭 At Power Stations

Voltage is stepped UP to very high values (up to 400 kV) for transmission. High voltage means low current for the same power.

🔌 Low Energy Loss

Low current means less energy lost as heat in transmission lines (P = I²R). This makes long-distance transmission efficient.

🏠 At Buildings

Voltage is stepped DOWN to safe levels (220V in SA) for use in homes, offices, and street lights.

🔄 Flexibility

Different voltages can be supplied for different applications - industrial (high) vs domestic (low) use.

RMS Values & Formulas

📝 Definition

Root Mean Square (RMS) is a statistical measure of the magnitude of a varying quantity. For AC, it represents the effective value that produces the same heating effect as an equivalent DC.

📊 Essential RMS Formulas

Vrms = Vmax / √2

RMS Voltage from Peak Voltage

Irms = Imax / √2

RMS Current from Peak Current

Paverage = Vrms × Irms

Average Power in AC Circuit

Paverage = V²rms / R = I²rms × R

Alternative Power Formulas

⚠️ Important Note

Remember that √2 ≈ 1.414. So Vrms ≈ 0.707 × Vmax. The RMS value is approximately 70.7% of the peak value.

Multi-plug Safety

Understanding why multi-plugs have cut-off switches is important for electrical safety. When multiple appliances are connected in parallel, total resistance decreases and current increases.

⚠️ Safety Warning

High current causes excessive heating (P = I²R) which can damage wires and cause fires. Cut-off switches disconnect the circuit when current exceeds safe limits.

Calculating Average Power from RMS Values

Calculate the average power dissipated by an AC generator if the peak current is 13 A and peak voltage is 325 V.

Solution:
Step 1: Calculate Vrms

Vrms = Vmax / √2

Vrms = 325 / √2

Vrms = 325 / 1.414

Vrms = 229.8 V ≈ 230 V

Step 2: Calculate Irms

Irms = Imax / √2

Irms = 13 / 1.414

Irms = 9.19 A

Step 3: Calculate Average Power

Paverage = Vrms × Irms

Paverage = 230 × 9.19

Paverage = 2113.7 W ≈ 2114 W

Alternative: Pavg = (Vmax × Imax)/2 = (325 × 13)/2 = 2112.5 W

NSC November 2008 Paper 1 - Power Cutback Problem

The municipality of Dinaledi implements a power cutback in the town. As a result of the cutback, the RMS voltage drops from 220 Vrms to 200 Vrms.

2.1 Calculate the peak voltage during the cutback.

2.2 A certain electrical appliance dissipates 1200 W when operated at 220 Vrms. Calculate the power at which it will operate during the cutback.

2.3 Explain why multi-plugs have cut-off switches.

Solution 2.1: Calculate Peak Voltage
Using Vrms = Vmax/√2

200 = Vmax / √2

Vmax = 200 × √2

Vmax = 200 × 1.414

Vmax = 282.84 V

Solution 2.2: Calculate Power at Reduced Voltage
Step 1: Find the resistance of the appliance

At 220V: P = V²rms/R

1200 = (220)²/R

R = 48400/1200

R = 40.33 Ω

Step 2: Calculate power at 200V

Paverage = V²rms/R

Paverage = (200)²/40.33

Paverage = 40000/40.33

Paverage = 991.82 W

Solution 2.3: Multi-plug Cut-off Switch
Physics Explanation
  • Voltage (V) stays constant at the mains supply
  • When more appliances are connected to the multi-plug, they are connected in parallel
  • Parallel connection causes the total resistance to decrease
  • From I = V/R, if R decreases and V is constant, current (I) increases
  • Due to the high current, the heating effect increases (P = I²R)
  • Excessive heating can damage the main switch and wiring, potentially causing a fire hazard
  • The cut-off switch disconnects the circuit when current exceeds safe limits
