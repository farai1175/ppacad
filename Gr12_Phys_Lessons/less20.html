⚡ Electrical Machines

Sub-topic: Introduction to Generators and Motors

Lesson 1: Understanding Electrical Machines & Faraday's Law

🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:

  • Understand what is meant by electrical machines
  • State that generators convert mechanical energy to electrical energy
  • State that motors convert electrical energy to mechanical energy
  • Use Faraday's Law to explain why a current is induced in a coil rotated in a magnetic field
  • Identify factors that affect the strength of induced current

📚 Pre-Knowledge Review

Before we dive into electrical machines, let's review some important concepts:

Electromagnetism: The study of the properties of and relationship between electric currents and magnetic fields.

Magnetic Field: A region around a magnetic material or a moving electrical charge where the force of magnetism acts.

Solenoid: A large number of insulated turns forming a cylindrical coil.

Electromagnet: A conductor which becomes magnetic when there is current flowing through it.

📋 Factors Affecting Electromagnet Strength:

  • The current in the conductor
  • The number of turns around the core
  • The type of substance used in the core

⚙️ What are Electrical Machines?

Electrical machines are devices that convert energy from one form to another through the interaction between a magnetic field and a set of windings (coils).

Key Principles:

  • A current-carrying conductor has a magnetic field induced around it
  • A changing magnetic field can induce an EMF, resulting in the flow of current
  • Such a current is called an induced current
  • Both generators and motors operate through the interaction between magnetic fields and coils

Two Types of Electrical Machines

🔌

⚡ Generator

Converts mechanical energy into electrical energy

Mechanical ➡️ Electrical

Principle: Based on Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction

Examples: Power stations, wind turbines, hydroelectric dams

🔧

🔄 Motor

Converts electrical energy into mechanical energy

Electrical ➡️ Mechanical

Principle: Based on Fleming's Motor Rule

Examples: Electric fans, washing machines, electric vehicles

💡
Easy Memory Tip: Generators Generate electricity (mechanical → electrical). Motors Move things (electrical → mechanical). They are essentially opposites!

📜 Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction

⚡ Faraday's Law
Whenever there is a change in the magnetic field linked with a conductor, a potential difference (EMF) is induced, the magnitude of which is directly proportional to the rate of change of the magnetic field linked with the conductor.

Explaining Induced Current Using Faraday's Law

When a magnet moves towards a coil:

  • The magnetic field through the coil increases momentarily
  • This change induces a current in the coil
  • The faster the change, the greater the induced current

When a magnet moves away from a coil:

  • The magnetic field through the coil decreases
  • A current is induced, but in the opposite direction

When there is no relative motion:

  • No current is induced
  • It is the relative motion that matters!
  • The magnet can be held steady and the coil moved – this also induces an EMF
🔑 Key Principle: It doesn't matter whether the magnet moves or the coil moves. What matters is that there is relative motion between them, causing a change in magnetic flux through the coil.

📊 Factors Affecting Induced Current Strength

The strength of the induced current can be increased by:

⚡
Speed of Movement

Increase the speed of movement of the magnet relative to the coil

🧲
Magnetic Strength

Increase the magnetic flux linkage (use a stronger magnet)

🔄
Number of Turns

Increase the number of turns in the coil

📝
Exam Tip: When asked how to increase induced current, mention ALL three factors: faster relative motion, stronger magnet, and more coil turns. Each factor independently increases the induced current!

📋 Key Concepts Summary

🔑 Important Points to Remember:

  • Generators: Convert mechanical energy → electrical energy (Faraday's Law)
  • Motors: Convert electrical energy → mechanical energy (Fleming's Rule)
  • Both work through the interaction of magnetic fields and coils
  • Faraday's Law: A changing magnetic field induces an EMF proportional to the rate of change
  • Relative motion between magnet and coil is essential for induction
  • Induced current direction reverses when the direction of motion reverses

📊 Increasing Induced Current:

  1. Increase speed of relative motion
  2. Use a stronger magnet (increase magnetic flux)
  3. Increase number of turns in the coil
