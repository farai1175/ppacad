📖 Overview

Electrical machines are devices that convert energy between mechanical and electrical forms. They are fundamental to modern technology, powering everything from household appliances to industrial equipment. In this lesson, we'll explore the two main types of electrical machines: generators and motors, and understand the principles that make them work.

💡 Key Concept: Electromagnetism

Electromagnetism is the study of the properties of and relationship between electric currents and magnetic fields. This principle forms the foundation of how all electrical machines operate.

Electromagnetic Induction

  • A current-carrying conductor has a magnetic field induced around it
  • A changing magnetic field can induce an EMF, resulting in the flow of current
  • This current is called an induced current
  • Both generators and motors operate through the interaction between a magnetic field and a set of windings (coils)

Factors That Increase Induced Current Strength

  • 🔄 Increasing the speed of movement of the magnet relative to the coil
  • 🧲 Increasing the magnetic flux linkage (using a stronger magnet)
  • 🔗 Increasing the number of turns in the coil

📜 Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction

Faraday's Law states that whenever there is a change in the magnetic field linked with a conductor, a potential difference (EMF) is induced. The magnitude of this EMF is directly proportional to the rate of change of the magnetic field linked with the conductor.

Applying Faraday's Law to Rotating Coils

Faraday's Law explains why current is induced in a coil rotated in a magnetic field:

  • ✅ A current is induced when a magnet moves toward a coil (magnetic field increases)
  • ✅ The induced current reverses direction when the magnet moves away (magnetic field decreases)
  • ❌ No current is induced if the magnet does not move relative to the coil
  • 💡 It is relative motion that matters — the coil can move while the magnet stays stationary

Essential Definitions

  • Magnetic Field: A region around a magnetic material or moving electrical charge where the force of magnetism acts
  • Solenoid: A large number of insulated turns forming a cylindrical coil
  • Electromagnet: A conductor which becomes magnetic when current flows through it

Factors Affecting Electromagnet Strength

  • ⚡ The current flowing through the conductor
  • 🔄 The number of turns around the core
  • 🔩 The type of substance used in the core
