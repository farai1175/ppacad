The Electromagnetic Spectrum

🔑 Key Concept

Visible light is only one small part of a whole range of possible frequencies and wavelengths of electromagnetic waves. The complete range is called the electromagnetic spectrum.

Type: Radio Waves
Frequency: 10⁴ - 10¹¹ Hz
Wavelength: 10⁻³ - 10⁴ m
Energy: Low
Radio Waves

The Seven Regions of the EM Spectrum

📻 Radio Waves

Frequency: 10⁴ - 10¹¹ Hz
Wavelength: 10⁻³ - 10⁴ m
Uses: Broadcasting, communication

🍽️ Microwaves

Frequency: 10⁹ - 10¹² Hz
Wavelength: 10⁻³ - 0.3 m
Uses: Cooking, radar, satellite

🔥 Infrared

Frequency: 10¹² - 4×10¹⁴ Hz
Wavelength: 7×10⁻⁷ - 10⁻³ m
Uses: Heating, remote controls

👁️ Visible Light

Frequency: 4×10¹⁴ - 8×10¹⁴ Hz
Wavelength: 4×10⁻⁷ - 7×10⁻⁷ m
Colors: ROYGBIV

☀️ Ultraviolet

Frequency: 8×10¹⁴ - 10¹⁷ Hz
Wavelength: 10⁻⁸ - 4×10⁻⁷ m
Effects: Sunburn, vitamin D

🦴 X-rays

Frequency: 10¹⁷ - 10²⁰ Hz
Wavelength: 10⁻¹¹ - 10⁻⁸ m
Uses: Medical imaging

☢️ Gamma Rays

Frequency: >10²⁰ Hz
Wavelength: <10⁻¹¹ m
Sources: Nuclear reactions

⚠️ Important Relationship

As frequency increases, wavelength decreases, and energy increases. This is why gamma rays (high frequency, short wavelength) are extremely harmful, while radio waves (low frequency, long wavelength) are generally safe.

📐

The Wave Equation: c = fλ

📝 Fundamental Equation

Given the wavelength of EM waves, we can calculate the frequency and vice versa using the equation:

📊 Essential Wave Equation

c = f × λ

Speed of light = frequency × wavelength

c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s

Constant speed of EM waves in vacuum

f = c / λ

Frequency from wavelength

λ = c / f

Wavelength from frequency

⚠️ Unit Consistency

Always ensure consistent units:

  • Speed of light (c): m/s
  • Frequency (f): Hz (1/s)
  • Wavelength (λ): meters (m)

Common conversions: 1 MHz = 10⁶ Hz, 1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m

Energy & Harmful Effects

The energy of electromagnetic radiation is directly related to its frequency through the Planck-Einstein relation:

E = hf

Energy = Planck's constant × frequency

📻 Radio Waves

Energy: Very low (10⁻¹⁰ - 10⁻²² J)
Effect: Generally harmless

👁️ Visible Light

Energy: Moderate (10⁻¹⁹ J)
Effect: Can be seen, heating

☀️ Ultraviolet

Energy: High enough to damage cells
Effect: Sunburn, skin cancer risk

☢️ Gamma Rays

Energy: Very high (>10⁻¹⁴ J)
Effect: Ionizing radiation, cell damage

🤔 Why are gamma rays harmful?

According to E = hf, energy is directly proportional to frequency. Gamma rays have very high frequency, which means they have very high energy. This high energy allows them to ionize atoms and damage DNA in cells, making them harmful to humans.

Frequency Comparison

Which has the lower frequency between infrared and X-rays?

Solution:
Understanding the Spectrum

The electromagnetic spectrum in order of increasing frequency:

Radio → Microwaves → Infrared → Visible → Ultraviolet → X-rays → Gamma rays

Comparison

Infrared comes before X-rays in the spectrum, therefore:

Infrared has lower frequency than X-rays

2

Wavelength Comparison

Which has a shorter wavelength between infrared and X-rays?

Solution:
Inverse Relationship

Since c = fλ is constant, frequency and wavelength are inversely proportional:

Higher frequency ↔ Shorter wavelength

Comparison

X-rays have higher frequency than infrared, therefore:

X-rays have shorter wavelength than infrared

Gamma Ray Harmfulness

Energy-Frequency Relationship

From E = hf, energy (E) is directly proportional to frequency (f).

Higher frequency → Higher energy

Gamma rays have the highest frequency in the EM spectrum.

Therefore, they have very high energy.

This high energy allows gamma rays to:

5

Radio Wave Calculation

Calculate the wavelength of Metro FM radio waves of frequency 96.4 MHz.

Solution:
Step 1: Write the equation

c = fλ

Step 2: Rearrange for wavelength

λ = c / f

Step 3: Convert MHz to Hz

96.4 MHz = 96.4 × 10⁶ Hz = 9.64 × 10⁷ Hz

Step 4: Substitute values

λ = (3 × 10⁸ m/s) / (9.64 × 10⁷ Hz)

Step 5: Calculate

λ = 3.11 m

The wavelength of Metro FM radio waves is approximately 3.11 meters.

6

Frequency Calculation

An electromagnetic wave has a wavelength of 986 nm. Find its frequency.

Solution:
Step 1: Write the equation

c = fλ

Step 2: Rearrange for frequency

f = c / λ

Step 3: Convert nm to m

986 nm = 986 × 10⁻⁹ m = 9.86 × 10⁻⁷ m

Step 4: Substitute values

f = (3 × 10⁸ m/s) / (9.86 × 10⁻⁷ m)

Step 5: Calculate

f = 3.04 × 10¹⁴ Hz

The frequency is 3.04 × 10¹⁴ Hz, which is in the visible light range (infrared-visible boundary).
