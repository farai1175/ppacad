📋 Overview & Pre-Knowledge

▼

📌 Pre-Knowledge Required

  • Understanding of internal resistance and its effect on circuits
  • Calculating equivalent resistance for series and parallel networks
  • Using Ohm's Law: V = IR
  • Difference between EMF and terminal potential difference

🔑 Key Concepts Review

EMF (ε): The voltage across a battery when there is no current flowing (switch open).


Terminal Potential Difference (Vload): The voltage across the battery terminals when there is current flowing (switch closed).

ε = Vload + Ir

Therefore: r = (ε - Vload) / I

🧪 Practical Investigations

▼

🔋 Part 1: Determining Internal Resistance of a Battery

Aim: To determine the internal resistance of a battery using voltage and current measurements.

Apparatus Required:
🔋 Battery 🔌 Connecting wires ⚡ Resistor (known value) 📊 Voltmeter 📏 Ammeter 🔘 Switch
⚠️
Safety Warning: The ammeter must be connected in series in the circuit; otherwise its fuse may burn out. Always have your circuit checked before closing the switch.
Method:
1 Connect the resistor in series with the battery, ammeter and switch.
2 Connect the voltmeter across the battery's terminals.
3 Record the voltmeter reading when the switch S is open. This is the EMF (ε).
4 Now close the switch and record the ammeter and voltmeter readings. The voltmeter now shows Vload.
5 Disconnect the circuit and repeat steps 1-4 until you have three sets of measurements.

📝 Questions to Answer:

1. What quantity does the voltmeter measure when the switch is open?
2. What quantity does the voltmeter measure when the switch is closed?
3. Draw up a neat table of measurements made in this experiment.
4. Calculate the battery's internal resistance (r). Show your working.
5. List two sources of error in this experiment.

Expected Answers:

1. The EMF of the battery (ε)

2. The terminal potential difference (Vload)

4. Using r = (ε - Vload) / I

5. Sources of error: Parallax error when reading meters, zero error on meters, heating effects changing resistance

🔗 Part 2: Determining Equivalent Resistance of a Series-Parallel Network

Aim: To determine the equivalent resistance of a series-parallel network experimentally and compare it with the theoretical value.

Apparatus Required:
🔋 Battery 🔌 Connecting wires ⚡ Three resistors (different values) 📊 Voltmeter 📏 Ammeter 🔘 Switch
Method:
1 Record the value of each resistor R₁, R₂ and R₃.
2 Connect the series-parallel network of resistors. Ensure that the circuit is open.
3 Connect the ammeter in series with the network of resistors.
4 Connect the voltmeter across the battery's terminals.
5 Close the switch and record the ammeter and voltmeter readings.
6 Disconnect the circuit and repeat steps 1-5 until you have three sets of measurements.
7 Calculate the average for each measurement.

📝 Questions to Answer:

1. Draw up a neat table of your measurements.
2. Use the ammeter and voltmeter readings to calculate the resistance of the series-parallel network.
3. Calculate the theoretical value of the equivalent resistance.
4. Compare the experimental value (Q2) and theoretical value (Q3).
5. How is the ammeter reading affected when one of the parallel resistors is removed? Explain.

Expected Answers:

2. Rexperimental = V / I

3. For parallel: 1/Rp = 1/R₂ + 1/R₃, then Rtotal = R₁ + Rp

5. Ammeter reading decreases. When a parallel resistor is removed, total resistance increases, which decreases the current in the circuit (I = V/R).
