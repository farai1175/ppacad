Grade 12 Physical Sciences Week 4 Lesson 5 Collisions Explosions

TOPIC: Momentum and Impulse

Lesson 5: Inelastic Collisions & Explosions

Apply conservation of momentum to real-world scenarios including bullets embedding in blocks, car crashes, railway coupling, and rifle recoil.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Apply conservation of momentum when objects stick together after collision
  • Solve problems involving bullets embedding in wooden blocks
  • Calculate velocities in car crash scenarios where vehicles become entangled
  • Understand and apply momentum conservation to explosions (e.g., rifle recoil)
  • Recognize that total momentum before an explosion is zero

📖 Overview

In this lesson, we apply the conservation of momentum to two important types of scenarios:

  1. Inelastic collisions where objects stick together
  2. Explosions where objects separate from rest
Pre-knowledge Required: Conservation of momentum principle

The total linear momentum in an isolated system is constant in both magnitude and direction.

Baseline Questions

Test yourself before continuing:
  1. State the principle of conservation of momentum.
  2. An object of 4 kg moving to the right at 2 m·s⁻¹ collides with a stationary object with a mass of 6 kg. After collision the 4 kg mass moves at 1.5 m·s⁻¹ to the left. Calculate the velocity of the 6 kg after collision.
Answers:
  1. The total linear momentum in an isolated system is constant in both magnitude and direction.
  2. Take right as positive:
    pbefore = pafter
    m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = m₁v₁f + m₂v₂f
    (4)(2) + (6)(0) = (4)(-1.5) + (6)v₂f
    8 + 0 = -6 + 6v₂f
    v₂f = 14/6 = 2.3 m·s⁻¹ to the right

🎯 Types of Momentum Problems

🔗💥

Objects Stick Together

After collision, objects combine into one mass and move together with the same velocity.

m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = (m₁ + m₂)vf

Examples: Bullet in block, car crashes, railway coupling

💥⬅️➡️

Explosions

Objects start at rest (or together) and separate. Total initial momentum is zero!

0 = m₁v₁f + m₂v₂f

Examples: Rifle recoil, fireworks, pushing apart

🔫🎯

Bullet Problems

A bullet embeds in a stationary block. Find initial bullet velocity or final combined velocity.

mbvb + 0 = (mb + mblock)vf

Convert grams to kg! (÷1000)

Comparing Collisions vs Explosions

Property Collision (Stick Together) Explosion
Initial State Objects moving, then combine Objects at rest or together
Final State One combined mass moving Separate objects moving apart
Total p (before) Sum of individual momenta Zero (at rest)
Conservation pbefore = pafter 0 = p₁ + p₂ (opposite momenta)
Kinetic Energy Decreases (lost to deformation) Increases (from chemical/stored energy)
Explosion Key Insight: In an explosion, the total momentum before is ZERO (objects at rest). After the explosion, the momenta of the separating objects are equal in magnitude but opposite in direction, so they still sum to zero!

📚 Worked Examples

1 Worked Example: Railway Trucks Coupling Stick Together
Problem: A railway truck with a mass of 2 × 10³ kg is moving at a speed of 5 m·s⁻¹. It collides with a stationary truck which has a mass of 3 × 10³ kg. If the two trucks couple together during the collision, what is their combined velocity after the collision?
📍 Take direction of moving truck as POSITIVE

Given:
m₁ = 2 × 10³ kg = 2000 kg, v₁ᵢ = +5 m·s⁻¹
m₂ = 3 × 10³ kg = 3000 kg, v₂ᵢ = 0 (stationary)
vf = ? (combined velocity)

Apply conservation of momentum (stick together):
pbefore = pafter
m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = (m₁ + m₂)vf
(2000)(5) + (3000)(0) = (2000 + 3000)vf
10000 + 0 = 5000vf
vf = 10000 / 5000
vf = 2 m·s⁻¹ in the original direction
2 Worked Example: Rifle Recoil (Explosion) Explosion
Problem: A bullet with a mass of 100 g is fired from a rifle. The bullet's velocity as it leaves the barrel of the rifle is 300 m·s⁻¹. If the mass of the rifle is 5 kg, calculate its recoil velocity.
📍 Take bullet's direction as POSITIVE

Key Insight: This is an EXPLOSION! Before firing, total momentum = 0

Given:
mbullet = 100 g = 0.1 kg, vbullet = +300 m·s⁻¹
mrifle = 5 kg, vrifle = ?
pbefore = 0 (system at rest)

Apply conservation of momentum:
pbefore = pafter
0 = mbulletvbullet + mriflevrifle
0 = (0.1)(300) + (5)(vrifle)
0 = 30 + 5vrifle
5vrifle = -30
vrifle = -6 m·s⁻¹
vrifle = 6 m·s⁻¹ in the opposite direction to the bullet
The negative sign indicates: The rifle moves backward (recoils) in the opposite direction to the bullet. This is why you feel a "kick" when firing a gun!
3 Worked Example: Bullet Embedding in Block Stick Together
Problem: A 30 g bullet is shot horizontally with a certain velocity into a wooden block of mass 1.47 kg, which is standing on a smooth surface. After the impact, the bullet gets embedded in the wood and they move off together with a horizontal velocity of 4 m·s⁻¹. Calculate the initial velocity of the bullet.
📍 Take initial bullet velocity direction as POSITIVE

Given:
mbullet = 30 g = 0.03 kg, vbullet = ?
mblock = 1.47 kg, vblock = 0 (stationary)
mcombined = 0.03 + 1.47 = 1.5 kg
vf = 4 m·s⁻¹

Apply conservation of momentum (stick together):
pbefore = pafter
m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = (m₁ + m₂)vf
(0.03)(vbullet) + (1.47)(0) = (1.5)(4)
0.03vbullet = 6
vbullet = 6 / 0.03
vbullet = 200 m·s⁻¹
Unit Check: Always convert grams to kilograms (÷1000) before calculating!
4 Worked Example: Car Collision Stick Together
Problem: The driver of a 1800 kg car stops the car when he sees that the robot (traffic light) he is approaching turns red. A smaller car, whose driver tries to 'beat' the red light, collides into the back of the stationary car and the two cars become entangled during the collision. If the smaller car, of mass 900 kg, was moving at 20 m·s⁻¹ when it hit the stationary car, calculate the velocity of the entangled wreckage after the collision.
📍 Take direction of moving car as POSITIVE

Given:
m₁ = 1800 kg (stationary car), v₁ᵢ = 0
m₂ = 900 kg (moving car), v₂ᵢ = +20 m·s⁻¹
vf = ?

Apply conservation of momentum (stick together):
pbefore = pafter
m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = (m₁ + m₂)vf
(1800)(0) + (900)(20) = (1800 + 900)vf
0 + 18000 = 2700vf
vf = 18000 / 2700
vf = 6.67 m·s⁻¹
5 Worked Example: Railway Coaches Coupling Stick Together
Problem: A railway coach of mass 2 × 10⁴ kg moving at a speed of 4 m·s⁻¹ collides and couples with three other coupled coaches. The three coupled coaches each have the same mass as the single coach and are moving at 3 m·s⁻¹ in the same direction. Calculate the velocity of the coupled coaches after the collision.
📍 Take direction of motion as POSITIVE

Given:
m₁ = 2 × 10⁴ kg = 20000 kg, v₁ᵢ = +4 m·s⁻¹
m₂ = 3 × (2 × 10⁴) = 6 × 10⁴ kg = 60000 kg, v₂ᵢ = +3 m·s⁻¹
Total mass after = 20000 + 60000 = 80000 kg = 8 × 10⁴ kg
vf = ?

Apply conservation of momentum:
pbefore = pafter
m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = (m₁ + m₂)vf
(2 × 10⁴)(4) + (6 × 10⁴)(3) = (8 × 10⁴)vf
80000 + 180000 = 80000vf
260000 = 80000vf
vf = 260000 / 80000
vf = 3.25 m·s⁻¹ in the same direction
6 Worked Example: Rifle Recoil 2 Explosion
Problem: A bullet with a mass of 50 g leaves the barrel of a rifle with a velocity of 400 m·s⁻¹. If the mass of the rifle is 3 kg, calculate the recoil velocity of the rifle.
📍 Take bullet's direction as POSITIVE

Given:
mbullet = 50 g = 0.05 kg, vbullet = +400 m·s⁻¹
mrifle = 3 kg, vrifle = ?
pbefore = 0

Apply conservation of momentum (explosion):
pbefore = pafter
0 = mbulletvbullet + mriflevrifle
0 = (0.05)(400) + (3)(vrifle)
0 = 20 + 3vrifle
3vrifle = -20
vrifle = -6.67 m·s⁻¹
vrifle = 6.67 m·s⁻¹ in the opposite direction to the bullet
