Pre-Knowledge Review

🔥 Exothermic Reaction

A reaction in which more energy is released than absorbed. There is a net release of energy. ΔH is negative.

❄️ Endothermic Reaction

A reaction in which more energy is absorbed than released. There is a net absorption of energy. ΔH is positive.

⚡ Activated Complex

An unstable, high-energy intermediate structure formed during a reaction where bonds are breaking and forming simultaneously.

📊 Concentration

The amount of solute per unit volume of solution. Usually measured in mol·dm⁻³.

📝 Heat of Reaction (ΔH)

ΔH is the net amount of energy absorbed or released during a chemical reaction (unit: kJ·mol⁻¹).

  • ΔH < 0 (negative): Exothermic - energy of products is LESS than reactants
  • ΔH > 0 (positive): Endothermic - energy of products is GREATER than reactants
💥

The Collision Theory

📖 What is the Mechanism of a Reaction?

The mechanism of a reaction refers to the steps that atoms go through as their arrangement changes from reactants to products.

The collision theory is the model used to explain how reactions occur at the microscopic level.

💥 Key Points of Collision Theory

  • Collision: Reacting atoms, molecules, or ions must collide with each other
  • Sufficient Energy: Particles must have enough energy (≥ activation energy) to react
  • Correct Orientation: Particles must be oriented correctly during collision
  • Bond Breaking: Bonds in the original molecules must break
  • Bond Formation: New bonds must form as electrons are rearranged

💡 Not All Collisions Lead to Reactions!

Only collisions with sufficient energy AND correct orientation result in a chemical reaction. These are called effective collisions.

⚡

Activation Energy (Ea)

📖 Definition

Activation energy (Ea) is the minimum amount of energy required for a reaction to occur. It is the difference in energy between the reactants and the activated complex.

Unit: kJ·mol⁻¹

🔄 The Activated Complex

  • All reactions need energy to start - reactants must pass over an "energy hill"
  • The transition state that atoms must pass through is called the activated complex
  • The activated complex is an unstable structure where bonds are forming and breaking simultaneously
  • It exists for only a very brief moment
  • Once formed, it can break down to form products OR reform reactants

⚡ Why Do Reactions Need Activation Energy?

Energy is needed to break existing bonds between atoms. This allows particles to collide with particles of other reactants and form new bonds.

Energy Diagrams

Energy diagrams show how the potential energy of the reacting substances changes during a reaction.

🔥 Exothermic Reaction

  • Energy of products < Energy of reactants
  • Energy is released to surroundings
  • ΔH is negative
  • Products are at a lower energy level
E(products) < E(reactants)

❄️ Endothermic Reaction

  • Energy of products > Energy of reactants
  • Energy is absorbed from surroundings
  • ΔH is positive
  • Products are at a higher energy level
E(products) > E(reactants)

Maxwell-Boltzmann Distribution

📖 What is the Maxwell-Boltzmann Distribution?

The Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution shows the distribution of kinetic energies among molecules in a gas or liquid at a given temperature. It explains why only some molecules have enough energy to react.

Key Features of the Curve:

  • The curve starts at the origin (no molecules have zero energy)
  • Most molecules have energies around the peak (most probable energy)
  • The curve is asymmetric with a long tail to the right (high energies)
  • Only molecules with energy ≥ Ea can react (area under curve to the right of Ea)

Effect of Temperature on Reaction Rate

🌡️ Increasing Temperature

When temperature increases:

  1. Molecules gain more kinetic energy
  2. The Maxwell-Boltzmann curve shifts to the right and flattens
  3. More molecules have energy ≥ Ea
  4. Molecules collide more frequently
  5. Result: Reaction rate INCREASES
Factor Low Temperature High Temperature
Kinetic energy Lower Higher
Collision frequency Fewer collisions More collisions
Molecules with E ≥ Ea Fewer More
Reaction rate Slower Faster
⚡

Catalysts

📖 Definition

A catalyst is a substance that increases the rate of a chemical reaction without being consumed in the reaction. It is chemically unchanged at the end of the reaction.

⚡ How Catalysts Work

  • Catalysts provide an alternative reaction pathway
  • This alternative pathway has a lower activation energy
  • More molecules have sufficient energy to react
  • The catalyst forms part of the activated complex temporarily
  • When the activated complex decomposes, the catalyst is released unchanged

⚠️ Important: What Catalysts DON'T Do

Catalysts cannot:

  • Cause a reaction that would not otherwise occur
  • Change the position of equilibrium
  • Change the heat of reaction (ΔH)
  • Change the products formed

They only affect the rate at which equilibrium is reached.

Types of Catalysis

🧪 Homogeneous Catalysis

The catalyst is in the same phase as the reactants.

Example: H⁺ ions in esterification (all in solution)

🔩 Heterogeneous Catalysis

The catalyst is in a different phase from the reactants.

Example: Iron catalyst in Haber process (solid catalyst, gas reactants)

⬇️ Negative Catalysts (Inhibitors)

A negative catalyst or inhibitor is a substance that decreases the rate of a chemical reaction. It provides a pathway with higher activation energy or blocks the reaction pathway.
