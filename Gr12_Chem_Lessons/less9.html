⚗️ Measuring Rates of Reaction

In this lesson, we explore how to measure the speed at which chemical reactions occur. Understanding reaction rates is essential for controlling industrial processes, understanding biological systems, and predicting chemical behavior.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Define and explain what is meant by "rate of reaction"
  • Identify the different ways of measuring rates of reaction
  • Suggest suitable experimental techniques for measuring reaction rates
  • Measure gas volumes using appropriate apparatus
  • Understand changes in turbidity, colour, and mass as indicators of reaction rate
  • Interpret concentration vs. time graphs for reactions

📋 Pre-Knowledge Check

🔬 What is Reaction Rate?

📖 Definition

Reaction rate is an indication of the chemical change that takes place in a certain time. It measures how quickly reactants are converted into products, or how quickly products are formed.

Rate of Reaction can be expressed as:
Rate = Δ[concentration] / Δt
Change in concentration of reactants or products per unit time
📝 Worked Example: Zinc and Sulfuric Acid Reaction
Zn (s) + H₂SO₄ (aq) → ZnSO₄ (aq) + H₂ (g)

The rate of this reaction can be measured by:

  • Amount of zinc used per minute (mass)
  • Amount of sulfuric acid used per minute (mol)
  • Amount of zinc sulphate produced per minute (mol)
  • Volume of hydrogen gas produced per minute
💡
Key Insight: The rate of reaction is the rate at which the concentration of reactants decreases OR the rate at which the concentration of products increases.

🧪 Methods of Measuring Reaction Rates

Different reactions require different measurement techniques. Here are the main methods:

💨
Gas Volume

Use a gas syringe or inverted measuring cylinder to collect and measure gas produced

⚖️
Mass Change

Place reaction vessel on a balance and measure mass loss as gas escapes

🌫️
Turbidity

Measure time for a precipitate to obscure a mark (e.g., cross under beaker)

🎨
Colour Change

Use a colorimeter or visual observation to track colour intensity changes

🌡️
Temperature

Monitor temperature change in exothermic or endothermic reactions

📊
pH Change

Use a pH meter or indicator to track changes in acidity/alkalinity

✏️ Worked Examples: Choosing Measurement Techniques

Example 1: Comparing Boiling Rates

Question: Which boils faster - tap water or salt water?

Measurement technique: Measure the time until large bubbles form in each sample under identical heating conditions.

Example 2: Electroplating Rate

Question: Compare different concentrations of silver solution used to electroplate a nickel bar.

Measurement technique: Measure the time it takes for the object to be fully coated with a visible layer of silver.

Example 3: Corrosion Rate

Question: Compare the reaction of iron nails with tap water and salt water.

Measurement technique: Measure the time it takes for visible rust to form on each nail.

Example 4: Metal Reactivity with Acid

Question: Compare the reactivity of various metals with hydrochloric acid.

Measurement technique: Measure the rate at which hydrogen gas is produced using a gas syringe.

Example 5: Copper + Nitric Acid (Multiple Indicators)

Reaction: Copper shavings react with concentrated nitric acid (exothermic). Products include NO₂(g) (brown gas) and copper(II) nitrate (blue solution).

Possible measurement techniques:

  • Rate of brown gas formation (gas syringe)
  • Rate of mass decrease (balance)
  • Rate at which copper is used up (mass of copper)
  • Change in colour (blue solution formation)
  • Temperature increase (thermometer)
