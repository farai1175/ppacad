Overview

Acids and bases are fundamental concepts in chemistry that explain many chemical reactions we encounter daily. From the sourness of lemons to the slippery feel of soap, understanding acids and bases helps us comprehend the world around us.

🍋 Properties of Acids

  • Sharp, sour taste
  • pH less than 7
  • Conduct electricity in solution
  • React with metals to produce hydrogen gas

🧼 Properties of Bases

  • Soapy, slippery feel
  • pH greater than 7
  • Bitter taste
  • Conduct electricity in solution

Key Definitions

Ionisation: The process whereby covalent compounds break up into ionic substances for the first time when dissolved in water.
Dissociation: The process whereby ionic compounds break up into their separate ions in the presence of water.
Acid Solution: A solution that has an excess of H⁺ or H₃O⁺ (hydronium) ions.
Base Solution: A solution that has an excess of OH⁻ (hydroxide) ions.
Neutral Solution: A solution with equal concentrations of H⁺ and OH⁻ ions.
Alkali: A soluble base that contains hydroxide (OH⁻) ions which can be released into solution.

Acid-Base Models

There are different ways to describe and understand acids and bases. The two main models are the Arrhenius model and the Lowry-Brønsted model.

Arrhenius Theory (1884)

🔴 Arrhenius Acid

A substance that produces hydrogen ions (H⁺) in solution, which combine with water to form hydronium ions (H₃O⁺).

HCl → H⁺ + Cl⁻
H⁺ + H₂O → H₃O⁺
🔵 Arrhenius Base

A substance that produces hydroxide ions (OH⁻) in solution.

NaOH → Na⁺ + OH⁻

Note: The Arrhenius model is limited to aqueous solutions only.

Lowry-Brønsted Theory (1923)

🔴 Brønsted-Lowry Acid

A proton donor - a substance that can donate a hydrogen ion (H⁺/proton) to another substance.

🔵 Brønsted-Lowry Base

A proton acceptor - a substance that can accept a hydrogen ion (H⁺/proton) from another substance.

HCl + NH₃ ⇌ NH₄⁺ + Cl⁻
(acid) + (base) ⇌ (conjugate acid) + (conjugate base)

The Brønsted-Lowry model is more general and applies to non-aqueous solutions too!

Conjugate Acid-Base Pairs

Conjugate Acid-Base Pairs: Two compounds that differ by the presence of one proton (H⁺). When an acid donates a proton, it becomes its conjugate base. When a base accepts a proton, it becomes its conjugate acid.
Protolytic Reactions: Acid-base reactions involving the transfer of protons. These reactions undergo protolysis.

Understanding the Relationship:

Acid ⇌ Conjugate Base + H⁺

Base + H⁺ ⇌ Conjugate Acid
📝 Worked Example 1

Question: Indicate the conjugate acid-base pairs when HCl is dissolved in water.

HCl(g) + H₂O(l) ⇌ H₃O⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq)
Solution:

Pair 1:
• Acid: HCl
• Conjugate Base: Cl⁻ (HCl loses a proton)

Pair 2:
• Base: H₂O
• Conjugate Acid: H₃O⁺ (H₂O gains a proton)
📝 Worked Example 2

Question: What is the conjugate base of HNO₃?

Solution: NO₃⁻
When HNO₃ donates a proton (H⁺), it becomes NO₃⁻
📝 Worked Example 3

Question: What is the conjugate acid of HSO₄⁻?

Solution: H₂SO₄
When HSO₄⁻ accepts a proton (H⁺), it becomes H₂SO₄
