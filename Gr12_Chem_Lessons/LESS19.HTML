Overview - Key Definitions

▼

🔑 Baseline Knowledge Review

Before we begin, recall these important definitions:

🔴 Arrhenius Definition of Acid

Acids are substances that produce H+ ions in solution.

🔵 Arrhenius Definition of Base

Bases are substances that produce OH- ions in solution.

🔴 Lowry-Brønsted Definition of Acid

Acids are proton donors (donate H+).

🔵 Lowry-Brønsted Definition of Base

Bases are proton acceptors (accept H+).

In this lesson, we will explore the differences between strong and weak acids and bases, as well as the difference between concentrated and dilute solutions. These terms have very different meanings and must not be confused!

📝

Main Content: Acid-Base Terminology

▼

⚠️ Important Distinction

The terms 'strong' and 'concentrated', as well as 'weak' and 'dilute' have different meanings and must not be confused when classifying acids and bases!

🔴 Strong Acids

Definition

A strong acid is an acid that ionises completely in water to produce a large amount of H3O+ ions in solution.

HCl + H2O → H3O+ + Cl-

This reaction is technically reversible, but the strong acid ionises so well that we can think of the reaction as almost one-way. Almost 100% of the HCl molecules ionise, meaning that the forward reaction is very much favoured, resulting in many H3O+ ions in solution.

Table 1: Common Strong Acids
Formula Name of Strong Acid
HClHydrochloric acid
HNO3Nitric acid
H2SO4Sulphuric acid
HBrHydrobromic acid
HIHydroiodic acid
HClO4Perchloric acid

🟡 Weak Acids

Definition

A weak acid is one which ionises partially in water to form H3O+ ions.

CH3COOH + H2O ⇌ CH3COO- + H3O+

Ionisation of weak acids reaches equilibrium with the equilibrium lying far to the left. Very few of the CH3COOH molecules are ionised.

📝 Remember

Any acid NOT listed in Table 1 is considered a weak acid. Examples include: acetic acid (CH3COOH), carbonic acid (H2CO3), phosphoric acid (H3PO4).

🔵 Strong Bases

Definition

A strong base is a base that dissociates completely in water to form OH- ions.

NaOH → Na+ + OH-

Almost 100% of the NaOH molecules dissociate, meaning that the forward reaction is favoured, resulting in many OH- ions in solution.

Table 2: Common Strong Bases
Formula Name of Strong Base
KOHPotassium hydroxide
NaOHSodium hydroxide
LiOHLithium hydroxide
RbOHRubidium hydroxide
CsOHCaesium hydroxide

🩵 Weak Bases

Definition

A weak base is one which dissociates partially in water to form OH- ions.

NH3 + H2O ⇌ NH4+ + OH-

Dissociation of weak bases reaches equilibrium with the equilibrium lying far to the left. Very few of the NH3 molecules are dissociated.

📝 Remember

Any base NOT listed in Table 2 is considered a weak base. Examples include: ammonia (NH3), magnesium hydroxide (Mg(OH)2), calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)2).

📊 Concentration of Acids and Bases

Definition

The concentration of an acid or base is an indication of how many acid or base molecules there are per unit volume in the liquid.

🔥 Concentrated

Usually in excess of 4-5 mol·dm-3

Many molecules per unit volume

💧 Dilute

Usually less than 2 mol·dm-3

Fewer molecules per unit volume

💡 Key Example

Consider two samples of HCl:

  • 1 mol·dm-3 HCl → Strong acid, but DILUTE
  • 8 mol·dm-3 HCl → Strong acid, and CONCENTRATED

Both are strong acids (100% ionisation), but they have different concentrations!

⚡ Conductivity and Reactivity

The greater the number of ions in solution, the better the conductivity of a solution.

💪 Strong Acids/Bases

High degree of ionisation/dissociation

→ More ions

→ Better conductivity

→ Faster reactions

😐 Weak Acids/Bases

Low degree of ionisation/dissociation

→ Fewer ions

→ Poorer conductivity

→ Slower reactions

⚡ Reactivity Summary

A strong acid reacts faster than a weak acid of the same concentration because the strong acid has more ions present in solution due to its high degree of ionisation.


Similarly, a strong base reacts faster than a weak base of the same concentration because the strong base has more ions present in solution due to its high degree of dissociation.
