Lesson Overview

Chemical equilibrium is a fundamental concept in chemistry that describes the state of a reversible reaction when the rates of the forward and reverse reactions are equal. In this lesson, we will explore the foundational concepts necessary to understand equilibrium, including the difference between open and closed systems, the nature of reversible reactions, and what it means for a system to be in dynamic equilibrium.

📝 Pre-Knowledge Required:
  • Rates of reaction
  • Factors affecting reaction rates (surface area, concentration, pressure, temperature, catalysts)
  • Nature of reactants
💡

Main Concepts & Principles

🔓 Open System

A system where matter can enter or leave the system freely.

Example: A pot of boiling water without a lid - water vapor escapes into the atmosphere.

🔒 Closed System

A system that is covered - no particles can enter or leave the system.

Example: A sealed container with reactants - all products remain inside.

⚠️ Important: For chemical equilibrium to be established and maintained, the reaction must occur in a closed system. If it's an open system, gaseous products may escape, preventing equilibrium from being reached.

⟷ Reversible Reactions

Many chemical reactions are reversible in closed systems. Reversible reactions do not go to completion - they occur in both the forward and reverse directions.

H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g)
  • The double arrow (⇌) indicates a reversible reaction
  • Reactants form products (forward reaction)
  • Products react to reform the original reactants (reverse reaction)
  • Hydrogen iodide (HI) is not very stable and dissociates back into hydrogen and iodine

⚖️ Types of Equilibrium

Static Equilibrium Dynamic Equilibrium
Reaction stops completely Both forward and reverse reactions continue
No further change occurs Changes occur, but at equal rates
No molecular activity Constant molecular activity
Example: A book on a table Example: Evaporation-condensation equilibrium

🔄 Dynamic Equilibrium

Definition: Dynamic equilibrium occurs when two opposing reactions happen simultaneously and at the same rate, resulting in no visible or net change in the system.

Dynamic Phase Equilibrium Example:

H₂O(l) ⇌ H₂O(g)
  • Rate of evaporation = Rate of condensation
  • The equilibrium is represented by ⇌
  • No net change in the amount of liquid or vapor

📋 Factors Affecting the Position of Equilibrium

🌡️ Temperature

Changes in temperature affect the equilibrium position by favoring either the endothermic or exothermic reaction.

📊 Concentration

Changing the concentration of reactants or products shifts the equilibrium to counteract the change.

💨 Pressure

For reactions involving gases, changing pressure affects the equilibrium position based on moles of gas.

⚗️ Catalyst

A catalyst speeds up both forward and reverse reactions equally but does not change the equilibrium position.

📝 Worked Example: Sodium Chloride Equilibrium

A saturated solution of sodium chloride in water is prepared. NaCl is dissolved in water until a small amount remains at the bottom. The following dynamic equilibrium applies:

NaCl(aq) ⇌ Na⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq)

Q1: What is the meaning of the double arrows in the reaction?

✓ The double arrow indicates that dynamic equilibrium has been reached.

Q2: Explain what is meant by "dynamic equilibrium"?

✓ Two opposing reactions happen simultaneously and at the same rate, thus equally so fast that no external changes can be noticed.

Q3: Compare the concentration of sodium ion and chloride ion at equilibrium.

✓ [Na⁺] = [Cl⁻] (They are equal in concentration)

Q4: How do you know equilibrium has been reached?

✓ The amount of NaCl not dissolved at the bottom remains the same (constant).
