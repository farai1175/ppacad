Factors Affecting Chemical Equilibrium & Industrial Applications

Overview

In this lesson, we explore how the principles of chemical equilibrium are applied in industrial processes. We will examine how factors such as temperature, pressure, concentration, and catalysts are manipulated to optimize production in processes like the Haber Process (ammonia synthesis) and the Contact Process (sulphuric acid production).

🔑 Pre-knowledge Required: Reversible reactions, chemical equilibrium concepts, endothermic and exothermic reactions.
❓

Baseline Assessment

Consider the following reaction:

N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇌ 2NH3(g)    ΔH < 0

🔥 Forward Reaction

Since ΔH < 0 (negative), the forward reaction is EXOTHERMIC

Energy is released when ammonia forms.

❄️ Reverse Reaction

The reverse reaction is ENDOTHERMIC

Energy is absorbed when ammonia decomposes.

🏭

The Haber Process

Industrial preparation of ammonia (NH₃)

N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇌ 2NH3(g)    ΔH = -92.4 kJ·mol-1
⚙️ Industrial Conditions: 150 – 250 atm pressure; 300°C – 550°C temperature

Problems and Solutions in the Haber Process

⏱️ Rate of Reaction

  • At room temperature, reaction is very slow
  • Increasing temperature speeds up the reaction rate

⚖️ Chemical Equilibrium

  • Forward reaction is exothermic
  • Increasing temperature decreases ammonia formation
  • Lower temperature produces more ammonia but slows rate

🧪 Catalyst

  • Iron catalyst used to increase reaction rate
  • Requires temperature of ~400°C to be effective
  • Does NOT change equilibrium position

📊 Pressure Effects

  • High pressure favors ammonia formation
  • Extreme conditions (200 atm) pose safety risks
  • High cost to strengthen pipes and vessels
🎯 Optimized Yield Strategy
  • N₂ and H₂ constantly added to vessel (increases concentration)
  • Temperature maintained between 300°C and 500°C
  • Pressurized to approximately 200 atm
  • Ammonia liquefied and removed (decreases product concentration)
  • Result: Yield of approximately 15-20% per pass (but continuous production)
🧪

The Contact Process

Industrial preparation of sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄)

🔬 Critical Step
2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2SO3(g)

⚙️ Most Economic Conditions

  • Temperature: 450°C
  • Modest pressure: 1-2 atm
  • Catalyst: Vanadium pentoxide (V₂O₅)

📝 Important Note

  • The catalyst speeds up the reaction
  • Catalyst has NO effect on amount of SO₃ produced
  • Only affects how quickly equilibrium is reached
