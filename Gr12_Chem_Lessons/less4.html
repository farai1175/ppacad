Organic Chemistry: Types of Organic Molecules

📖 Lesson 4
⏱️ 45 minutes
🧪 Chemistry
🎯

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Identify different types of organic molecules (alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, haloalkanes)
  • Explain the terms: functional group, hydrocarbons, saturated, unsaturated
  • Understand the concept of a homologous series
  • Distinguish between different types of dienes (conjugated, cumulated, isolated)
  • Recognize cyclic structures (cycloalkanes and cycloalkenes)
  • Understand the properties and uses of alkyl halides
📖

Key Definitions

📖 Functional Group

A functional group is an atom or group of atoms which gives a compound its particular characteristics and determines its chemical properties.

📖 Hydrocarbons

Hydrocarbons are organic compounds containing only carbon (C) and hydrogen (H) atoms.

📖 Homologous Series

A homologous series is a family of compounds with the same functional group, the same general formula, and similar chemical properties. Each member differs from the next by a CH₂ unit.

✅ Saturated

Compounds where all carbon atoms are joined by single bonds only. No more atoms can be added to the molecule. Example: Alkanes

⚡ Unsaturated

Compounds containing double or triple bonds between carbon atoms. More atoms can be added during reactions. Examples: Alkenes, Alkynes

🔗

Alkanes (Saturated Hydrocarbons)

⛽ Alkanes

CnH2n+2
Single Bonds (C-C) Saturated

Alkanes are non-polar molecules containing only carbon and hydrogen atoms. They are the simplest hydrocarbons with only single bonds between carbon atoms.

Key Facts:
  • Most important fuels (fossil fuels)
  • Boiling points increase with molecular mass
  • Saturated - no more atoms can be added

Examples of Alkanes

Name Molecular Formula Structural Formula Carbon Atoms
Methane CH₄ CH₄ 1
Ethane C₂H₆ CH₃-CH₃ 2
Propane C₃H₈ CH₃-CH₂-CH₃ 3
Butane C₄H₁₀ CH₃-CH₂-CH₂-CH₃ 4
⭕

Cycloalkanes

🔄 Cycloalkanes

CnH2n
Single Bonds (Ring) Saturated

Cycloalkanes are hydrocarbons with single bonds that form a ring structure. They have similar physical and chemical properties as alkanes, but with higher melting and boiling points.

Naming: Same as alkanes, but add the prefix cyclo-
Example: Cyclobutane, Cyclohexane
Cyclobutane: CH₂ - CH₂ | | CH₂ - CH₂
⚡

Alkenes (Unsaturated Hydrocarbons)

🔌 Alkenes

CnH2n
Double Bond (C=C) Unsaturated

Alkenes are non-polar hydrocarbons containing at least one double bond between carbon atoms. They are unsaturated because more atoms can be added during reactions.

Key Facts:
  • The simplest alkene is ethene (C₂H₄)
  • Boiling points increase with molecular mass
  • More reactive than alkanes due to double bond

Structure of Ethene

H H \ / C = C / \ H H or: CH₂=CH₂
🔀

Dienes

📖 Definition

A diene is a compound that contains two double bonds. There are three types based on the arrangement of double bonds.

Conjugated Diene
C=C-C=C

Double bonds are separated by a single bond

Cumulated Diene
C=C=C

Two double bonds form to one carbon atom

Isolated Diene
C=C-C-C=C

One or more saturated carbons between double bonds

🔥

Alkynes

🔥 Alkynes

CnH2n-2
Triple Bond (C≡C) Unsaturated

Alkynes are hydrocarbons containing at least one carbon-carbon triple bond. They are highly reactive.

Key Facts:
  • Simplest member: Ethyne (C₂H₂) - also known as acetylene
  • Combustion is strongly exothermic with extremely hot flame
  • Used in oxyacetylene torches for welding metals

Structure of Ethyne (Acetylene)

H - C ≡ C - H Molecular formula: C₂H₂

🔥 Important Application

The combustion of ethyne (acetylene) produces an extremely hot flame, making it ideal for welding and cutting metals in oxyacetylene torches.

🧪

Alkyl Halides (Haloalkanes)

🧪 Haloalkanes

CnH2n+1X

Where X = F, Cl, Br, or I (halogen)

Haloalkanes have the formula R-X, where R is an alkyl group and X is a halogen atom. They are generally volatile liquids that do not mix with water.

Common Haloalkanes and Their Uses

Compound IUPAC Name Formula Use
Carbon tetrachloride Tetrachloromethane CCl₄ Dry-cleaning solvent (historical)
Chloroform Trichloromethane CHCl₃ Solvent, formerly anesthetic
Freon-12 Dichlorodifluoromethane CCl₂F₂ Refrigerant, aerosol propellant (banned)

Polymers from Haloalkenes

PVC (Polyvinyl Chloride)

Made from chloroethene (vinyl chloride). Used in pipes, cables, flooring, and many domestic products.

PTFE (Teflon)

Poly(tetrafluoroethene). Known for non-stick properties. Used in cookware and as a lubricant.

⚠️ Environmental Concern: CFCs

CFCs (Chlorofluorocarbons) such as dichlorodifluoromethane were used as refrigerants, aerosol propellants, and blowing agents. However, they are very stable molecules that can move to the upper atmosphere and break down ozone molecules, damaging the ozone layer. Their use is now banned or restricted.
