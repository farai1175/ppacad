Momentum and Impulse

Understanding momentum calculations and changes in momentum

Lesson Overview

+

This lesson introduces the concept of momentum, a fundamental quantity in physics that describes the motion of objects. We will explore how to calculate momentum and changes in momentum, with practical examples and problem-solving exercises.

Key Formula

p = m × v

Where:

  • p = momentum (kg•m•s⁻¹)
  • m = mass (kg)
  • v = velocity (m•s⁻¹)

Main Body

+

Introduction to Momentum

Momentum is defined as the product of an object's mass and its velocity. It is a vector quantity, meaning it has both magnitude and direction.

Momentum (p) = mass (m) × velocity (v)
Unit: kg•m•s⁻¹

Since velocity is a vector, momentum is also a vector. This means direction matters when calculating momentum.

Change in Momentum

When a net force acts on an object, its velocity changes, and therefore its momentum changes. The change in momentum (Δp) is calculated as:

Δp = m × Δv = m × (vf - vi)

Where:

  • Δp = change in momentum
  • m = mass
  • vf = final velocity
  • vi = initial velocity

Worked Examples

Example 1: Calculating Momentum

A 5000 kg bus moves due west at 10 m•s⁻¹. Find its momentum.

Solution:

p = m × v = 5000 × 10 = 50,000 kg•m•s⁻¹ west

Example 2: Change in Momentum

A batsman hits a 160 g cricket ball approaching him at 30 m•s⁻¹ so that it moves in the opposite direction at 40 m•s⁻¹. Calculate the change in momentum of the ball.

Solution:

Take approach as positive:

m = 0.16 kg, vi = 30 m•s⁻¹, vf = -40 m•s⁻¹

Δp = m(vf - vi) = 0.16(-40 - 30) = -11.2 kg•m•s⁻¹

Δp = 11.2 kg•m•s⁻¹ away from the batsman
