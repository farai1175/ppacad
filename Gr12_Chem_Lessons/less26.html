In this lesson, we explore galvanic cells - devices that convert chemical energy into electrical energy through spontaneous redox reactions. You'll learn about the components of a galvanic cell, the role of the anode and cathode, and how the salt bridge maintains electrical neutrality.

📚 Main Content: Galvanic Cells ▼

What is a Galvanic Cell?

📝 Definition

A galvanic cell is an electrochemical cell that converts chemical energy to electrical energy through spontaneous redox reactions.

Components of a Galvanic Cell

A simple galvanic cell consists of:

  • Two half cells - Each containing a metal electrode in contact with a solution containing that metal's ions
  • Electrodes - Solid electric conductors through which electric current enters or leaves the cell
  • Electrolyte - The solution in each half cell containing metal ions
  • Conducting wires - Connect the two electrodes to form the external circuit
  • Salt bridge - Connects the two half cells internally

Anode and Cathode

⚡ Key Concepts
  • Anode: The electrode at which oxidation takes place (loses electrons)
  • Cathode: The electrode at which reduction takes place (gains electrons)

Remember: AN OX (Anode = Oxidation) and RED CAT (Reduction = Cathode)

  • The anode becomes the negative electrode as electrons are released due to oxidation
  • The cathode becomes the positive electrode as it attracts electrons
  • Current flow is always from anode to cathode (negative to positive)

The Salt Bridge

The salt bridge consists of a glass tube filled with a saturated solution of KCl, NaCl, KNO₃ or Na₂SO₄.

🔗 Functions of the Salt Bridge
  • Completes the circuit (allows ion flow)
  • Separates the electrolytes
  • Maintains ionic neutrality in each half cell

The Zinc-Copper Cell

🔬 Worked Example: Zn-Cu Galvanic Cell
Property Zinc (Anode) Copper (Cathode)
Reaction Type Oxidation Reduction
Half Reaction Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu
Electrode Charge Negative (excess electrons) Positive (electrons removed)
Mass Change Decreases (Zn dissolves) Increases (Cu deposits)
Overall Reaction: Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu
