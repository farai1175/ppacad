Understanding the difference between elastic and inelastic collisions through kinetic energy analysis

Lesson Overview

+

This lesson explores the fundamental differences between elastic and inelastic collisions. We'll learn how to determine collision types by analyzing kinetic energy conservation and understand what happens to "lost" energy in inelastic collisions.

Key Formulas

Kinetic Energy: Ek = ½mv²
Momentum Conservation: m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = m₁v₁f + m₂v₂f

Unit of Kinetic Energy: Joules (J)

Elastic Collisions

In elastic collisions, both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved. This means:

  • Total kinetic energy before collision = Total kinetic energy after collision
  • Total momentum before collision = Total momentum after collision
  • Objects bounce off each other

Elastic collisions are rare in everyday life but common at atomic and molecular levels.

Inelastic Collisions

In inelastic collisions, momentum is conserved but kinetic energy is not. This means:

  • Total momentum before collision = Total momentum after collision
  • Total kinetic energy before collision ≠ Total kinetic energy after collision
  • The "lost" kinetic energy transforms into other forms like heat, sound, or deformation
  • Objects may stick together (perfectly inelastic) or separate

Most real-world collisions are inelastic to some degree.

Worked Examples

Problem 1: Vehicle Collision

At rush hour in Johannesburg, vehicle A (2000 kg) stopped as the robot turned red. Vehicle B (2400 kg) speeding behind A was unable to stop and crashed into the back of A at 12 m/s. Vehicle A was pushed into the intersection and B slowed down to 5 m/s.

a) Assume that momentum was conserved and calculate the speed of A immediately after the collision.

b) Determine by calculation whether this was an elastic or inelastic collision.

Interactive Calculator for Problem 1

Calculate the speed of vehicle A after collision

Using conservation of momentum:

m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = m₁v₁f + m₂v₂f

(2000 × 0) + (2400 × 12) = 2000v₁f + (2400 × 5)

0 + 28800 = 2000v₁f + 12000

2000v₁f = 16800

v₁f = 8.4 m/s

Vehicle A moves at 8.4 m/s after the collision.

Determine if the collision is elastic or inelastic

Calculate kinetic energy before collision:

Ek before = ½(2000)(0)² + ½(2400)(12)²

Ek before = 0 + ½(2400)(144) = 172800 J

Calculate kinetic energy after collision:

Ek after = ½(2000)(8.4)² + ½(2400)(5)²

Ek after = ½(2000)(70.56) + ½(2400)(25)

Ek after = 70560 + 30000 = 100560 J

Since Ek before (172800 J) ≠ Ek after (100560 J), the collision is inelastic.

Problem 2: Toy Car and Lorry

A toy car of mass 0.5 kg travelling at 2 m/s collides with a stationary lorry of mass 0.8 kg. The lorry is pushed ahead at 0.5 m/s. What is the final velocity of the car? Prove that the collision is inelastic.

Interactive Calculator for Problem 2

Calculate the final velocity of the car

Using conservation of momentum:

m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = m₁v₁f + m₂v₂f

(0.5 × 2) + (0.8 × 0) = 0.5v₁f + (0.8 × 0.5)

1 + 0 = 0.5v₁f + 0.4

0.5v₁f = 0.6

v₁f = 1.2 m/s

The car moves at 1.2 m/s after the collision.

Prove the collision is inelastic

Calculate kinetic energy before collision:

Ek before = ½(0.5)(2)² + ½(0.8)(0)²

Ek before = ½(0.5)(4) + 0 = 1 J

Calculate kinetic energy after collision:

Ek after = ½(0.5)(1.2)² + ½(0.8)(0.5)²

Ek after = ½(0.5)(1.44) + ½(0.8)(0.25)

Ek after = 0.36 + 0.1 = 0.46 J

Since Ek before (1 J) ≠ Ek after (0.46 J), the collision is inelastic.

Problem 3: Bullet and Wooden Block

A bullet of mass 2 g is fired horizontally into a stationary wooden block of mass 1 kg. The bullet strikes the wooden block at a velocity of 490 m/s. The impact causes the block-bullet system to slide a distance of 20 cm before coming to rest.

a) Name and state the law that can be used to calculate the velocity of the bullet-block system after collision.

b) Calculate the velocity of the block-bullet system immediately after collision.

c) Is this collision elastic or inelastic?

Interactive Calculator for Problem 3

Name and state the applicable law

Law of Conservation of Momentum

The total linear momentum of an isolated system is constant in both magnitude and direction.

Calculate the velocity after collision

Using conservation of momentum:

m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = (m₁ + m₂)vf

(0.002 × 490) + (1 × 0) = (0.002 + 1)vf

0.98 + 0 = 1.002vf

vf = 0.98 / 1.002 ≈ 0.978 m/s

The block-bullet system moves at approximately 0.978 m/s after the collision.

Determine if the collision is elastic or inelastic

Calculate kinetic energy before collision:

Ek before = ½(0.002)(490)² + ½(1)(0)²

Ek before = ½(0.002)(240100) + 0 = 240.1 J

Calculate kinetic energy after collision:

Ek after = ½(1.002)(0.978)²

Ek after = ½(1.002)(0.956) ≈ 0.479 J

Since Ek before (240.1 J) ≠ Ek after (0.479 J), the collision is inelastic.

Most of the kinetic energy is transformed into heat, sound, and deformation of the block.
