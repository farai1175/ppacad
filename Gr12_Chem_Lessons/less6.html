Organic Chemistry: Hydrocarbon Derivatives & Benzene

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Identify the functional groups of alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, and esters
  • Classify alcohols as primary, secondary, or tertiary
  • Understand the properties of each homologous series
  • Explain how esters are formed (esterification)
  • Draw and name benzene ring structures
  • Identify common aromatic compounds
🍷

Alcohols

🍷 Alcohols

-OH
Suffix: -ol
General Formula: CnH2n+1OH

Alcohols contain at least one hydroxyl (-OH) group. They form hydrogen bonds between molecules, resulting in higher melting and boiling points than similar-sized hydrocarbons.

Examples:
  • Methanol (CH₃OH) - methyl alcohol, simplest alcohol
  • Ethanol (C₂H₅OH) - product of fermentation, used in alcoholic beverages

Classification of Alcohols

Alcohols are classified according to the position of the hydroxyl group on the carbon chain:

Primary Alcohol (1°)
H | R-C-OH | H

The -OH is attached to a carbon bonded to only one other carbon

Secondary Alcohol (2°)
R | R-C-OH | H

The -OH is attached to a carbon bonded to two other carbons

Tertiary Alcohol (3°)
R | R-C-OH | R

The -OH is attached to a carbon bonded to three other carbons

Aldehydes and Ketones

🔗 Common Feature: Carbonyl Group

Both aldehydes and ketones contain the carbonyl functional group (C=O). The difference is in the position of this group.

🧪 Aldehydes

-CHO
Suffix: -al

The carbonyl group is at the end of the carbon chain, with a hydrogen atom bonded to it.

Key Facts:
  • More reactive than ketones
  • Simplest: Methanal (HCHO) - formaldehyde
  • Contribute to smells of foods and plants

⚗️ Ketones

>C=O
Suffix: -one

The carbonyl group is in the middle of the carbon chain, bonded to carbons on both sides.

Key Facts:
  • Less reactive than aldehydes
  • Simplest: Propanone (CH₃COCH₃) - acetone
  • Used as solvents, nail polish remover

Structural Comparison

Aldehyde (Ethanal): Ketone (Propanone): O O ‖ ‖ H-C-CH₃ CH₃-C-CH₃ (C=O at end) (C=O in middle)

Carboxylic Acids

🍋 Carboxylic Acids

-COOH
Suffix: -oic acid
General Formula: CnH2n+1COOH

All organic acids contain the carboxyl group (-COOH), which combines a carbonyl and hydroxyl group.

Common Carboxylic Acids

Name Formula Found In / Use
Methanoic acid HCOOH Stinging nettles, ant bites
Ethanoic acid CH₃COOH Vinegar (sour component)
Butanoic acid C₃H₇COOH Rancid butter (unpleasant smell)

Esters

🌸 Esters

-COO-
Suffix: -oate

Esters have pleasant smells and are largely responsible for the flavours and scents of fruits and flowers.

🧪 Esterification Reaction

Esters are formed when an alcohol reacts with a carboxylic acid in the presence of an acid catalyst (usually concentrated sulfuric acid). Water is also produced.

📝 Naming Esters

The name of an ester has two parts:

  1. First part: From the parent alcohol (e.g., ethyl from ethanol)
  2. Second part: From the parent carboxylic acid, ending in -oate (e.g., ethanoate from ethanoic acid)

Example: Methanol + Propanoic acid → Methyl propanoate

Benzene and Aromatic Compounds

💍 Benzene (C₆H₆)

C₆H₆

Benzene is an aromatic hydrocarbon with a six-membered ring containing alternating double bonds. It's an excellent organic solvent but does not mix with water.

🔬 Structure of Benzene

Benzene has a unique structure:

  • All six carbon-carbon bonds are the same length
  • The molecule is planar (flat)
  • Contains conjugated double bonds
  • More stable than expected (does not undergo typical addition reactions like alkenes)

Key Facts About Benzene

🏭 Production

Made from oil by catalytic reforming using platinum or molybdenum(IV) oxide catalysts.

⛽ Use in Fuel

Added to unleaded petrol to boost the octane rating.

⚗️ Stability

Extra stable due to resonance - electrons are delocalized around the ring.

Naming Benzene Compounds

When naming substituted benzene compounds:

  • Number the carbon atoms in the ring (1-6)
  • Give substituents the lowest possible numbers
  • List substituents in alphabetical order
  • Use prefixes: di- (2), tri- (3), tetra- (4) for multiple same groups

Examples

Structure Description IUPAC Name
Benzene + methyl on C1 + ethyl on C2 1-methyl-2-ethylbenzene
Benzene + two methyl groups Dimethylbenzene (xylene)
Benzene + propyl group Propylbenzene
Benzene + methyl on C1 + methyl on C3 1,3-dimethylbenzene
Benzene + bromo on C2 + methyl on C1 and C3 2-bromo-1,3-dimethylbenzene
