Oxidation Numbers Revision

▼

📚 Balancing Redox Equations - Ion-Electron Method

  1. Write out the equation in ionic form
  2. Assign oxidation numbers to each atom
  3. Identify changes in oxidation number (oxidation or reduction)
  4. Multiply half reactions to balance electrons transferred
  5. Recombine ions to get the final balanced equation
  6. Check that all atoms are balanced
Rule Oxidation Number
Elements in their natural state 0
Monoatomic ions Charge of the ion
Hydrogen (in most compounds) +1
Oxygen (in most compounds) -2
Sum of oxidation numbers = Charge of species

Extraction of Aluminium

▼

⚙️ The Hall-Héroult Process

Aluminium is extracted from bauxite (aluminium oxide, Al₂O₃). South Africa imports bauxite from Australia as it is not found naturally here.

Molten Al₂O₃/Cryolite

Carbon anodes (top) | Carbon cathode lining (bottom) | Molten aluminium collects at bottom

🔧 Cell Components

  • Carbon anodes - Oxidation occurs here, produce CO₂
  • Carbon cathode lining - As Al collects, it becomes the cathode
  • Cryolite (Na₃AlF₆) - Lowers melting point of Al₂O₃ from 2050°C to ~950°C
  • Molten aluminium - Denser than electrolyte, sinks to bottom

⚗️ Electrode Reactions

CATHODE (Reduction)

Al³⁺(aq) + 3e⁻ → Al(s)

Aluminium ions gain electrons

ANODE (Oxidation)

2O²⁻(aq) → O₂(g) + 4e⁻

Oxide ions lose electrons

💰 Economic & Environmental Considerations

⚡

High energy usage
Requires 3-5 V and up to 100,000 A

💵

Expensive electricity
Major operational cost

🔄

Anode replacement
Carbon reacts with O₂ forming CO₂

🧪

Cryolite production
High manufacturing costs

⚠️ Why Anodes Need Regular Replacement

The carbon anodes react with the oxygen gas produced, forming carbon dioxide (CO₂). This causes the electrodes to gradually disintegrate and need replacement.

The Chlor-Alkali Industry

▼

🌊 The Membrane Cell Process

The membrane cell is the most widely used electrolytic cell in the chlor-alkali industry. It electrolyses brine (concentrated NaCl solution) to produce chlorine, hydrogen, and sodium hydroxide.

📚 Key Definition

Brine: A concentrated solution of sodium chloride (NaCl) in water.

Cl₂ gas

H₂ gas

Anode (left) | Ion-Selective Membrane (center) | Cathode (right)

🔶 Function of the Ion-Selective Membrane

The membrane allows only Na⁺ ions to pass through to the cathode compartment. This keeps the products separated and allows NaOH to form in the cathode compartment.

⚗️ Electrode Reactions

ANODE (Oxidation)

2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻

Chloride ions lose electrons

CATHODE (Reduction)

2H₂O + 2e⁻ → H₂ + 2OH⁻

Water molecules gain electrons

📝 Products of the Chlor-Alkali Industry

  • Chlorine gas (Cl₂) - Produced at the anode
  • Hydrogen gas (H₂) - Produced at the cathode
  • Sodium hydroxide (NaOH) - Forms in the cathode compartment

⚠️ Environmental & Safety Risks

🚨 Dangers of Products

  • Hydrogen gas (H₂) - Highly flammable, can react explosively
  • Chlorine gas (Cl₂) - Highly toxic, causes severe lung damage
  • Sodium hydroxide (NaOH) - Highly corrosive and toxic
  • H₂ + Cl₂ mixture - React explosively when in contact - major safety concern!

📝 Question 1: Membrane Cell

Consider the membrane cell used in the chlor-alkali industry:

  1. Electrode D is positively charged. State the name for this electrode and the type of half reaction that takes place here.
  2. Write down the half reaction that takes place at electrode D.
  3. Write down the half reaction that takes place at the cathode.
  4. State the function of the membrane.
  5. State the main safety risk of the chlor-alkali industry.
✅ Solutions:
  1. Electrode: Anode | Reaction: Oxidation
  2. 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻
  3. 2H₂O + 2e⁻ → H₂ + 2OH⁻
  4. To allow only the passage of Na⁺(aq) ions into the cathode compartment
  5. H₂ gas and Cl₂ are produced no matter what cell is used. These gases react explosively when they come into contact with each other – hence a very big concern.

📝 Question 2: Aluminium Refining

South Africa is one of the world's largest refiners of aluminium. The overall reaction is:

Al₂O₃(l) + 3C → 4Al(l) + 3CO₂(g)
  1. What is the function of the cryolite?
  2. Referring to the overall reaction, write down the cathode half reaction.
  3. The graphite anodes need to be replaced regularly. Explain why.
✅ Solutions:
  1. To reduce the melting point of aluminium oxide (from ~2050°C to ~950°C)
  2. Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al
  3. Carbon in the electrodes reacts with O₂ gas produced, forming CO₂. The carbon is consumed, causing electrodes to disintegrate.
