⚡ Factors Affecting Reaction Rates

In this lesson, we explore the Collision Theory and discover what factors determine how fast chemical reactions occur. Understanding these factors allows us to control and optimize chemical processes in industry and everyday life.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Explain what is meant by reaction rate
  • Distinguish between homogeneous and heterogeneous reactions
  • List the factors that affect the rate of chemical reactions
  • Explain the Collision Theory and requirements for effective collisions
  • Explain how each factor affects reaction rate using the Collision Theory

🔄 Types of Reactions

🧪 Homogeneous Reactions

Reactants and products are in the same phase (all liquids, all gases, or all in solution).

Example: HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l)

4 factors affect rate

⚗️ Heterogeneous Reactions

Reactants and products are in different phases (e.g., solid reacting with liquid).

Example: Zn(s) + 2HCl(aq) → ZnCl₂(aq) + H₂(g)

5 factors affect rate (includes surface area)

💥 The Collision Theory

The Collision Theory explains why chemical reactions occur and why they occur at different rates.

Requirements for a Chemical Reaction

🎯
Particles Must Collide

Atoms or molecules must come into contact - their outer electron energy levels must overlap

⚡
Sufficient Energy

Particles must have enough kinetic energy to overcome the activation energy barrier

🔄
Correct Orientation

Particles must collide at the correct angle for bonds to break and form

💡
Effective Collision: A collision in which the reacting particles have sufficient kinetic energy AND the correct orientation to result in a reaction.

📊 Five Factors Affecting Reaction Rate

🧪
1. Concentration

Higher concentration = more particles per volume = more collisions = more effective collisions = faster rate

🌡️
2. Temperature

Higher temperature = more kinetic energy = particles move faster = more collisions with enough energy = faster rate

🧊
3. Surface Area

Greater exposed surface = more particles available for collision = more effective collisions = faster rate (heterogeneous only)

⚡
4. Catalyst

Lowers activation energy = more particles have sufficient energy = more effective collisions = faster rate

🔬
5. Nature of Reactants

Bond strength and complexity affect how easily reactions occur. Ionic compounds react faster than covalent compounds.

📖 Detailed Explanations Using Collision Theory

🧪 Concentration Effect

  • A higher concentration means a greater number of particles in a given volume
  • If there are more particles, there will be more collisions
  • More collisions means more effective collisions
  • Hence the rate of reaction increases

🌡️ Temperature Effect

  • Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of particles
  • Higher temperature means particles have more kinetic energy
  • Particles move faster and collide more frequently
  • More particles have energy ≥ activation energy
  • More successful collisions = faster reaction rate
  • Both exothermic and endothermic reactions speed up with increased temperature

🧊 Surface Area Effect (Heterogeneous Reactions)

  • Breaking a solid into smaller pieces allows for more vigorous mixing
  • Smaller pieces have more exposed surface area
  • More surface = more particles available for collision
  • The greater the exposed surface area, the faster the reaction rate

⚡ Catalyst Effect

  • A catalyst lowers the activation energy needed for successful collisions
  • With lower activation energy, more particles have sufficient energy to react
  • More collisions are now effective
  • The rate of both endothermic and exothermic reactions increases
  • The catalyst is not consumed in the reaction

✏️ Worked Example

Magnesium and Hydrochloric Acid Reaction
Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂(g)

A 50 mm piece of clean magnesium ribbon reacts with 20 cm³ of hydrochloric acid (1 mol·dm⁻³). How will the rate change if:

Change Made Effect on Rate Explanation
Magnesium filed to powder INCREASE Greater surface area → more particles exposed → more collisions
HCl concentration increased to 2 mol·dm⁻³ INCREASE More HCl particles → more frequent collisions → more effective collisions
Mixture is cooled DECREASE Lower temperature → less kinetic energy → fewer particles with E ≥ Eₐ
Example 2: Iron and Copper Sulfate

Question: An iron nail displaces copper from copper sulfate solution. Give three ways to increase the rate of this reaction.

Fe(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → FeSO₄(aq) + Cu(s)

Answers:

  1. Use iron powder instead of nails (increased surface area)
  2. Heat the reaction mixture (increased temperature)
  3. Increase the concentration of CuSO₄ solution

📋 Summary: Key Terms

Term Definition
Reaction Rate The change in concentration of reactants or products per unit time
Collision Theory Theory that explains chemical reactions occur when particles collide with sufficient energy and correct orientation
Effective Collision A collision where particles have sufficient kinetic energy and correct orientation to react
Activation Energy (Eₐ) The minimum energy particles must have for a successful collision
Catalyst A substance that speeds up a reaction without being consumed, by lowering activation energy
Temperature A measure of the average kinetic energy of particles in a substance
