🔑 What You Should Already Know

  • Neutralisation reactions - when an acid reacts with a base to form salt and water
  • Common acids - HCl, H2SO4, HNO3
  • Common bases - NaOH, KOH, NH3

⚖️ What is Neutralisation?

A neutralisation reaction occurs when an acid reacts with a base. The H+ ions from the acid combine with OH- ions from the base to form water (H2O).

The general equation is:

Acid + Base → Salt + Water

🧂 Salt Formation - Important Notes

A salt is an ionic compound produced by the reaction of an acid with a base. The type of salt formed depends on the acid used:

🔬 Reaction Type 1: Acids + Metal Hydroxides

Acid + Metal Hydroxide → Salt + Water

📌 Example

Word equation:

Hydrochloric acid + Sodium hydroxide → Sodium chloride + Water

Chemical equation:

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H2O

🔬 Reaction Type 2: Acids + Metal Oxides

Acid + Metal Oxide → Salt + Water

📌 Example

Word equation:

Sulphuric acid + Sodium oxide → Sodium sulphate + Water

Chemical equation:

H2SO4 + Na2O → Na2SO4 + H2O

🔬 Reaction Type 3: Acids + Metal Carbonates

Acid + Metal Carbonate → Salt + Water + Carbon Dioxide

📌 Example

Word equation:

Nitric acid + Sodium carbonate → Sodium nitrate + Water + Carbon dioxide

Chemical equation:

2HNO3 + Na2CO3 → 2NaNO3 + H2O + CO2

Note: Reactions with carbonates always produce carbon dioxide gas (CO2), which causes bubbling/effervescence!

⭐ Key Summary
