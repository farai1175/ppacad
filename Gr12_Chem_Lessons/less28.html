What is an Electrolytic Cell?

▼
📝 Definition: Electrolytic Cell

An electrolytic cell is an electrochemical cell that converts electrical energy into chemical energy. It uses an external power source to drive a non-spontaneous chemical reaction.

In an electrolytic cell, electrical energy from an external source (like a battery or power supply) forces a chemical reaction to occur that would not happen spontaneously. This process is called electrolysis.

🔌

Energy Conversion

Electrical energy → Chemical energy

External power drives the reaction

⚙️

Non-Spontaneous

Requires external energy input

Reaction won't occur on its own

Structure of an Electrolytic Cell

🔧 Key Structural Features

  • Single electrolyte - Both electrodes are placed in the same solution
  • Electrodes - Often made of inert materials like carbon (graphite)
  • External power supply - Provides the electrical energy
  • No salt bridge needed - Only one container is used

Electrodes in Electrolytic Cells

⚠️ Important: Electrode Polarity is Reversed!

In an electrolytic cell, the electrode polarity is opposite to that of a galvanic cell:

  • Anode = POSITIVE electrode (connected to + terminal of power supply)
  • Cathode = NEGATIVE electrode (connected to − terminal of power supply)

However, oxidation still occurs at the anode and reduction at the cathode!

➕

Anode (Positive Electrode)

  • Oxidation occurs here
  • Anions are attracted here
  • Anions lose electrons
  • Connected to + terminal
➖

Cathode (Negative Electrode)

  • Reduction occurs here
  • Cations are attracted here
  • Cations gain electrons
  • Connected to − terminal

Types of Electrolytic Cells

🔥 Molten Electrolyte

Uses a molten (melted) ionic compound

Example: Molten PbBr₂

💧 Aqueous Electrolyte

Uses an ionic compound dissolved in water

Example: NaI solution

📝 Key Principle

In both types of electrolytic cells:

  • Anions (negative ions) are oxidised at the anode
  • Cations (positive ions) are reduced at the cathode
📝 Exercise 1: Check Your Understanding
  1. What energy conversion takes place in an electrolytic cell?
    → Electrical energy is converted into chemical energy
  2. What is the major structural difference between an electrolytic and galvanic cell?
    → In an electrolytic cell, both electrodes are placed in a single common electrolyte; in a galvanic cell, they are in two separate electrolytes
  3. Why does an electrolytic cell need an external energy source?
    → The electrical source provides the energy to cause the non-spontaneous chemical reaction to occur

Galvanic vs Electrolytic Cells

▼

Understanding the differences between galvanic and electrolytic cells is crucial for this topic.

Property Galvanic Cell Electrolytic Cell
Energy Conversion Chemical → Electrical Electrical → Chemical
Reaction Type Spontaneous Non-spontaneous
External Power Not needed Required
Number of Electrolytes Two (separate) One (common)
Salt Bridge Required Not needed
Anode Polarity Negative (−) Positive (+)
Cathode Polarity Positive (+) Negative (−)
Anode Process Oxidation Oxidation
Cathode Process Reduction Reduction
Examples Batteries Electroplating, electrolysis
🧠 Remember: Same Processes, Different Polarity

In BOTH types of cells:
Oxidation occurs at the Anode (AN OX)
Reduction occurs at the Cathode (RED CAT)

Electrolysis of Molten Lead(II) Bromide

▼

Let's examine the electrolysis of molten lead(II) bromide (PbBr₂) as an example of an electrolytic cell with a molten electrolyte.

🔥 Why Molten?

When PbBr₂ is heated until it melts, the ions (Pb²⁺ and Br⁻) are freed from the crystal lattice and can move. This allows them to conduct electricity and undergo electrolysis.

Dissociation

PbBr₂(l) → Pb²⁺(l) + 2Br⁻(l)

Electrode Reactions

At the Anode (+)

Oxidation of Br⁻ ions:

2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻

Bromide ions lose electrons to form bromine gas

Observation: Orange-brown bromine gas bubbles off

At the Cathode (−)

Reduction of Pb²⁺ ions:

Pb²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Pb

Lead ions gain electrons to form lead metal

Observation: Silvery lead metal deposits on the cathode

Net Ionic Reaction

2Br⁻ + Pb²⁺ → Br₂ + Pb
📝 Exercise 2: Electrolysis of Molten CuCl₂

A current supply is connected to two carbon electrodes immersed in molten copper(II) chloride.

  1. Give an equation for the dissociation of copper(II) chloride:
    → CuCl₂ → Cu²⁺ + 2Cl⁻
  2. Write the oxidation half-reaction (at anode):
    → 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻
  3. Write the reduction half-reaction (at cathode):
    → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu
  4. Write the net ionic reaction:
    → 2Cl⁻ + Cu²⁺ → Cu + Cl₂
  5. What energy conversion takes place?
    → Electrical energy is converted to chemical energy
  6. What observation would be made at the anode?
    → Chlorine gas will be seen bubbling off. Cl⁻ ions are oxidised to Cl₂ gas.
