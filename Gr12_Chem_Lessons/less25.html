The Chlor-Alkali Industry

▼

1. What is the Chlor-Alkali Process?

Chlorine gas is produced in vast quantities in industry by electrolysis — the chemical decomposition of a compound produced by passing an electric current through a conducting liquid.

📌 Key Concept: Electrolysis

In the chlor-alkali process, an electric current is passed through a solution of seawater, known as brine (concentrated NaCl solution). This process produces three valuable products:

  • ⚡ Chlorine gas (Cl₂) — at the anode
  • ⚡ Hydrogen gas (H₂) — at the cathode
  • ⚡ Sodium hydroxide (NaOH) — in solution

2. The Redox Reactions

The chlor-alkali process involves redox reactions (reduction-oxidation reactions). Let's examine what happens at each electrode:

⚡ At the Anode (Positive Electrode) — OXIDATION
2Cl⁻(aq) → Cl₂(g) + 2e⁻

Chloride ions lose electrons (oxidation) to form chlorine gas

⚡ At the Cathode (Negative Electrode) — REDUCTION
2H₂O(l) + 2e⁻ → H₂(g) + 2OH⁻(aq)

Water molecules gain electrons (reduction) to form hydrogen gas and hydroxide ions

🔄 Overall Reaction
2NaCl(aq) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H₂(g) + Cl₂(g)

Chemistry of Hair

▼

1. pH of Scalp and Hair

The natural pH of the scalp and hair is about 5 (slightly acidic). Understanding this is crucial for hair care:

📌 Shampoo pH Levels

  • 🔹 Ordinary shampoos: Slightly acidic (pH < 7) to clear off scum and excess oils
  • 🔹 Neutral shampoos (pH ≈ 7): Used for babies or people with sensitive scalps
  • 🔹 Hair treatments: Often have pH > 7, which can cause potential problems

💆 Hair Relaxers

Hair relaxers work to straighten the hair using strong bases that alter the structure of the hair.

Key Chemicals:
NaOH (Sodium Hydroxide) Guanidine Hydroxide
Type Main Chemical Characteristics
Lye Relaxer NaOH (very strong base) or Guanidine hydroxide Works quickly but may rinse out easily
No-Lye Relaxer Ca(OH)₂ (Calcium hydroxide) Better for sensitive scalps, but dries hair more
⚠️ Common Complaints
  • Hair brittleness
  • Weakened hair shaft
  • Hair breakage
  • Some hair loss

💡 Tip: When using no-lye relaxers, use a "de-calcifying" shampoo to prevent calcium build-up!

🌀 Permanent Waves (Perms)

A permanent wave involves using chemicals to break and reform the bonds of the hair, creating curls.

Key Chemicals:
HSCH₂CO₂H (Thioglycolic acid) NH₃ (Ammonia)

🎨 Hair Colouring

Hair dyes use various chemicals to change hair colour. The main components keep the dye bonded to the hair.

Key Chemicals:
NH₃ (Ammonia) H₂O₂ (Hydrogen Peroxide)
Dye Type How It Works Duration
Temporary Dyes Coat the outside of the hair Wash out easily
Permanent Dyes Remove natural colour and deposit new colour into the cortex Must grow out to see natural colour

Summary: Chemicals in Hair Treatments

Treatment Main Chemicals pH
Hair Relaxers NaOH, Guanidine hydroxide, Ca(OH)₂ > 7 (Basic)
Permanent Waves Thioglycolic acid + Ammonia > 7 (Basic)
Hair Colouring Ammonia + Hydrogen peroxide > 7 (Basic)
