📝 Baseline Answers

  • Polarisation: The phenomenon in which waves of light or other radiation are restricted in direction of vibration.
  • Transverse wave: A wave vibrating at right angles to the direction of its propagation.
  • Reflection: The change in direction of a wave away from a boundary it encounters.
  • Refraction: The bending of light as it passes from one substance to another.
  • Interference: The combining or cancelling of two or more waves of the same frequency.
  • Diffraction: The bending of waves around an object.

Source of Electromagnetic Waves

🔑 Key Discovery

Electromagnetic waves originate from accelerating charges. This fundamental principle was established by James Maxwell in the 19th century.

How are EM Waves Generated?

🌀 Accelerating Charges

When electric charges accelerate, they create changing electric and magnetic fields.

⚡ Changing Fields

A changing electric field produces a changing magnetic field, and vice versa.

🔄 Mutual Induction

The electric and magnetic fields continuously regenerate each other, creating a self-sustaining wave.

📡 Practical Example

Radio waves are produced when electrons move back and forth in an antenna.

🧠 Maxwell's Revolutionary Insight

Changing E → Changing B → Changing E → ...

Mutual regeneration of electric and magnetic fields

c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s

Constant speed of EM waves in vacuum

⚠️ Important Note

Stationary charges create electric fields, and moving charges at constant velocity create magnetic fields. But only accelerating charges create electromagnetic waves!

🌊

EM Wave Propagation

Electromagnetic waves propagate through the mutual induction of oscillating electric and magnetic fields. Let's examine this process step by step:

1. Initial Acceleration

A charged particle accelerates, creating a changing electric field.

2. Magnetic Field Creation

The changing electric field produces a changing magnetic field at right angles to it.

3. Electric Field Regeneration

The changing magnetic field creates another changing electric field.

4. Continuous Process

The process continues indefinitely, with fields regenerating each other.

📐 Right Angle Relationship

The electric field (E), magnetic field (B), and direction of propagation are all mutually perpendicular to each other. This is why EM waves are transverse waves.

📊

Properties of Electromagnetic Waves

Electromagnetic waves possess several distinctive properties that set them apart from other types of waves:

🌀 Source

Originate from accelerating electric charges

⚡ Field Composition

Consist of electric and magnetic fields at right angles to each other

🚀 Constant Speed

Travel at 3 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum (speed of light)

📏 Transverse Nature

Are transverse waves and can be polarised

🌌 Vacuum Propagation

Can pass through vacuum (no medium required)

⚖️ Neutral Charge

Do not carry electric charge

🪞 Reflection/Refraction

Can be reflected and refracted like other waves

🌈 Wave Effects

Show interference and diffraction effects

Polarization Demonstration

Transverse waves can be polarized

This is evidence they are transverse!

Classwork & Examples

1

Evidence for Transverse Nature

Evidence that electromagnetic waves are transverse waves is that:

A. the electric charges undergo acceleration.

B. they undergo interference.

C. they undergo diffraction through narrow slits.

D. they can be polarised.

Solution:
Analysis

Let's evaluate each option:

  • A: Acceleration of charges is how EM waves are generated, but doesn't prove they are transverse
  • B: Interference is a wave property but doesn't distinguish between transverse and longitudinal waves
  • C: Diffraction is also a general wave property
  • D: Polarization is unique to transverse waves - longitudinal waves cannot be polarized
Conclusion

Correct answer: D

The ability to be polarized is definitive evidence that electromagnetic waves are transverse waves.

2

Properties of EM Waves

List four properties of electromagnetic waves.

Solution:
Key Properties
  1. Originate from accelerating charges
  2. Are transverse waves and can be polarized
  3. Travel at constant speed of 3 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum
  4. Can pass through vacuum (no medium required)
Additional Properties

Other important properties include:

  • Consist of mutually perpendicular electric and magnetic fields
  • Do not carry electric charge
  • Can be reflected, refracted, diffracted, and show interference
  • Are generated by oscillating charges
3

Propagation Without Medium

Explain how EM waves can be propagated without a medium.

Solution:
Mutual Induction

EM waves are generated by the mutual induction of electric and magnetic fields:

  • A changing electric field produces a changing magnetic field
  • The changing magnetic field produces another changing electric field
  • This process continues indefinitely
Self-Sustaining Process

The electric and magnetic fields continuously regenerate each other, creating a self-sustaining wave that requires no material medium for propagation.

Constant Speed

Maxwell calculated that this mutual induction process would propagate through space at a constant speed of 3 × 10⁸ m/s, which is the speed of light.
