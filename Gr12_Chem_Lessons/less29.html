Electron Flow (External Circuit)

🔌 Electron Movement
  • Electrons flow from the anode to the cathode through the external wire
  • At the anode (Zn): Zn atoms lose electrons → Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻
  • At the cathode (Cu): Cu²⁺ ions gain electrons → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu
  • This flow of electrons creates an electric current

Ion Movement (Internal Circuit)

🔄

In the Electrolyte Solutions

Ions are free to move in aqueous solutions. This movement of charged particles allows the internal circuit to be complete.

🌉

Through the Salt Bridge

Ions migrate through the salt bridge to maintain electrical neutrality in each half-cell.

Function of the Salt Bridge

🧂 Salt Bridge Functions

  1. Completes the circuit - allows ion flow between half-cells
  2. Separates the electrolytes - prevents direct mixing of solutions
  3. Maintains electrical neutrality - keeps balance of cations and anions in each half-cell

Common salt bridge solutions: KCl, NaCl, KNO₃, Na₂SO₄

⚠️ Why Electrical Neutrality Matters

As the cell operates:

  • At the anode: Zn²⁺ ions are produced → positive charge builds up
  • At the cathode: Cu²⁺ ions are removed → negative charge builds up
  • The salt bridge allows anions to move toward the anode and cations to move toward the cathode
📝 Exercise 1: Understanding Galvanic Cells
  1. What energy conversion takes place in a galvanic cell?
    → Converts chemical energy to electrical energy
  2. How are the two electrodes in the external circuit connected?
    → Using a conducting wire connected to a voltmeter or galvanometer
  3. How are the electrolytes in the internal circuit connected?
    → Using a salt bridge
  4. What is the name given to the half-cell in which oxidation takes place?
    → Anode half-cell
  5. What is the name given to the half-cell in which reduction takes place?
    → Cathode half-cell

Cell Notation

▼

Cell notation is a shorthand way of representing a galvanic cell. It provides all the essential information about the cell in a compact format.

📋 Rules for Writing Cell Notation
1 Cell notations are always written from anode to cathode (left to right)
2 The anode is always placed on the left side
3 The cathode is always placed on the right side
4 A single vertical line | represents a phase boundary (solid/solution interface)
5 A double vertical line || represents the salt bridge

Example: Zinc-Copper Cell

For the reaction: Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu

Zn | Zn²⁺ || Cu²⁺ | Cu
← Anode (oxidation) Salt Bridge Cathode (reduction) →

More Examples

Iron-Silver Cell

Reaction: Fe + 2Ag⁺ → Fe²⁺ + 2Ag

Fe | Fe²⁺ || Ag⁺ | Ag

Magnesium-Iron Cell

Reaction: Mg + Fe²⁺ → Mg²⁺ + Fe

Mg | Mg²⁺ || Fe²⁺ | Fe

Electrolysis of Water

▼
🔬 Experiment: Part 1 - Electrolysis of Water

Aim: To investigate the electrolysis of water and identify the products formed at each electrode.

Apparatus

  • Water bowl or beaker
  • Two carbon (graphite) electrodes
  • Two test tubes
  • Water
  • Two conducting wires
  • 9V battery or DC power supply
  • Rubber stops
  • Matches

Electrode Reactions

At the Anode (+)

Oxidation of water:

2H₂O → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻

Oxygen gas is produced

Test: Glowing splint relights

At the Cathode (−)

Reduction of water:

2H₂O + 2e⁻ → H₂ + 2OH⁻

Hydrogen gas is produced

Test: Burning splint makes "pop" sound

Overall Reaction

2H₂O(l) → 2H₂(g) + O₂(g)

📊 Observations

  • Gas bubbles form at both electrodes
  • More gas is produced at the cathode (hydrogen) than at the anode (oxygen)
  • The ratio is approximately 2:1 (H₂ : O₂)
🧪

Electrolysis of Aqueous Sodium Iodide

▼
🔬 Experiment: Part 2 - Electrolysis of NaI Solution

Aim: To investigate the electrolysis of aqueous sodium iodide and identify the products formed.

Dissociation

NaI(aq) → Na⁺(aq) + I⁻(aq)

Electrode Reactions

At the Anode (+)

Oxidation of iodide ions:

2I⁻ → I₂ + 2e⁻

Iodine is produced

Observation: Dark brown/purple discolouration

At the Cathode (−)

Reduction of water:

2H₂O + 2e⁻ → H₂ + 2OH⁻

Hydrogen gas is produced

Test: Burning splint makes "pop" sound

❓ Why doesn't Na⁺ get reduced?

Sodium is a very reactive metal. It's easier (requires less energy) to reduce water than to reduce Na⁺ ions. Therefore, water is preferentially reduced at the cathode instead of sodium ions.

📝 Experiment Questions & Answers
  1. What do you observe taking place at each electrode when current flows?
    → At one electrode: dark discolouration (iodine forming). At the other: gas bubbles forming.
  2. At which electrode (anode or cathode) did the gas form?
    → Cathode
  3. What did you observe when a burning match was placed near the gas?
    → The match ignited with a popping sound
  4. Name the gas that is produced.
    → Hydrogen gas
  5. Name the substance discolouring the electrolyte.
    → Iodine
