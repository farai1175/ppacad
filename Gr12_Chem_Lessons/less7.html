Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Identify the types of reactions that hydrocarbons undergo
  • Explain what happens during each type of reaction
  • Compare the reactivity of different hydrocarbons (alkanes, alkenes, alkynes)
  • Write equations for combustion, substitution, elimination, and addition reactions
  • Identify reaction conditions for different organic reactions

Types of Organic Reactions

Organic compounds undergo four main types of reactions:

🔥 Combustion (Oxidation)

Reaction with oxygen, producing carbon dioxide and water. Releases energy (exothermic).

Hydrocarbon + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + Energy

🔄 Substitution

One atom or group is replaced by another atom or group.

CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl

➖ Elimination

Atoms are removed to form a smaller molecule and an unsaturated product.

CH₃CH₃ → CH₂=CH₂ + H₂

➕ Addition

Atoms are added across a double or triple bond.

CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ → CH₂Br-CH₂Br

⛽ Alkane Reactions Saturated

💡 Key Point

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons with only single bonds. They are relatively unreactive because all bonds are strong C-C and C-H single bonds.

Reactions Alkanes Undergo:

🔥 Combustion

Alkanes react with oxygen in the air, forming carbon dioxide and water. This reaction is exothermic (releases energy).

CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O + energy
Examples:
  • Methane burns with a blue flame
  • Propane (C₃H₈) - used in Bunsen burners
  • Butane (C₄H₁₀) - used in cigarette lighters

➖ Elimination (Cracking)

Heating at high temperatures produces unsaturated products and a small molecule (H₂).

CH₃CH₃ → CH₂=CH₂ + H₂
Conditions: High temperature (cracking)

🔄 Substitution (Halogenation)

A hydrogen atom is replaced by a halogen atom, forming a haloalkane.

CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl
Conditions: Sunlight or heat (activation energy required)

Combustion Examples:

Alkane Balanced Equation Use
Methane CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O Natural gas
Propane C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O Bunsen burners, LPG
Butane 2C₄H₁₀ + 13O₂ → 8CO₂ + 10H₂O Lighters, camping stoves

⚡ Alkene Reactions Unsaturated

💡 Key Point

Alkenes are unsaturated hydrocarbons containing a C=C double bond. They are more reactive than alkanes because the double bond can easily undergo addition reactions.

Reactions Alkenes Undergo:

🔥 Combustion

Alkenes undergo combustion like alkanes, forming CO₂ and H₂O.

C₂H₄ + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 2H₂O

➕ Addition Reactions

Atoms are added across the double bond, converting it to single bonds.

CH₂=CH₂ + X₂ → CH₂X-CH₂X

Types of Addition Reactions:

🧪 Hydrohalogenation
CH₂=CH₂ + HCl → CH₃-CH₂Cl

Addition of hydrogen halide (HX). No water present.

⚗️ Halogenation
CH₂=CH₂ + Cl₂ → CH₂Cl-CH₂Cl

Addition of halogen (Cl₂ or Br₂).

💧 Hydration
CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O → CH₃CH₂OH

Addition of water. Catalyst: H₃PO₄ or HX.

🔧 Hydrogenation
CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ → CH₃-CH₃

Addition of hydrogen. Catalyst: Pt, Pd, or Ni.

📝 Markovnikov's Rule

When HX or H₂O is added to an unsymmetrical alkene:

  • The H atom attaches to the carbon with more hydrogen atoms
  • The X or OH attaches to the more substituted carbon

🔥 Alkyne Reactions Unsaturated

💡 Key Point

Alkynes contain a C≡C triple bond and are also unsaturated. They undergo similar reactions to alkenes.

Reactions Alkynes Undergo:

🔥 Combustion

Alkynes undergo combustion like other hydrocarbons.

2C₂H₂ + 5O₂ → 4CO₂ + 2H₂O
Note: Ethyne (acetylene) burns with a very hot flame - used in welding torches.

➕ Addition (Hydrogenation)

Hydrogen adds across the triple bond.

CH≡CH + 2H₂ → CH₃-CH₃
Conditions: Catalyst (Pt, Pd, or Ni) in H₂ atmosphere

Comparing Reactivity

⚠️ Why Don't Alkanes React Easily?

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons with only single bonds. All bonds are strong and require more energy to break. They do not react with bromine easily because there is no double bond to add across.

Hydrocarbon Bonds Reactivity Main Reactions
Alkanes Single (C-C) Low (saturated) Combustion, Substitution
Alkenes Double (C=C) High (unsaturated) Combustion, Addition
Alkynes Triple (C≡C) High (unsaturated) Combustion, Addition

🧪 Testing for Unsaturation

Bromine water test: Add bromine water (orange-brown) to the hydrocarbon.

  • Alkene/Alkyne: Decolorizes bromine water (addition reaction occurs)
  • Alkane: No color change (no reaction without UV light)
