📏 The pH Scale

The pH scale is used to measure how acidic or basic a liquid is. It ranges from 0 to 14.

🔴 Acids

pH range: less than 7

Lower pH = stronger acid

🟢 Neutral

pH = exactly 7

Distilled water has pH 7

🔵 Bases

pH range: greater than 7

Higher pH = stronger base

🧪 What is an Indicator?

An indicator is usually a weak organic dye that changes colour at definite pH values. Different indicators change colour at various pH values.

Common laboratory indicators include: methyl orange, bromothymol blue, phenolphthalein and universal indicator.

⚠️ Important Notes

  • The pH scale is logarithmic - each pH unit represents a 10-fold change in H3O+ concentration
  • A solution with pH 3 is 10 times more acidic than one with pH 4
  • Universal indicator gives a range of colors across the entire pH scale

pH Calculations

▼

📐 Calculating Concentration

Concentration is measured in the units mol·dm-3. The equation used to calculate concentration is:

c = n V

where c = concentration (mol·dm-3), n = number of moles, V = volume (dm3)

It may be necessary to calculate the number of moles first, using the equation:

n = m M

where n = number of moles, m = mass (g), M = molar mass (g·mol-1)

📝 Worked Example: Calculating Concentration

Problem: If 25 g of HCl is mixed with water to produce 375 cm³ of solution, what is the concentration of the solution?

1 Find the molar mass of HCl:

M(HCl) = 1 + 35.5 = 36.5 g·mol-1

2 Calculate the number of moles:

n = m/M = 25/36.5 = 0.685 mol

3 Convert volume to dm³:

V = 375 cm³ = 375/1000 = 0.375 dm³

4 Calculate concentration:

c = n/V = 0.685/0.375 = 1.83 mol·dm-3

🧮 Calculating pH

The pH of a solution can be calculated using the equation:

pH = -log[H3O+]

where [H3O+] is the concentration of hydronium ions in mol·dm-3

💡 Key Point for Strong Acids

For strong acids that ionise completely:

  • Monoprotic acids (HCl, HNO3): [H3O+] = concentration of acid
  • Diprotic acids (H2SO4): [H3O+] = 2 × concentration of acid

📝 Worked Example 1: pH of HNO3

Problem: Calculate the pH of a solution of HNO3 with a concentration of 1 × 10-2 mol·dm-3.

1 Write the ionisation equation:

HNO3(l) + H2O(l) ⇌ H3O+(aq) + NO3-(aq)

2 Determine [H3O+]:

Since HNO3 is a strong acid, it ionises completely. 1 molecule of HNO3 forms 1 H3O+ ion.

[H3O+] = 1 × 10-2 mol·dm-3

3 Calculate pH:

pH = -log[H3O+] = -log(1 × 10-2) = 2

📝 Worked Example 2: pH from Mass of HCl

Problem: 0.18 g of HCl is dissolved in 5 dm³ of water. Calculate the pH of the solution.

1 Calculate the number of moles:

M(HCl) = 1 + 35.5 = 36.5 g·mol-1

n = m/M = 0.18/36.5 = 4.93 × 10-3 mol

2 Calculate concentration:

c = n/V = 4.93 × 10-3/5 = 9.86 × 10-4 mol·dm-3

3 Determine [H3O+]:

[H3O+] = 9.86 × 10-4 mol·dm-3 (HCl ionises completely)

4 Calculate pH:

pH = -log(9.86 × 10-4) = 3.01

Class Exercises

▼

Practice these pH calculation problems:

📝 Exercise 1

4.5 g of HNO3 is dissolved in 500 cm³ of water. Calculate the pH of the solution.

(M of HNO3 = 63 g·mol-1)

✅ Solution:

Step 1: Ionisation equation:

HNO3(l) + H2O(l) ⇌ H3O+(aq) + NO3-(aq)

Step 2: Calculate moles: n = m/M = 4.5/63 = 0.0714 mol

Step 3: Convert volume: V = 500/1000 = 0.5 dm³

Step 4: Calculate concentration: c = n/V = 0.0714/0.5 = 0.143 mol·dm-3

Step 5: Calculate pH: pH = -log(0.143) = 0.85

📝 Exercise 2

Calculate the pH of H2SO4 of concentration 1.2 × 10-4 mol·dm-3, assuming complete ionisation of the acid occurs.

✅ Solution:

Step 1: Ionisation equation:

H2SO4(l) + 2H2O(l) ⇌ 2H3O+(aq) + SO42-(aq)

Step 2: Since H2SO4 is diprotic:

[H3O+] = 2 × 1.2 × 10-4 = 2.4 × 10-4 mol·dm-3

Step 3: Calculate pH: pH = -log(2.4 × 10-4) = 3.62

📝 Exercise 3

Calculate the pH of HCl of concentration 1 × 10-2 mol·dm-3.

✅ Solution:

Step 1: Ionisation equation:

HCl(l) + H2O(l) ⇌ H3O+(aq) + Cl-(aq)

Step 2: Since HCl is monoprotic and ionises completely:

[H3O+] = 1 × 10-2 mol·dm-3

Step 3: Calculate pH: pH = -log(1 × 10-2) = 2
