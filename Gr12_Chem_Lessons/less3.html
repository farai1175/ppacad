Integrated Problem Solving

In real-world applications, impulse, conservation of momentum, and projectile motion concepts are often combined in single problems. Let's work through some examples:

Problem 1: Bouncing Ball

The roof of a tall building is 25 m above the ground. A rigid ball of mass 0.3 kg falls freely when dropped from the roof. It strikes the concrete floor on the ground with velocity v₁. It bounces to a maximum vertical height of 6 m.

Interactive Calculator for Problem 1

Calculate the velocity v₁ when the ball first hits the floor.

Using projectile motion equations (take up as positive):

vf² = vi² + 2gΔy

vf² = 0² + 2(-9.8)(-25)

vf² = 490

vf = -22.14 m/s (downward)

∴ v₁ = 22.14 m/s downward

Calculate the impulse of the ball as a result of the collision.

First, find the velocity after bounce using projectile motion:

vf² = vi² + 2gΔy

0² = vi² + 2(-9.8)(6)

vi² = 117.6

vi = 10.84 m/s (upward)

Now calculate impulse:

Impulse = Δp = m(vf - vi)

Impulse = 0.3[10.84 - (-22.14)]

Impulse = 0.3 × 32.98 = 9.89 N•s upward

Calculate the magnitude of the net force exerted on the ball.

Using the impulse-momentum theorem:

FnetΔt = Δp

Fnet × 0.9 = 9.89

Fnet = 10.99 N

Problem 2: Shopping Trolley Collision

Two shopping trolleys, X and Y, are both moving to the right along the same straight line. The mass of trolley Y is 12 kg and its kinetic energy is 37.5 J. Trolley X of mass 30 kg collides with trolley Y and they stick together on impact. After the collision the combined speed of the trolleys is 3.2 m/s. (Ignore the effects of friction)

Interactive Calculator for Problem 2

Calculate the speed of trolley Y before the collision.

Using kinetic energy formula:

Ek = ½mv²

37.5 = ½ × 12 × v²

37.5 = 6v²

v² = 6.25

v = 2.5 m/s

∴ Speed of trolley Y = 2.5 m/s to the right

Calculate the speed of trolley X before the collision.

Using conservation of momentum (take to the right as positive):

Σpbefore = Σpafter

mXvXi + mYvYi = (mX + mY)vf

30 × vXi + 12 × 2.5 = (30 + 12) × 3.2

30vXi + 30 = 134.4

30vXi = 104.4

vXi = 3.48 m/s

∴ Speed of trolley X = 3.48 m/s to the right

Calculate the magnitude of the force that trolley X exerts on trolley Y.

Using impulse-momentum theorem on trolley Y:

FnetΔt = mΔv

F × 0.2 = 12 × (3.2 - 2.5)

F × 0.2 = 12 × 0.7

F × 0.2 = 8.4

F = 42 N

∴ Magnitude of force = 42 N

Impulse and Vehicle Safety

During a collision in which a car is brought to rest, the people in the car must also be brought to rest. From the impulse equation (FnetΔt = Δp), we can see that if Δt increases, the net force acting on the people to bring about the required decrease in momentum is smaller, thus reducing injury.

🚗

Crumple Zones

The front of the car is designed to crumple, increasing collision time and reducing force on passengers.

💨

Airbags

Airbags deploy and compress, exerting force over a longer period to reduce impact force on occupants.

🔗

Seatbelts

Seatbelts stretch slightly during collision, increasing stopping time and reducing force on the body.
