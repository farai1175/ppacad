Lesson Overview

In this lesson, we explore practical applications of electromagnetic radiation and understand why different types have different effects on matter. You will learn about the relationship between energy, frequency, and penetrating ability, and the important safety considerations for high-energy radiation.

💡 Prior Knowledge Required

Before starting this lesson, you should understand:

  • The EM spectrum from Lesson 3
  • The relationship E = hf (energy and frequency)
  • Wave properties (wavelength, frequency)
  • Basic human biology (skin, bones, cells)

Applications of EM Radiation

Each type of electromagnetic radiation has unique properties that make it suitable for specific applications:

📻 Radio Waves

Applications:

  • Radio broadcasting (AM/FM)
  • Television broadcasting
  • Mobile phone communication
  • GPS systems

Key Property: Long wavelengths allow diffraction around obstacles

🍽️ Microwaves

Applications:

  • Microwave ovens (heating food)
  • Satellite communications
  • Radar (air traffic control)
  • Speed trap guns

Key Property: Absorbed by water molecules (heating effect)

🔥 Infrared

Applications:

  • Remote controls
  • Night vision equipment
  • Burglar alarms (PIR sensors)
  • Thermal imaging cameras

Key Property: Detected as heat by sensors and animals

👁️ Visible Light

Applications:

  • Human vision
  • Photography
  • Fiber optic communications
  • Lasers

Key Property: Detected by human eyes (400-700 nm)

☀️ Ultraviolet

Applications:

  • Vitamin D production (UV-B)
  • Sterilization (germ killing)
  • Fluorescent lamps
  • Forensic analysis

Key Property: Causes chemical reactions and fluorescence

🦴 X-rays

Applications:

  • Medical imaging (bone fractures)
  • Airport security scanners
  • Material inspection
  • Cancer treatment (radiotherapy)

Key Property: Penetrate soft tissue but absorbed by bones

☢️ Gamma Rays

Applications:

  • Cancer treatment (radiation therapy)
  • Sterilizing medical equipment
  • Food preservation
  • Nuclear medicine imaging

Key Property: Highest energy, most penetrative

🔑 Key Relationship

Penetrating ability is directly related to energy: Higher frequency = Higher energy = Greater penetration

This is why gamma rays can pass through several centimeters of lead, while radio waves are stopped by walls.

Dangers of High-Energy EM Radiation

While EM radiation has many beneficial applications, high-energy radiation can be dangerous to humans and other living organisms:

☢️ Gamma Rays

⚠️ Extreme Danger

• Emitted by radioactive substances

• Can pass through several cm of lead/concrete

• Damage and change DNA structure

• Cause cancer and radiation sickness

Protection: Thick lead or concrete shielding required

🦴 X-rays

⚠️ Controlled Use Required

• Penetrate soft tissue, absorbed by bones

• Can damage living cells with prolonged exposure

• Radiation workers wear lead aprons

• Limited exposure in medical settings

Protection: Lead shielding, limited exposure time

☀️ Ultraviolet Radiation

⚠️ Sun Safety Important

• UV-A: Causes skin aging and wrinkles

• UV-B: Causes sunburn and skin cancer

• UV-C: Most dangerous but filtered by atmosphere

• Damages eyes (cataracts)

Protection: Sunscreen, hats, sunglasses

🧬 Biological Effects

High-energy EM radiation is ionizing radiation - it has enough energy to remove electrons from atoms, creating ions. This can:

  • Break chemical bonds in DNA molecules
  • Cause mutations in genetic material
  • Lead to cancer development
  • Kill cells (used in cancer treatment)

Low-energy radiation (radio, microwave, infrared, visible) is non-ionizing and generally safer.

Classwork & Examples

1

Medical Imaging

Which type of waves are energetic and able to penetrate the soft skin tissue of the body easily but are stopped by other parts such as bones?

A. Radio waves

B. UV rays

C. X-rays

D. Infrared

Solution:
Analysis of Options
  • A. Radio waves: Low energy, cannot penetrate skin deeply
  • B. UV rays: Absorbed by skin, cause sunburn
  • C. X-rays: High energy, penetrate soft tissue but absorbed by bones
  • D. Infrared: Absorbed as heat by skin
Conclusion

Correct answer: C (X-rays)

X-rays have just the right energy level to pass through soft tissue but be absorbed by denser materials like bone, making them ideal for medical imaging.

2

Radiation Dangers

These rays are emitted by radioactive substances and they are especially dangerous to humans and have enough energy to damage and change the structure of our body cells.

A. Gamma rays

B. UV rays

C. X-rays

D. Infrared

Solution:
Key Clues
  • "Emitted by radioactive substances" → Gamma rays are produced in nuclear reactions
  • "Especially dangerous to humans" → Gamma rays are the most harmful
  • "Damage and change structure of body cells" → Gamma rays are ionizing radiation that can mutate DNA
Conclusion

Correct answer: A (Gamma rays)

Gamma rays have the highest energy and are the most penetrative and dangerous form of EM radiation.

3

Diffraction Effects

Which region of the spectrum would you expect to exhibit marked diffraction effects? Explain.

Solution:
Understanding Diffraction

Diffraction is the bending of waves around obstacles. The amount of diffraction depends on wavelength relative to obstacle size:

Large wavelength relative to obstacle size → Significant diffraction

Small wavelength relative to obstacle size → Little diffraction

Application to EM Spectrum

Radio waves have the longest wavelengths in the EM spectrum (from centimeters to kilometers).

These long wavelengths are comparable to or larger than typical obstacles (buildings, hills), allowing radio waves to diffract around them.

Conclusion

Radio waves exhibit marked diffraction effects because of their long wavelengths.

This is why radio and TV signals can reach areas behind hills and inside buildings.

4

High Penetration Radiation

Name two types of electromagnetic radiation which have high penetrative power.

Solution:
Understanding Penetration

Penetrative power increases with energy and frequency:

Higher frequency → Higher energy → Greater penetration

Highest Frequency Radiation

The highest frequency (and therefore highest energy) EM radiation are:

  1. Gamma rays (highest frequency)
  2. X-rays (second highest frequency)
Conclusion

X-rays and gamma rays have high penetrative power.

Gamma rays can penetrate several centimeters of lead, while X-rays can penetrate soft tissue but are stopped by bone.

5

EM Spectrum Extremes

The range of wavelengths in the EM spectrum is from 1 × 10⁻¹³ m to 1 × 10³ m.

5.1 Name the two types of EM radiation found at the extremes of the wavelength spectrum stated above.

5.2 Give one application of each of these types of EM waves in 5.1 above.

Solution 5.1:
Identifying Extremes

Shortest wavelength (1 × 10⁻¹³ m): Gamma rays

Longest wavelength (1 × 10³ m = 1000 m): Radio waves

Solution 5.2:
Gamma Ray Application

Medicine: Cancer treatment (radiation therapy)

Gamma rays are used to kill cancer cells in radiotherapy. Their high energy allows them to penetrate deep into tissues to reach tumors.

Radio Wave Application

Communication: Radio/TV broadcasting, satellite communication

Radio waves are used to transmit information over long distances. Their long wavelengths allow them to diffract around obstacles and travel long distances.
