Overview

  • Rate of chemical reactions

Have you ever noticed that batteries "run out" over time? A brand new battery has a strong voltage, but as you use it, the voltage decreases until eventually it reads 0V and the battery is "dead."

In this lesson, we'll explore why this happens by examining the relationship between current, potential difference, and chemical equilibrium in galvanic cells.

🔋 Key Question

Why does the voltage of a galvanic cell decrease over time until it eventually reaches zero?

⚡

Current and Rate of Reaction

▼

Let's consider the zinc-copper galvanic cell and understand how current relates to the rate of reaction.

Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) ⇌ Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s)
📝 Key Relationship

The current flowing through a galvanic cell is directly related to the rate of electron transfer at the electrodes.

  • Higher rate of reaction → More electrons transferred per second → Higher current
  • Lower rate of reaction → Fewer electrons transferred per second → Lower current

The Reversible Nature of the Cell Reaction

🔄 Important Understanding

The net cell reaction in a galvanic cell is reversible. This means:

  • Forward reaction: Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ (oxidation at anode)
  • Reverse reaction: Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Zn (can occur when conditions change)
⚡

Current = Rate of Electron Flow

Current is the flow of electrons through the external circuit. The faster the redox reaction, the more electrons are released and accepted per second.

🔋

Cell Potential = Driving Force

The potential difference (voltage) is the "driving force" that pushes electrons through the circuit. It represents the energy available per unit charge.

⚖️

Cell Potential and Equilibrium

▼

The cell potential (Vcell) tells us how far the cell reaction is from equilibrium.

📝 Cell Potential and Equilibrium

The potential difference (Vcell) of a galvanic cell is related to the extent to which the spontaneous cell reaction has reached equilibrium.

What Happens Over Time?

1

Initially (Fresh Cell)

High concentration of reactant ions (Cu²⁺), low concentration of product ions (Zn²⁺). Forward reaction is fast. Vcell is at maximum (e.g., 1.10 V for Zn-Cu cell).

2

As Reaction Proceeds

Reactant ions (Cu²⁺) decrease, product ions (Zn²⁺) increase. The rate of the forward reaction decreases. Vcell decreases.

3

Approaching Equilibrium

The rate of the reverse reaction increases to balance the forward reaction. Current and potential continue to decrease.

4

At Equilibrium (Dead Battery)

Rate of forward reaction = Rate of reverse reaction. No net electron flow. Vcell = 0 V. The battery is "dead."

📉

Reactant Ions DECREASE

Cu²⁺ ions are consumed at the cathode as they are reduced to Cu metal.

Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu

📈

Product Ions INCREASE

Zn²⁺ ions are produced at the anode as Zn metal is oxidised.

Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻

💡 Key Insight

When Vcell = 0, it doesn't mean the reactions have stopped. It means the forward and reverse reactions are occurring at equal rates (dynamic equilibrium), so there is no net flow of electrons.

🔄

Le Chatelier's Principle in Galvanic Cells

▼
🔄 Le Chatelier's Principle

When a system at equilibrium is disturbed by a change in concentration, temperature, or pressure, the system will shift to counteract the change and establish a new equilibrium.

Applying Le Chatelier's Principle to Galvanic Cells

Consider the zinc-copper cell reaction:

Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) ⇌ Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s)

As the Cell Operates:

  • Cu²⁺ concentration decreases (reactant consumed)
  • Zn²⁺ concentration increases (product formed)

Le Chatelier's Response:

  • System shifts to the left (reverse reaction)
  • This opposes the buildup of products
  • Forward reaction rate decreases
📝 The Result
  • As [Cu²⁺] decreases and [Zn²⁺] increases, the equilibrium shifts LEFT
  • The rate of the forward reaction decreases
  • Fewer electrons are transferred per second
  • Both current AND cell potential decrease
  • At equilibrium: Vcell = 0 V, Current = 0 A

Summary of Relationships

Variable Over Time At Equilibrium
Reactant ion concentration [Cu²⁺] Decreases ↓ Constant (low)
Product ion concentration [Zn²⁺] Increases ↑ Constant (high)
Rate of forward reaction Decreases ↓ = Rate reverse
Rate of reverse reaction Increases ↑ = Rate forward
Cell potential (Vcell) Decreases ↓ 0 V
Current (I) Decreases ↓ 0 A

Classwork: Worked Example

▼
📋 Zinc-Silver Cell Problem

A zinc-silver cell has a cell potential difference of 1.56 V under standard conditions. The cell is allowed to run for 3 hours and the cell potential drops to 1.45 V.

Question 1: Explain using Le Chatelier's principle why this will happen.

Answer:

As the reaction proceeds:

  • The Ag⁺ ion concentration (reactant ions) will decrease
  • The Zn²⁺ ion concentration (product ions) will increase

Applying Le Chatelier's principle:

  • The rate of the reverse reaction will increase to reduce the product ion concentration
  • This also works to increase the reactant ion concentration
  • This will reduce the rate of the forward reaction
  • Thus lowering the overall cell potential

Question 2: Explain what will happen to the current flowing through the circuit.

Answer:

As the cell potential decreases, the current will also decrease.

This is because:

  • V ∝ I (voltage is proportional to current)
  • Lower driving force (voltage) means fewer electrons flow per second
  • The rate of electron transfer decreases as the forward reaction slows

Question 3: After several days, the cell potential reads 0 V. Explain using Le Chatelier's principle.

Answer:

Using Le Chatelier's principle:

  • Dynamic chemical equilibrium will be established
  • This occurs when the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction
  • At this point, there is no net flow of electrons
  • Therefore, the overall cell potential (EMF) will be 0 V
  • The current will also be 0 A
