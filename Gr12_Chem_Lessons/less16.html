Lesson Overview

When a chemical system is at equilibrium, the forward and reverse reactions occur at equal rates, and there is no net change in the concentrations of reactants and products. But what happens when we disturb this equilibrium? Le Chatelier's Principle helps us predict how the system will respond to changes.

📝 Pre-Knowledge Required:
  • Reversible reactions and chemical equilibrium
  • Endothermic and exothermic reactions
  • The meaning of ΔH < 0 (exothermic) and ΔH > 0 (endothermic)

🔥 Exothermic Reactions

Reactions that release heat to the surroundings.

ΔH < 0 (negative)

❄️ Endothermic Reactions

Reactions that absorb heat from the surroundings.

ΔH > 0 (positive)

⚖️

Le Chatelier's Principle

📜 Le Chatelier's Principle

"When the equilibrium in a closed system is disturbed by changing any of the conditions (i.e., concentration, temperature, volume, or partial pressure), the equilibrium will shift in such a way as to cancel the effect of the change."

💡 Key Insight: The system always tries to oppose or counteract any change made to it. Think of it as the system "fighting back" against the disturbance to restore balance.

📋 Factors Affecting Equilibrium

🧪 Concentration

Adding or removing reactants or products

🌡️ Temperature

Heating or cooling the system

📊 Pressure/Volume

Compressing or expanding gases

⚗️ Catalyst

Adding a catalyst to the system

🧪

Factor 1: Concentration

Consider the following equilibrium reaction in a closed system:

CoCl42- + 6H2O ⇌ Co(H2O)62+ + 4Cl-
(blue) → (pink/red)

→ Add Water

Adding water to the blue solution makes it turn pink/red.

✓ Forward reaction is favored (uses up the added water)

← Add HCl

Adding concentrated HCl to the red solution makes it turn blue.

✓ Reverse reaction is favored (uses up the added Cl⁻)

📌 Conclusion - Effect of Concentration:

  • Increase concentration on the LEFT → Equilibrium favors the FORWARD reaction
  • Increase concentration on the RIGHT → Equilibrium favors the REVERSE reaction
  • Decrease concentration on the LEFT → Equilibrium favors the REVERSE reaction
  • Decrease concentration on the RIGHT → Equilibrium favors the FORWARD reaction
🌡️

Factor 2: Temperature

Consider the following equilibrium reaction:

2NO2(g) ⇌ N2O4(g)     ΔH < 0
(brown) → (yellow/colorless)
Forward reaction is EXOTHERMIC

🔥 Heating the Mixture

The gas turns dark brown (more NO₂ forms).

✓ Reverse reaction (endothermic) is favored

System absorbs the added heat

❄️ Cooling the Mixture

The gas turns lighter/yellow (more N₂O₄ forms).

✓ Forward reaction (exothermic) is favored

System releases heat to replace lost heat

📌 Conclusion - Effect of Temperature:

  • Increase temperature → Equilibrium favors the ENDOTHERMIC reaction (absorbs heat)
  • Decrease temperature → Equilibrium favors the EXOTHERMIC reaction (releases heat)
⚠️ Important: Temperature is the only factor that changes the value of Kc. Changing concentration or pressure does NOT change Kc.
📊

Factor 3: Pressure (and Volume)

Consider the following equilibrium reaction:

2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2SO3(g)
2 mol + 1 mol = 3 mol → 2 mol
💡 Key Concept: Count the total moles of gas on each side:
  • Left side: 2 + 1 = 3 mol of gas (exerts MORE pressure)
  • Right side: 2 mol of gas (exerts LESS pressure)

⬆️ Increase Pressure

Equilibrium shifts to the side with FEWER gas molecules.

✓ Forward reaction is favored (3 mol → 2 mol)

System reduces pressure by making fewer molecules

⬇️ Decrease Pressure

Equilibrium shifts to the side with MORE gas molecules.

✓ Reverse reaction is favored (2 mol → 3 mol)

System increases pressure by making more molecules

📌 Conclusion - Effect of Pressure:

  • Increase pressure → Equilibrium favors the side with FEWER gas molecules
  • Decrease pressure → Equilibrium favors the side with MORE gas molecules
  • Equal moles on both sides → No effect on equilibrium position
📝 Note: Decreasing volume has the same effect as increasing pressure (and vice versa). PV = nRT, so if V decreases at constant n and T, P increases.
⚗️

Factor 4: Catalyst

⚗️ Adding a Catalyst

A catalyst speeds up BOTH the forward and reverse reactions equally.

  • Equilibrium is reached faster
  • The position of equilibrium does NOT change
  • The value of Kc does NOT change
  • The yield of products remains the same

📌 Conclusion - Effect of Catalyst:

A catalyst does NOT affect the equilibrium position or Kc. It only helps the system reach equilibrium more quickly.
