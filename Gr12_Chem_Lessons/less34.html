The Photoelectric Effect

Lesson 3: Photons, Work Function & Threshold Frequency

  • Recall, use and explain the significance of hf = W₀ + ½mv²

📚 Overview

The photoelectric effect is one of the most important phenomena in modern physics. It describes the emission of electrons from a metal surface when light of sufficient frequency shines on it. This effect could not be explained by classical wave theory of light and led to a revolutionary understanding of light as having particle-like properties.

💡
Key Insight: The photoelectric effect provided crucial evidence that light consists of discrete packets of energy called photons, establishing the foundation for quantum mechanics.

🔄 Pre-Knowledge Review

Before studying the photoelectric effect, let's review essential concepts:

Essential Background Knowledge

  • Metallic Bonding: Metals bond through electrostatic forces between delocalised electrons (the "electron sea") and positively charged metal ions.
  • Delocalised Electrons: In metals, some electrons are free to move throughout the metallic lattice, held in place by attraction to positive nuclei.
  • Electromagnetic Spectrum: UV light has higher frequency than visible light.
  • Energy: The ability to do work. Light carries energy.
  • Kinetic Energy: KE = ½mv² (energy of motion)
🔬
Electroscope Demonstration: A gold-leaf electroscope can detect electric charge. When a negatively charged zinc plate on an electroscope is exposed to UV light, the leaves collapse as electrons are ejected from the metal!

📖 Key Definitions

⚡ Photoelectric Effect

The emission of electrons from a metal surface when electromagnetic radiation (light) of sufficient frequency shines on it. The ejected electrons are called photoelectrons.

✨ Photon

A discrete "packet" or quantum of electromagnetic energy. The energy of a photon is given by E = hf, where h is Planck's constant and f is frequency.

🔒 Work Function (W₀)

The minimum amount of energy needed to remove an electron from the surface of a metal. Different metals have different work functions. Unit: Joules (J)

📊 Threshold Frequency (f₀)

The minimum frequency of light required to eject electrons from a metal surface. Below this frequency, no electrons are ejected regardless of light intensity.

📖 Main Concepts & Principles

1. Experimental Observations

Key experimental observations about the photoelectric effect:

  • A negatively charged zinc plate discharges when exposed to ultraviolet (UV) light
  • Increased intensity of UV light causes the electroscope to discharge faster
  • A positively charged zinc plate is NOT discharged by UV light
  • Visible light, even at high intensity, cannot discharge a negatively charged zinc plate

Key Conclusions

  • Light can cause electrons to be ejected from a metal surface
  • Only light of sufficiently high frequency can eject electrons
  • This minimum frequency is called the threshold frequency (f₀)
  • Increasing light intensity increases the number of electrons ejected per second

2. Max Planck's Contribution

MP
Max Planck (1858-1947)

Planck proposed that vibrating particles radiate energy not continuously, but in small discrete packages called quanta.

  • Energy is emitted in "packets" (quanta), not as a continuous stream
  • The energy of each quantum is directly proportional to frequency
  • Quanta are indivisible - you cannot have half a quantum!

Planck's Equation

E = hf

E = energy of photon (J)

h = Planck's constant = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s

f = frequency of light (Hz)

3. Einstein's Contribution

AE
Albert Einstein (1879-1955)

Einstein explained the photoelectric effect and earned the Nobel Prize in 1921 for this work. His key insights:

  • Energy is needed to remove an electron from a metal
  • A minimum energy (work function, W₀) is required
  • The photon's energy must be ≥ work function for electrons to be ejected
  • Extra energy becomes kinetic energy of the ejected electron

Einstein's Photoelectric Equation

hf = W₀ + ½mv²

hf = energy of incident photon

W₀ = work function of the metal (or hf₀)

½mv² = maximum kinetic energy of ejected electron

Understanding the Equation

Energy of photon = Work function + Kinetic energy

The incoming photon provides energy. Part of this energy is used to "free" the electron (work function). Any leftover energy becomes the kinetic energy of the ejected electron.

Also note: W₀ = hf₀ (work function equals Planck's constant × threshold frequency)

4. The Dual Nature of Light

🌊
Wave Nature

Light undergoes refraction and diffraction, demonstrating wave properties.

Predominates during propagation of radiation

⚡
Particle Nature

The photoelectric effect demonstrates that light consists of discrete particles (photons).

Predominates during interaction with matter

🔑
Significance of the Photoelectric Effect:
  • It established the quantum theory of light
  • It demonstrates the particle nature of light
  • Classical wave theory could NOT explain why threshold frequency exists

✏️ Worked Examples

Example 1: Calculating Photon Energy

Question: Calculate the energy of a photon with frequency 2.2 × 10¹⁴ Hz. Will this radiation be able to eject an electron from a metal with work function 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J?

1 Write down the formula:

E = hf

2 Substitute values:

E = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴)(2.2 × 10¹⁴)

E = 1.46 × 10⁻¹⁹ J

3 Compare with work function:

Photon energy: 1.46 × 10⁻¹⁹ J

Work function: 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J

Since E < W₀, the photon does NOT have enough energy to eject an electron.

Answer: E = 1.46 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. No, this radiation will NOT be able to eject electrons because E < W₀.

Example 2: Work Function and Kinetic Energy

Question: The photoelectric threshold frequency of copper is 9.4 × 10¹⁴ Hz.

(a) What is the work function of copper?

(b) With what maximum kinetic energy will electrons be ejected when light of frequency 2 × 10¹⁵ Hz shines on copper?

1 Part (a) - Calculate work function:

W₀ = hf₀

W₀ = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴)(9.4 × 10¹⁴)

W₀ = 6.23 × 10⁻¹⁹ J

2 Part (b) - Calculate photon energy:

E = hf = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴)(2 × 10¹⁵)

E = 1.33 × 10⁻¹⁸ J

3 Use photoelectric equation:

E = W₀ + KE

1.33 × 10⁻¹⁸ = 6.23 × 10⁻¹⁹ + KE

KE = 1.33 × 10⁻¹⁸ - 6.23 × 10⁻¹⁹

KE = 7.07 × 10⁻¹⁹ J

Answers:
(a) W₀ = 6.23 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
(b) KE = 7.07 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
