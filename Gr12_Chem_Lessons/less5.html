Overview: Hydrocarbon Derivatives

Hydrocarbon derivatives are organic compounds derived from hydrocarbons by replacing one or more hydrogen atoms with a functional group. These functional groups determine the chemical properties and reactivity of the compound.

🔑 Key Point

The functional group is an atom or group of atoms which gives a compound its particular characteristics. It determines the compound's chemical behavior and how it is named.

The Five Main Hydrocarbon Derivatives

🍷 Alcohols

Contain -OH group. End in -ol

🧪 Aldehydes

Contain -CHO group. End in -al

💅 Ketones

Contain C=O (middle). End in -one

🍋 Carboxylic Acids

Contain -COOH group. End in -oic acid

🍎 Esters

Contain -COO- group. End in -oate

🍷

Alcohols

🍷 Alcohols (-OH)

R - OH
Suffix: -ol

Alcohols contain at least one hydroxyl (-OH) group. They have the general formula CnH2n+1OH for alcohols with one hydroxyl group.

Key Properties:
  • Form hydrogen bonds between molecules
  • Higher melting and boiling points than similar-sized hydrocarbons
  • Most alcohols are polar molecules

Common Alcohols

Name Formula Carbons Common Name/Use
Methanol CH₃OH 1 Methyl alcohol, wood alcohol (toxic!)
Ethanol C₂H₅OH 2 Alcoholic beverages, fermentation product
Propan-1-ol C₃H₇OH 3 Propyl alcohol, solvent

Classification of Alcohols

Alcohols are classified according to the position of the hydroxyl group on the carbon chain:

Primary Alcohol (1°)
R | R - C - OH | H
The -OH is attached to a carbon with one other carbon attached
Secondary Alcohol (2°)
R | R - C - OH | R
The -OH is attached to a carbon with two other carbons attached
Tertiary Alcohol (3°)
R | R - C - OH | R
The -OH is attached to a carbon with three other carbons attached
🧪

Aldehydes and Ketones

🔗 The Carbonyl Group

Both aldehydes and ketones contain the carbonyl functional group (C=O). The difference is in the position of this group within the molecule.

Aldehydes (-CHO)
O ‖ R - C - H

Suffix: -al

Carbonyl at the END of chain (H attached)

Ketones (>C=O)
O ‖ R - C - R

Suffix: -one

Carbonyl in MIDDLE of chain (C on both sides)

🧪 Aldehydes

-CHO
Suffix: -al

The simplest aldehyde is methanal (formaldehyde) - H₂C=O. Used as a preservative.

Examples: Methanal, Ethanal, Propanal

💅 Ketones

>C=O
Suffix: -one

The simplest ketone is propanone (acetone). Used as nail polish remover and solvent.

Examples: Propanone, Butanone, Pentanone

⚠️ Important Differences

  • Aldehydes are more reactive than ketones
  • Both contribute to distinctive smells of foods and plants
  • Aldehydes have the carbonyl at the end; ketones have it in the middle
🍋

Carboxylic Acids

🍋 Carboxylic Acids (-COOH)

O ‖ R - C - OH
Suffix: -oic acid

All organic acids are carboxylic acids. They contain the functional group -COOH and have the general formula CnH2n+1COOH.

Common Carboxylic Acids

IUPAC Name Formula Common Name Found In
Methanoic acid HCOOH Formic acid Ant stings, stinging nettles
Ethanoic acid CH₃COOH Acetic acid Vinegar (sour component)
Butanoic acid C₃H₇COOH Butyric acid Rancid butter (bad smell)
🍎

Esters

🍎 Esters (-COO-)

O ‖ R - C - O - R'
Suffix: -oate

Esters have pleasant smells and are largely responsible for the flavours and scents of fruits and flowers.

🧪 Esterification Reaction

Esters are formed when an alcohol reacts with a carboxylic acid in the presence of a catalyst (usually concentrated sulfuric acid). This reaction also produces water.

Naming Esters

📝 How to Name an Ester

The name of an ester has TWO parts:

  1. First part: Derived from the parent alcohol (alkyl group)
  2. Second part: Derived from the parent carboxylic acid (ending in -oate)

Example: Methanol + Propanoic acid → Methyl propanoate

Worked Examples

✅ Example 1: Identifying Alcohol Type

Question: Classify the following alcohol as primary, secondary, or tertiary:

CH₃ | CH₃ - C - OH | CH₃
1 Find the carbon with -OH: The middle carbon has the -OH attached
2 Count carbons attached to this carbon: Three CH₃ groups = 3 carbons attached
Answer: This is a tertiary alcohol (the -OH carbon has 3 other carbons attached)

✅ Example 2: Naming an Ester

Question: Name the ester formed when ethanol reacts with ethanoic acid.

1 Identify the alcohol: Ethanol → provides the "ethyl" part
2 Identify the acid: Ethanoic acid → provides the "ethanoate" part
Answer: The ester is named ethyl ethanoate
Structural Formula:
CH₃COOCH₂CH₃

✅ Example 3: Identifying Functional Groups

Question: Identify the functional group in CH₃CH₂CHO

1 Look at the ending: The compound ends in -CHO
2 Identify: -CHO is the aldehyde functional group (carbonyl at the end with H attached)
Answer: This is an aldehyde (propanal)
