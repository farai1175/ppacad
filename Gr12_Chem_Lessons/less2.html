Lesson Overview

+

This lesson explores the concept of impulse, which is the product of force and time. We'll learn how impulse relates to changes in momentum and how this principle applies to real-world situations like car safety systems.

Key Formula

FnetΔt = Δp

Where:

  • Fnet = net force (N)
  • Δt = time interval (s)
  • Δp = change in momentum (kg•m•s⁻¹)

Impulse has units of Newton-seconds (N•s)

Main Body

+

Introduction to Impulse

Isaac Newton realized that the larger the change of momentum and the shorter the time that it takes place in, the larger the force that is necessary to bring it about.

In terms of Newton's Second Law of Motion:

Fnet = Δp / Δt

Rearranging this equation gives us:

FnetΔt = Δp

The product FnetΔt is called impulse.

Impulse has the unit N•s, which is equivalent to kg•m•s⁻¹ (the same as momentum).

The Impulse-Momentum Theorem

The impulse-momentum theorem states that the impulse on an object equals the change in its momentum.

Impulse = FnetΔt = m(vf - vi) = Δp

This relationship explains why:

  • Airbags increase collision time to reduce force
  • Bending knees when landing reduces impact force
  • Follow-through in sports increases impulse

Impulse and Safety

Short Δt

Large Force

Long Δt

Small Force

For the same change in momentum, increasing the time reduces the force

Worked Examples

Example 1: Car Braking

The driver of a car of mass 1200 kg traveling at 20 m•s⁻¹ along a straight road, slows down uniformly for 4s. The brakes apply a net force of 4800 N.

a) Calculate the impulse exerted by the brakes.

Impulse = FnetΔt = -4800 × 4 = -19200 N•s

b) What is the change in momentum of the car?

Change in momentum = impulse = -19200 kg•m•s⁻¹

c) Calculate the velocity, vf, of the car after 4s.

FnetΔt = m(vf - vi)

-19200 = 1200(vf - 20)

vf = 4 m•s⁻¹

Example 2: Ball Collision

A rubber ball with a mass 80g, is thrown horizontally and collides perpendicularly with a wall at a velocity of 7 m•s⁻¹. The ball rebounds in the opposite direction at 6 m•s⁻¹.

a) Calculate the magnitude and state the direction of the change in momentum.

Take towards the wall as positive:

Δp = m(vf - vi) = 0.08(-6 - 7) = -1.04 kg•m•s⁻¹

Δp = 1.04 kg•m•s⁻¹ away from the wall

b) If the collision lasts for 0.02s, calculate the magnitude of the force.

FnetΔt = Δp

Fnet(0.02) = -1.04

Fnet = -52 N

Fnet = 52 N away from the wall
