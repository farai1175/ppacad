What is a Galvanic Cell?

▼
📝 Definition: Galvanic Cell

A galvanic cell (also called a voltaic cell) is an electrochemical cell that converts chemical energy into electrical energy through spontaneous redox reactions.

In a galvanic cell, a spontaneous chemical reaction produces an electric current. The cell harnesses the energy released during electron transfer in a redox reaction to do electrical work.

⚡

Energy Conversion

Chemical energy → Electrical energy

The spontaneous redox reaction produces electricity

🔄

Self-Sustaining

No external power source needed

The reaction drives itself until reactants are depleted

⚠️ Important Distinction

Galvanic cells convert chemical energy to electrical energy (like batteries).

Electrolytic cells do the opposite - they convert electrical energy to chemical energy.

🔧

Components of a Galvanic Cell

▼

A simple galvanic cell consists of the following essential components:

🧪
Two Half Cells

Separate compartments for each electrode

⚙️
Electrodes

Solid conductors (usually metals)

💧
Electrolytes

Solutions containing metal ions

🔌
Conducting Wires

Connect electrodes externally

🌉
Salt Bridge

Connects the two half cells

📊
Voltmeter

Measures potential difference

The Electrodes: Anode and Cathode

➖

Anode (Negative Electrode)

  • Oxidation takes place here
  • Electrons are released
  • Metal atoms become ions
  • Electrode mass decreases
  • Negative because of excess electrons
➕

Cathode (Positive Electrode)

  • Reduction takes place here
  • Electrons are accepted
  • Metal ions become atoms
  • Electrode mass increases
  • Positive as electrons flow towards it

🔌 Current Flow

In a galvanic cell, electrons flow from the anode to the cathode through the external circuit (wire). This is because:

  • The anode releases electrons (oxidation)
  • The cathode accepts electrons (reduction)
  • Conventional current flows from cathode to anode (opposite to electron flow)

The Salt Bridge

📝 Salt Bridge

A salt bridge is a tube filled with a saturated solution of an electrolyte (such as KCl, NaCl, KNO₃, or Na₂SO₄) that connects the two half cells.

🔧 Functions of a Salt Bridge
  1. Completes the circuit - allows ion flow between half cells
  2. Separates the electrolytes - prevents direct mixing
  3. Maintains electrical neutrality - balances charges in each half cell

The Zinc-Copper Cell

▼

The zinc-copper cell (also called a Daniell cell) is a classic example of a galvanic cell. Let's examine what happens in each half cell.

Electron Flow →

Property Anode (Zinc Half Cell) Cathode (Copper Half Cell)
Process Oxidation Reduction
Half Reaction Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu
Electrode Charge Negative (excess electrons) Positive (electrons removed)
What Happens Zn atoms become Zn²⁺ ions Cu²⁺ ions become Cu atoms
Mass Change Decreases (Zn dissolves) Increases (Cu deposits)
📝 Net Cell Reaction
Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s)

Zinc is oxidised (loses electrons) and copper ions are reduced (gain electrons).

📝 Exercise 1: Check Your Understanding
  1. What energy conversion takes place in a galvanic cell?
    → Chemical energy is converted to electrical energy
  2. How are the two electrodes in the external circuit connected?
    → Using a conducting wire connected to a voltmeter
  3. How are the electrolytes connected?
    → Using a salt bridge
  4. What is the name of the half cell where oxidation occurs?
    → Anode half cell
  5. What is the name of the half cell where reduction occurs?
    → Cathode half cell
📝 Exercise 2: Magnesium-Silver Cell

Consider a magnesium-silver galvanic cell with the net reaction:

Mg + 2Ag⁺ → Mg²⁺ + 2Ag
  1. Which substance is oxidised? → Mg: Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻
  2. Which substance is reduced? → Ag⁺: Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag
  3. Which half cell contains the anode? → Magnesium half cell
  4. Which half cell contains the cathode? → Silver half cell
  5. Which electrode increases in mass? → Silver electrode
  6. Which electrode decreases in mass? → Magnesium electrode
