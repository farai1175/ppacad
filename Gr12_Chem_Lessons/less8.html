🧪 Reactions of Alcohols and Alkyl Halides

In this lesson, we explore the chemical reactions that alcohols and alkyl halides undergo, including elimination and substitution reactions. Understanding these reactions is fundamental to organic synthesis and industrial chemistry.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand the different types of alcohols (primary, secondary, tertiary) and alkyl halides
  • Identify and describe elimination reactions (dehydrohalogenation and dehydration)
  • Identify and describe substitution reactions of alcohols and alkyl halides
  • Write balanced chemical equations for these reactions
  • State the reaction conditions required for each type of reaction

📋 Overview

Alcohols and alkyl halides are important functional groups in organic chemistry that undergo two main types of reactions:

🔄 Elimination Reactions

Removal of atoms/groups to form a double bond (unsaturated compound)

🔀 Substitution Reactions

Replacement of one atom/group with another (functional group transformation)

🔬 Reactions of Alkyl Halides

1. Elimination (Dehydrohalogenation)

The elimination of HX (where X is Cl, Br, or I) from a haloalkane is called dehydrohalogenation.

Elimination
CH₂Cl-CH₂Cl → CH₂=CHCl + HCl
⚗️ Reaction Conditions:
  • Heat under reflux (vapour condensates and returns to reaction vessel)
  • Concentrated solution of NaOH or KOH
  • Pure ethanol as solvent (hot ethanolic NaOH/KOH)
📌 Important Rule: If more than one elimination product is possible, the major product is the one where the H atom is removed from the carbon atom with the least number of H atoms (Zaitsev's Rule).

2. Substitution (Hydrolysis)

Haloalkanes react with bases to produce alcohols. This substitution reaction is called hydrolysis.

Substitution
C(CH₃)₃X + KOH → C(CH₃)₃OH + KX
⚗️ Reaction Conditions:
  • Haloalkane dissolved in ethanol
  • Treatment with aqueous sodium hydroxide (dilute)
  • Warm the mixture
  • Note: The same hydrolysis occurs more slowly without alkali (just H₂O added to haloalkane in ethanol)

🧫 Reactions of Alcohols

1. Elimination (Dehydration)

Alcohols can undergo elimination to form alkenes by removing water (H₂O).

Dehydration
C₂H₅OH → C₂H₄ + H₂O
⚗️ Reaction Conditions:
  • Concentrated sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) or phosphoric acid (H₃PO₄)
  • Heat (excess of 170°C)

2. Substitution with Hydrogen Halides

Alcohols react with hydrogen halides (HX; X = Cl, Br) to produce haloalkanes.

For Tertiary Alcohols:
Substitution
C(CH₃)₃OH + HBr → C(CH₃)₃Br + H₂O

Conditions: HBr or HCl at room temperature

For Primary & Secondary Alcohols:

Treat with concentrated H₂SO₄ and solid NaBr (or KBr).

Step 1: Forming HBr
H₂SO₄ + NaBr → HBr + NaHSO₄
Step 2: Substitution
CH₃CH₂OH + HBr → CH₃CH₂Br + H₂O
