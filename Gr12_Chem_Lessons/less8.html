Work on Inclined Planes

Calculating work done when forces are applied at angles and on inclined surfaces

Lesson Overview

+

This lesson explores how to calculate work done when forces are applied at angles to the direction of motion, particularly on inclined planes. We'll learn to resolve forces into components and apply the work-energy theorem.

Key Formulas

W = F × d × cosθ
Wnet = ΔEk = Ekf - Eki
Fparallel = Fg × sinθ
Fperpendicular = Fg × cosθ

Where θ is the angle of the incline

Main Body

+

Forces on Inclined Planes

When an object rests on an inclined plane, its weight force (Fg = mg) can be resolved into two components:

Fparallel = Fg × sinθ = mg × sinθ
Fperpendicular = Fg × cosθ = mg × cosθ

Key points:

  • Fparallel acts parallel to the incline and causes the object to slide down
  • Fperpendicular acts perpendicular to the incline and is balanced by the normal force
  • Only the component of force parallel to the displacement does work
  • Friction acts opposite to the direction of motion (or potential motion)

Work-Energy Theorem

Wnet = ΔEk = Ekf - Eki

The net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy.

Key implications:

  • Positive net work increases kinetic energy (object speeds up)
  • Negative net work decreases kinetic energy (object slows down)
  • Zero net work means constant kinetic energy (constant speed)
  • Work done by conservative forces (like gravity) can be calculated using potential energy changes

On an inclined plane, the work-energy theorem becomes:

Wgravity + Wfriction + Wapplied = ½mvf² - ½mvi²

Worked Examples

Problem 1: Sledge Pulled at an Angle

An Inuit man pulling a sledge exerts a force of 200 N and moves the sledge 20 m along level ground. The rope is inclined at an angle of 30° to the horizontal. Determine the work done by the man.

Interactive Calculator for Problem 1

Identify the force component that does work

Only the horizontal component of the force does work since the displacement is horizontal.

Horizontal force component: Fx = F × cosθ

Fx = 200 N × cos(30°) = 200 × 0.866 = 173.2 N

Calculate work done

Work done: W = Fx × d

W = 173.2 N × 20 m = 3464 J

The man does 3464 J of work on the sledge.

Problem 2: Car on Incline

A 1020.41 kg car moves at constant speed on an incline road that makes an angle of 60° with the horizontal. A constant friction force of 1000 N acts on the car.

What is the applied force exerted by the road on the tyres if the car is moving up the slope?

Interactive Calculator for Problem 2

Calculate the weight and its components

Weight: Fg = m × g = 1020.41 kg × 9.8 m/s² = 10000 N

Parallel component: Fg// = Fg × sinθ = 10000 × sin(60°) = 10000 × 0.866 = 8660 N

This component acts down the slope.

Apply Newton's First Law (constant speed)

Since the car moves at constant speed, the net force is zero.

Forces up the slope: Fapplied

Forces down the slope: Fg// + Ffriction = 8660 N + 1000 N = 9660 N

Fapplied = 9660 N up the slope

Problem 3: Work-Energy Theorem Application

A box of mass 60 kg starts from rest at height h and slides down a rough slope of length 10 m, which makes an angle of 25° with the horizontal. It undergoes a constant acceleration of magnitude 2 m/s² while sliding down the slope.

Calculate: a) The kinetic energy at the bottom, b) Work done by gravity, c) Work done by friction

Interactive Calculator for Problem 3

Calculate kinetic energy using equations of motion

Using v² = u² + 2as

v² = 0² + 2 × 2 × 10 = 40 m²/s²

Kinetic energy: Ek = ½mv² = ½ × 60 × 40 = 1200 J

Calculate work done by gravity

Work by gravity: Wg = Fg// × d = mg × sinθ × d

Wg = 60 × 9.8 × sin(25°) × 10 = 588 × 0.4226 × 10 = 2485 J

Calculate work done by friction using work-energy theorem

Work-energy theorem: Wnet = ΔEk

Wg + Wf = Ekf - Eki

2485 + Wf = 1200 - 0

Wf = 1200 - 2485 = -1285 J

Friction does -1285 J of work (energy is dissipated as heat).
